# Horikita Suzune

*Horikita Suzune is the deuteragonist of Classroom of the Elite and Ayanokōji Kiyotaka's principal partner in Year 1. A first-year student in Class 1-D — sorted there despite the third-highest entrance exam score in her grade — she is a fiercely proud, socially isolated meritocrat whose entire strategy for Class A runs through the one classmate she initially despised. Year 1 charts her transformation from a girl who calls struggling classmates "dead weight" to one who gambles her own exam score to save them and publicly declares her class repairable rather than trash.*

## Personality & voice

Horikita speaks in formal, precise keigo laced with open hostility — "bestow additional punishment," "regret while you suffer" (p.16–17) — and her default posture toward people is dismissal: *"Do you mind if I refuse?"* (p.28), *"I refuse. I won't participate."* (p.135). She draws a hard philosophical line between her own rational convictions and mere apathy: unlike Ayanokōji, she kept her bus seat "because I thought it would be pointless" (p.25), and she justifies even karate chops as the human race's oldest resolution tool (p.61).

Three facts define her:

1. **Nine years alone.** She has not had a single friend for over nine consecutive years and states it without sentiment: *"It will be my ninth year in a row of being alone, so I'll be fine"* (p.100). Solitude is her normal state, not a wound she admits to.
2. **The brother wound.** Student council president Horikita Manabu immobilizes her completely — frozen statue at his club-fair speech (p.67–68), docile against a wall during his night assault (p.192–193) — and every declaration of Class A is addressed to him: *"I'm going to drag myself up to Class A even if it kills me"* (p.196).
3. **Meritocracy with a crack in it.** She argues people are calculating creatures (p.200) and calls failing classmates dead weight (p.178) — until Ayanokōji diagnoses her flaw (she treats everyone as baggage) and the midterms force the question of what she owes her own class. Her closing answer becomes the volume's thesis: *"maybe the students in Class D are defective. However, that doesn't mean they're trash"* (p.254).

Her voice turns surprisingly soft at the exact points of growth: the researched, bow-level apology to Sudō about his basketball dream (p.245), the first voluntary speech of her school career (p.249), and the closing wish Ayanokōji notes — *"I wouldn't mind seeing Horikita smile"* is his, not hers (p.262).

## Story summary — Year 1, Volume 1

**Two refusals (Ch. 1–4).** On the entrance bus Horikita declines to yield her seat to an elderly woman, stone-faced through the social pressure, and later intercepts Ayanokōji at the school gate to establish that they are nothing alike (p.24–25). In class she sits beside him, exchanges names only under duress, walks out of Hirata's forced self-introductions without a word, and lectures Ayanokōji for cleaning up Sudō's spilled ramen — pushover behavior offends her more than conflict. Day 2 lunch she mocks his inner monologue aloud: *"How pathetic"* (p.54), then drops the nine-years bombshell at the café trap she herself springs (p.98–100).

**The system shock and the pawn offer (Ch. 6–7).** Class D's zero-point month and the "defective" verdict land as personal insults; she protests her Class D placement directly to Chiyabashira and is shaken by Ayanokōji's all-50s papers (p.142–147). On the stairs she recruits him — *"I need your help, Ayanokouji-kun"* (p.150) — and when he refuses, ignores the refusal, inventing a pact out of thin air. The shogi metaphor defines her method: *"Right now, I don't need a gold or silver general. I want a pawn"* (p.151).

**The study group collapse (Ch. 8).** Compass-stab discipline, a paid cafeteria lunch to buy his cooperation, a midnight veto keeping Kushida out of the group (p.167–168) — and then the library implosion: she torments Sudō over fractions, tells Ike she doesn't care if he's expelled, and concludes *"it's better to get rid of the dead weight"* (p.178). The night walk that follows is her real crucible: Manabu assaults her, Ayanokōji intercepts, and under the night sky he diagnoses her fatal flaw — she counts everyone inferior and blind to their strengths. She concedes the argument point by point (p.201) and extends her hand: *"We've come to an arrangement, then"* — for her own sake (p.202).

**Campaign, confession, sacrifice (Ch. 9–11).** One and a half months in, she engineers the reunion campaign: written workhorse contracts, honest selfishness to win Kushida, and the double-edged *"I hate you, Sudou-kun."* — however, *"our mutual abhorrence is trivial right now"* (p.207). Before the exam she confirms Kushida's hatred to her face and accepts it as a working relationship (p.238), coaches Sudō through his ten-minute English crisis, and bows a researched apology over his basketball dream (p.244–245). On results day she springs the decimal trap and reveals her true midterms play — a self-sabotaged 51 in English — only for Chabashira's rounding rule to kill it (p.249–250). She then silently joins the 100,000-point point purchase (p.253) and wins her teacher's approval with the defective-not-trash philosophy (p.254).

**The war council (Ch. 12).** She attends the victory party — reading outside the circle, the presence itself a milestone — guards the point-purchase secret, kills the celebration with Class D's last-place average, and lays down her terms for the recruits: *"In this school, ability is paramount… you can't do so half-heartedly. Otherwise you'll be a burden"* (p.262). The volume closes with her will to Class A declared unshakeable and her partner chasing after her, chopped in the ribs *"For whatever."*
