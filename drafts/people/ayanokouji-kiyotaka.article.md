# Ayanokōji Kiyotaka

*Ayanokōji Kiyotaka is the principal narrator and central figure of Classroom of the Elite. A first-year student placed in Class 1-D at Tokyo Metropolitan Advanced Nurturing High School, he outwardly presents as an unremarkable, trouble-averse loner while concealing exceptional academic and physical abilities developed in the White Room. Year 1 chronicles his gradual, reluctant entanglement with classmates — above all Horikita Suzune and Kushida Kikyō — as he probes the school's true nature and his own stalled development as a person.*

## Personality & voice

Ayanokōji narrates in a dry, analytical register, treating his own life as a dataset. His opening thesis — "No, we are not equal. To be human is to be unequal" (y1v1 p.14) — frames every later interaction: he sorts people by capability and sorts himself just as coldly. He is conflict-avoidant on the surface ("letting sleeping dogs lie," p.25), yet the volume repeatedly shows him choosing the hard, people-facing path once someone he has begun to care for is at risk (Sudou's expulsion, p.250–254).

Socially he is inexperienced rather than shy. He rehearses friend-making scripts the night before day one (p.26), engineers belonging through small purchases and group-chat participation (p.74, p.69), and reads social mechanics with clinical precision while failing at ordinary banter (the razor joke, p.44). His inner voice mocks his own compliance ("filled with trepidation," p.16) and registers attraction instantly before suppressing it for stability (p.79, p.83).

Two anchors define his private self in this volume:

1. **Freedom from observation.** He chose the school because outside contact is forbidden: "No one else's eyes or words would ever reach me… I could begin anew entirely" (p.52–53). Bottom-of-Class-D placement is secretly welcome — "I couldn't go any lower" (p.126) — because anonymity is safety.
2. **A search he cannot yet name.** Cornered by Horikita on the night walk, his mission slips out unbidden: he came "to find answers" to what a person with true merit looks like and what equality is (p.202). The volume closes on a quieter wish — "I wouldn't mind seeing Horikita smile" (p.262).

## Story summary — Year 1, Volume 1

**Arrival and first bonds (Ch. 1–5).** Ayanokōji enters ANHS seeking an unobserved life. On the entrance-ceremony bus he and Horikita Suzune both decline to yield a seat; she confronts him at the gate, and they part mutually wary. In class he exchanges names with her, endures Chiyabashira's orientation on the point system, and deliberately bombs his self-introduction to stay invisible. A kindness to Sudō at the campus store draws Class D's stigma ("Since you're 'defective,' we'll let you off the hook," p.50) and Horikita's contempt for pushovers. In his dorm room he privately celebrates freedom (p.52–53). Kushida Kikyō seeks him out, remembers his lame intro, and asks about Horikita; he files happiness, not suspicion. A café outing engineered by Kushida collapses when Horikita sees the setup — and reveals she has been alone for nine straight years (p.99–100). He nonetheless drifts into a real male friend group (Ike, Yamauchi, Sudō) via the club fair, group chat, and swim class, while noticing Koenji's 23.22s pool time and his own cover holding under Kushida's scrutiny.

**The point crisis and the pawn offer (Ch. 6–8).** A May morning deposit never arrives: Class D received zero points — 98 absences and 391 infractions consumed the month's allowance (p.122). Chiyabashira names Class D "defective"; seven students face dropout-tier quiz scores. Ayanokōji photographs the standings board and privately welcomes the bottom (p.127). Horikita corners him: she scored third on the entrance exam, demands the school's true sorting logic, and offers him a place at her side as "a pawn" in a shogi game aimed at Class A (p.151). He refuses — for now. Chiyabashira separately shows Horikita his all-50s papers and plants the seed that he may be smarter than she is (p.146–147).

**Study group, rooftop, night fight (Ch. 8–10).** Blackmailed by Horikita (café-betrayal debt), he recruits the at-risk trio for tutoring. Kushida volunteers; Horikita vetoes her from the group; Kushida comes anyway and the first session collapses under Horikita's contempt teaching (p.172–177). On the rooftop Ayanokōji overhears Kushida drop her public mask — pure hatred of Horikita — and she counters with a fabricated assault threat, then snaps back to sunshine (p.182–188). He files the Jekyll switch without flinching. Later, walking out at night, he witnesses Horikita's brother, student-council president Manabu, dismiss and physically dominate her; he intervenes, spars Manabu to a standstill, and escapes on a deliberately inconsistent cover story (p.192–196). The walk back produces his longest honest exchange with Horikita: her flaw is treating everyone as a burden (p.200); his own chest tightens when she says ability is paramount (p.262). Their mission statements surface — she wants Class A "for my own sake"; he wants to know what true merit looks like — and they shake hands: "I would soon realize that I'd just made a deal with the devil" (p.202).

**Midterms and the point purchase (Ch. 10–12).** Ayanokōji buys old exam papers from a third-year, verifies the mock problems are word-identical, and has Kushida distribute them the night before — fingerprints off, credit laundered (p.229–235). Sudō still fails English by 0.8 points against a pass line the teacher had never announced (class average ÷ 2, rounded up this time, p.247–250). Horikita had deliberately bombed her own English to drag the average down; the rounding policy defeats her. Ayanokōji chases Chiyabashira into the corridor and asks to *buy* one test point for 100,000 points; Horikita matches the payment, and the expulsion is voided (p.253–254). That evening the group consolidates in his room: Kushida formalizes the Class A project and recruits the Idiot Trio; Sudō, freshly crushed on Horikita after her public apology for mocking his basketball dream, joins. The volume ends with Ayanokōji resolving, at least for now, to do his best — and hoping to see Horikita smile.
