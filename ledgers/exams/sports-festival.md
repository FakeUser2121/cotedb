### y1v5 p.16–23 — Ch.2 §2.1: the rules briefing
[scene] (p.16) Chabashira’s two-hour homeroom hands out the printed sports festival rules; September to early October becomes heavy physical education.
[rule] (p.17) Classes A and D form the Red Team, Classes B and C the White Team, across all grade levels; Class D competes alongside Class A.
[rule] (p.17) Individual events score 15/12/10/8; fifth place loses one point and each place below loses one more; a team-competition win is worth 500.
[rule] (p.18) Recommended-participant events score 50/30/15/10; fifth loses two points and each place below loses two more; the 1200-metre relay pays three times those values.
[rule] (p.18) After combined class scores are reviewed, 100 class points are deducted from the first-, second- and third-year batches of the two classes on the losing team.
[note] (p.18) Chabashira adds that points move class-by-class, not just by team: even a Red Team win can still hit Class D with the 100-point penalty if it holds the lowest combined overall score.
[rule] (p.18) Grade ranking is separate: 50 class points to each grade’s first-place class, no change for second, 50 deducted for third, 100 for fourth.
[rule] (p.19) An individual winner chooses 5000 private points or three written-test points, second chooses 3000 or two, third chooses 1000 or one; test points taken this way cannot be given to another student.
[rule] (p.19) The lowest scorer in an individual competition loses 1000 private points, or one written-test point if they hold fewer than 1000.
[rule] (p.19) Overall MVP takes 100,000 private points; each grade’s top three scorers take 10,000 private points apiece.
[rule] (p.20) The ten lowest-scoring students in each year lose ten points on their next written exam.
[rule] (p.21) Thirteen events in order: 100-metre dash, hurdle race, capture the flag (boys), ball toss (girls), tug-of-war by sex, obstacle course race, three-legged race, cavalry battle, 200-metre dash; then scavenger hunt, four-way tug-of-war, mixed three-legged race, and the mixed-grade 1200-metre relay.
[rule] (p.22) The participation table must be a class consensus; the window opens one week before the festival and closes at 5:00 pm the day before; a missed window means random assignment.
[rule] (p.22) Falling below the minimum for an all-participant event means disqualification; a recommended-event substitute can be bought for 100,000 private points per competition.
[rel:horikita-suzune] (p.22–23) Her absence and health questions draw out the substitute price and the student-decides health policy.
[rel:chabashira-sae] (p.16) Delivers the whole briefing in a detached, matter-of-fact manner and lets out one slight laugh at Ike’s question.

### y1v5 p.39–53 — Ch.3 §3: Four-way tug-of-war and the luck events
[rule] (p.51) The four-way tug-of-war takes four people from each of the four classes — sixteen in all — who pull simultaneously, so tactics matter beyond raw strength.
[rule] (p.51) Luck-based events such as the scavenger hunt are staffed by rock-paper-scissors among the volunteers, on Hirata’s call: “Simple is best. How about we go with rock-paper-scissors?”
[scope] (p.44) Class D’s roster fills only about a third of the all-participant spots at the first pass; the rest stay open for later practice.

### y1v5 p.86–114 — Ch.6 §6: the festival runs
[scene] (p.86) Festival day: opening ceremony, then the thirteen-event schedule begins with the 100-metre dash under a finish-line camera.
[rule] (p.87) First-year boys form ten groups of eight for the 100-metre dash — two from each class — run at about twenty-second intervals.
[rule] (p.97) Hurdle penalties: 0.5 seconds for a knocked-down hurdle, 0.3 for a touch; ten hurdles at ten-metre intervals; all down adds five seconds.
[rule] (p.100) Capture the flag: the group that makes two captures wins; offense may not contact the other side’s offense; blatant punches and kicks are prohibited but a degree of roughhousing is overlooked.
[rule] (p.105) Tug-of-war and ball toss run as team competitions; the first team to two points wins the tug, same structure as capture the flag.
[rule] (p.105) Girls’ ball toss result printed: “With fifty-four points total, the Red Team wins.”
[rule] (p.96) After the 100-metre dash across all grades: Red Team 2011 points, White Team 1891 — Red slightly ahead as the festival opens.
[rel:sudou-ken] (p.88, 112) Takes first in the 100-metre dash and the boys’ obstacle course — three individual firsts in a row by p.112.
[rel:kouenji-rokusuke] (p.88–89) Absent from his lane; the school marks the absence without searching — a missing Red Team scorer.
[rel:sakayanagi-arisu] (p.88–89) Also non-participating, but with a valid reason in the text’s account — two Red Team handicaps against B and C full rosters.

### y1v5 p.115–143 — Ch.6 §6.7–6.10: afternoon rules and the morning’s end
[scene] (p.115) Second half runs three-legged through cavalry to the lunch break that ends the morning half.
[rule] (p.115) Three-legged: two people per team, only four teams run at once; races follow one another like a live television program.
[rule] (p.120) Cavalry: three-minute period; points from enemy units defeated and allied units remaining; four horsemen per unit; each horseman fifty points, each class’s “general” one hundred; a stolen headband scores even if the opponent stands.
[rule] (p.124) Knocking down a jockey in cavalry counts as a self-KO, not a headband steal — the fifty points vanish.
[rule] (p.135) Absentees in an event are disqualified and earn no points; the show goes on.
[rule] (p.140) Morning half ends at the lunch break; the contests will run in reverse order in the second half.
[rel:sudou-ken] (p.115, 123) Three-legged first and boys’ cavalry spearhead before the punch removes him from the board.
[rel:ryuuen-kakeru] (p.123, 128, 135) Girls’ cavalry win for B/C, boys’ cavalry win for Class C alone, then first in the 200-metre dash.
[rel:horikita-suzune] (p.115–123) Third injured student of the day; her rankings fall across every event after the obstacle fall.
