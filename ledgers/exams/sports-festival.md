### y1v5 p.16–23 — Ch.2 §2.1: the rules briefing
[scene] (p.16) Chabashira’s two-hour homeroom hands out the printed sports festival rules; September to early October becomes heavy physical education.
[rule] (p.17) Classes A and D form the Red Team, Classes B and C the White Team, across all grade levels; Class D competes alongside Class A.
[rule] (p.17) Individual events score 15/12/10/8; fifth place loses one point and each place below loses one more; a team-competition win is worth 500.
[rule] (p.18) Recommended-participant events score 50/30/15/10; fifth loses two points and each place below loses two more; the 1200-metre relay pays three times those values.
[rule] (p.18) After combined class scores are reviewed, 100 class points are deducted from the first-, second- and third-year batches of the two classes on the losing team.
[note] (p.18) Chabashira adds that points move class-by-class, not just by team: even a Red Team win can still hit Class D with the 100-point penalty if it holds the lowest combined overall score.
[rule] (p.18) Grade ranking is separate: 50 class points to each grade’s first-place class, no change for second, 50 deducted for third, 100 for fourth.
[rule] (p.19) An individual winner chooses 5000 private points or three written-test points, second chooses 3000 or two, third chooses 1000 or one; test points taken this way cannot be given to another student.
[rule] (p.19) The lowest scorer in an individual competition loses 1000 private points, or one written-test point if they hold fewer than 1000.
[rule] (p.19) Overall MVP takes 100,000 private points; each grade’s top three scorers take 10,000 private points apiece.
[rule] (p.20) The ten lowest-scoring students in each year lose ten points on their next written exam.
[rule] (p.21) Thirteen events in order: 100-metre dash, hurdle race, capture the flag (boys), ball toss (girls), tug-of-war by sex, obstacle course race, three-legged race, cavalry battle, 200-metre dash; then scavenger hunt, four-way tug-of-war, mixed three-legged race, and the mixed-grade 1200-metre relay.
[rule] (p.22) The participation table must be a class consensus; the window opens one week before the festival and closes at 5:00 pm the day before; a missed window means random assignment.
[rule] (p.22) Falling below the minimum for an all-participant event means disqualification; a recommended-event substitute can be bought for 100,000 private points per competition.
[rel:horikita-suzune] (p.22–23) Her absence and health questions draw out the substitute price and the student-decides health policy.
[rel:chabashira-sae] (p.16) Delivers the whole briefing in a detached, matter-of-fact manner and lets out one slight laugh at Ike’s question.

### y1v5 p.26–34 — Ch.2 §2.2: the assembly
[scene] (p.26) Gymnasium 1, four hundred strong: Fujimaki briefs the Red Team, first-year Class A assembles under Katsuragi, Class C walks out, Sakayanagi is introduced, and the Class A–Class D pact is struck.
[rule] (p.26) Only the final event — the 1200-metre relay — brings all classes from all grade levels together; every other event is divided by grade level.
[rel:fujimaki] (p.26) Third-year Class A takes Red Team command in place of the student council president.
[rel:katsuragi-kouhei] (p.31) Class A and Class D agree to keep out of each other’s way: no shared competition details, only a meeting to come for the group events.
[rel:ryuuen-kakeru] (p.27) Ryuuen refuses Class B’s cooperation talk and walks Class C out — the White Team’s first-year halves open the festival estranged.
[rel:ichinose-honami] (p.27) Ichinose confronts the walkout to his face and gets gratitude for the trouble saved.
