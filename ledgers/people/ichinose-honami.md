### y1v2 p.102–107 — Ch.4 §4.4: she walks into the special building alone and offers Class B's help
[scene] (p.102) Her entrance is unannounced and physically striking to the narrator: "We turned in response to the unexpected voice. A beautiful girl with strawberry-blonde hair stood behind us. I recognized her, though I hadn’t ever spoken to her before. She was a student from Class B, Ichinose. Rumor had it she was a superb student."
[says] (p.102) “Hey, you two. What are you doing?”
[says] (p.102) “Sorry to interrupt you like that. Do you have a moment? Oh, if you happen to be in the middle of a so-so date, though, I’d like you to break it up right away.”
[says] (p.103) “Ha ha, I see. This place is a little too hot to be a date spot.”
[says] (p.103) She has been watching Class D without speaking to it: “I’ve met you twice before. We didn’t talk directly, though. I remember seeing you in the library once, too.”
[says] (p.103) “I have a very good memory, you know.” — AK's receipt of it as a backhanded comment on his own unremarkableness.
[says] (p.103–104) Her reason for being there is the case itself: “I thought that surely there’d be something here related to the brawl. I wasn’t here yesterday when Class B heard about the witness. Later, I heard that Class D was looking for evidence of Sudou’s innocence.” and “Hmm, a connection? Well, I’m not connected. When I heard about what happened, I had some doubts. So I thought I’d come here myself to take a look. Would you mind filling me in?”
[says] (p.104) She takes refusal as data, not as an insult: “Guess not, huh? Well, if the other classes were interested…”
[says] (p.104) To Horikita's accusation of an ulterior motive: “Ulterior motive? You think we’re working in the shadows to undermine both C and Class D?” — "Ichinose wore a baffled expression. 'Do you need to be that on guard? I’m really just curious, that’s all.'"
[says] (p.105) Her first principle, aimed at Class D's own logic: “So that’s what happened. This story hasn’t made its way to Class B yet. Hey, isn’t this a pretty big problem, though? It doesn’t really matter who lied since it’s an issue about violence, right? Shouldn’t you be hurrying to uncover the truth?”
[says] (p.105) The question she asks instead of taking a side: “So you believe Sudou-kun because he’s your classmate. And your friend, of course, that much is obvious. So Class D is in an uproar because Sudou is being falsely charged, right?”
[rule] (p.105) The answer she gets back, from one of the two Class D students — the text does not say which: “I’d report on it honestly. After all, covering for a lie would only come back to haunt us later.” — and her reaction is agreement, not argument; AK's own line follows, "Yeah, okay. I think so, too."
[says] (p.105) She does not take the dismissal personally: “Are you finished? You heard what you wanted.” is Horikita's; her answer is “Hmm. Yeah, how about I help you out? To look for the witness, or whatever. You’re bound to get further with more people, right?”
[says] (p.106) Her argument for intervening in another class's discipline case, stated as system logic: “Are Classes D and B completely unrelated to each other? We don’t know when these cases will spring up, or whom they’ll involve. Since the classes are in constant competition, there’s always the chance that this will happen. This was the merely the first case. If the party that lied wins, that’d set a bad precedent. Also, I personally can’t turn away now that I know what happened.” (sic — "the merely")
[feat] (p.106) She prices her own value honestly, including the downside: “If Class B works with you, wouldn’t that dramatically improve your credibility? Though I suppose the opposite could be true, too. Class D may suffer greater consequences if the truth’s uncovered…” and “What do you think? I don’t believe it’s a bad suggestion.”
[status] (p.106–107) She is taken on: Horikita says yes, gives her own name first, and AK records the result — "We had unexpectedly made Ichinose of Class B’s acquaintance and accepted her as an ally. There was still the risk that this could lead to bad things. No matter what, things were going to change."
[says] (p.107) She knows Ayanokōji's name the moment Horikita gives her own: “It’s nice to meet you, Horikita-san. And you too, Ayanokouji-kun.” — against AK's assumption a minute earlier that "she probably didn’t even know my name. I was only one of many students to her."
[says] (p.107) Her reading of Class D's witness problem: “Well, that means there isn’t another witness. I mean, I guess someone from another class might have witnessed it, but it isn’t likely.”
[rule] (p.107) She is the source of the class-points half of the club rule, which Class D's own teacher had never given them: “the school evaluates club and philanthropic activities, right? So if he enters a tournament and does well, Sudou-kun could earn points. Those’ll be tied to your class points, too. Wait… Did you guys not know that? Did your teacher not tell you?”
[says] (p.107) Her diagnosis of the school's teaching staff: “Your homeroom teacher is kind of weird.” / “She doesn’t seem motivated to tell us anything. She’s completely apathetic. Some teachers are like that.” / “Did you know that the school evaluates homeroom teachers when their class graduates?”
[trait] (p.102–107) She arrives with nothing to gain, says so twice, is called a liar's ally to her face, and still ends the afternoon as Class D's first outside partner — the volume's first Class B agent and the counter-example to Horikita's theory that nobody helps for free.
[rel:horikita-suzune] (p.104–107) Horikita suspects her on sight and hires her within the same conversation; the two are the pair who decide, over Sakura's silence, to bring another class in.
[note] (p.102–107) This is her first direct contact with Ayanokōji in the volume; every fact about her here comes from what she says in one afternoon, and no Class B student corroborates any of it.

### y1v2 p.108–115 — Ch.4 §4.4–4.6: what Class B's representative actually does
[says] (p.108) The reason her own teacher is different, given as a third party's motto: “Our homeroom teacher, Hoshinomiya-sensei, says it like it’s her motto. She says she wants to do her best because the homeroom teacher for Class A gets a special bonus. Looks like it’s pretty different for you guys.”
[says] (p.108) “I think it would probably be good for us to meet up and discuss things.” — Horikita's answer, which she takes without offence: “I never thought I’d receive aid from the enemy.”
[says] (p.108) “This seems like a problem we need to address before we can fight. We’re not really on equal footing, are we?” — the clearest statement in the volume of what the class-rank system does between classes.
[says] (p.108) She says the quiet part about Class D's teacher out loud: “I’m envious of your relationship with your homeroom teacher. And your class environment.”
[feat] (p.108) Horikita, who has just insulted her, is charmed: "Ha ha ha, that’s probably true. You’re pretty interesting."
[scene] (p.109) Her contact details are exchanged on her initiative: “How about we exchange contact information, so things can progress smoothly?” — and AK learns her given name in the process: "Also, I learned that Ichinose’s first name was Honami—an unnecessary bit of information."
[status] (p.110) Her reliability is measurable: "Every time she wanted permission to try something she’d contact us, even though she’d already said to leave things to her. I didn’t think she needed to limit herself so severely."
[scene] (p.114) A month after the entrance exam she is already her class's channel to the school's staff: "Thank you very much. It’s been a pleasure." — she has been at the dormitory manager's desk handling other students' complaints: “Some people from my class wanted to make requests about their dorms. I gathered everyone’s thoughts on the matter and told the dorm manager. Stuff like water usage, noise, and so on.”
[status] (p.114–115) Class B's self-organisation is the fact this scene exists to reveal: “Class Rep? Why Class Rep?” — “I’m my class’s representative. It seems like that, anyway.” and “No, that’s just something Class B set up on its own. I think it’s good to have some assigned roles, right?”
[rule] (p.115) The full informal officer set, in her own words: “Whether or not they serve a function is a different question, but we do have other roles for formality’s sake. Stuff like vice class representative, and secretary. They could be more useful when we have a cultural fair, or sports festival, or something. We could try deciding things on the spot, but that might lead to trouble.”
[trait] (p.115) AK's read of why she holds the role: “Most people wouldn’t want to be class representative. They’d be forced to do annoying stuff... However, with Ichinose taking the initiative for Class B, she probably didn’t foist things on others. I’d bet she handled her duties smoothly.”
[says] (p.115) She refuses the grandeur of it: “Do you think it’s weird? It’s all purely informal. Besides, there are quite a few troublemakers. We have to deal with a lot of stuff.” — AK's note: "As she said, 'We have to deal with a lot of stuff,' Ichinose laughed."
[scene] (p.114) Her physical entrance in this section is written by AK as attraction; it is his narration, not her characterisation: "She had beautiful long, wavy hair and big eyes. The second button of her blazer strained over her large chest. Her upright posture matched her dignified personality. I found myself more attracted to her cool temperament than how cute she was."
[rel:horikita-suzune] (p.108–110) She is the first person outside Class D Horikita will negotiate with, and the negotiation is conducted entirely by Horikita's hostility plus Horikita's yes.
[rel:ayanokouji-kiyotaka] (p.114–115) Two weeks in, she greets him first, knows his name, and walks to school with him — while he is still cataloguing her as an asset with unclear motive.

### y1v2 p.116–121 — Ch.4 §4.6: walking to school, and what she thinks the class system is hiding
[says] (p.116) "Aren’t you usually a little late? This reminds me that I’ve never seen you around this time." — his narration of the ask: "Ichinose’s question sounded harmless, as if she were following some kind of template."

But if students are ranked by overall ability, doesn’t that mean that the lower classes are at an overwhelming disadvantage?"
[says] (p.119) "If we were competing as individuals, sure. But this is a competition between classes, right? If you simply put all of the superior students into Class A, then doesn’t that mean the rest of us have virtually no chance of succeeding?"
[says] (p.119) "There’s definitely a big difference between classes A through D right now. However, I think they’re trying to hide something, but going about it in a weird way. Don’t you agree?"
[says] (p.120) "Ha, no reason, really. It’s just something that popped into my head. If it weren’t true, then it’d be fair to say the whole situation is cruel. I think that good students and good athletes were placed into Class D for a reason, as a countermeasure."
[says] (p.120) "I don’t think so. There’s a lot to be gained from an exchange of ideas. Also, since we’re cooperating now, there shouldn’t be any problems." — his gloss: "She wasn’t complacent about being in Class B. That was just Ichinose’s natural personality."
[says] (p.120) "I don’t mind if I’m the one who does the talking and thinking. If you think it’s helpful information, then it’s okay for you to use it."
[says] (p.121) "Has a girl ever confessed her feelings to you?" — the question she stops herself from explaining.
[trait] (p.119–120) She works out the class-sorting problem in front of a stranger from the worst class in the school, offers her conclusion to him, and refuses to treat it as leverage: the only structural analysis of the four-class system a student gives all volume arrives unprompted and unguarded.
[limit] (p.121) She asks the question she cannot follow up on: "Oh no, I’m sorry. It’s nothing." — and his note on it: "It didn’t look like it was nothing."
