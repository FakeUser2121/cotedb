### y1v1 p.20–22 — Bus priority seat debate: absolute egoism

[action] (p.20) Ignores the wobbling elderly woman standing near him. When Kushida approaches and politely asks him to give up his seat, he smirks and rebuffs her without shifting an inch.

[says] (p.20) “I don’t understand. Priority seats are just that: priority seats. I have no legal obligation to move. [action] (p.21) Flashes a pointlessly vigorous smile, slides his earphones in, and begins listening to cacophonous music; his smug, self-important attitude remains fixed as the office lady clenches her teeth.
[says] (p.21) “A contribution to society, you say? Well, that is a rather interesting opinion. It’s certainly true that offering up one’s seat to the elderly could be viewed in such a positive light. Unfortunately, I have no interest in contributing to society. I care only for my own satisfaction. Oh, and one more thing. You’re asking me, the one in the priority seat, to give up his spot, but couldn’t you simply ask one of the other people seated on this crowded bus? If you truly cared for the elderly, then something like priority seating would be a rather trivial concern, wouldn’t you agree?”
[trait] (p.20–22) Flawless aristocratic arrogance, unshakeable rationalized selfishness, completely immune to public shaming, peer pressure, or conventional morality; sharp philosophical debater who exposes the hypocrisy of the crowd.
