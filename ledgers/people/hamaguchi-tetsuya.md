### y1v4 p.82–90 — Ch.3 §3.1: the Class B counter
[scene] (p.82) First Class B hand in the Rabbit room, then the steady counter to Machida’s silence until he and Ichinose hold the middle of the circle.
[says] (p.82) “I agree, of course. We’re a group, and it’s only natural for us to cooperate,” said Tetsuya.
[trait] (p.82) Introduced as Hamaguchi Tetsuya — thin, somewhat androgynous, blue smooth hair; his early yes helps Ichinose’s opening trap work.
[says] (p.83) “Isn’t that a perfectly valid question, though? Ichinose-san didn’t threaten us. She didn’t order us to answer. If you don’t want to, you don’t have to respond,” he said.
[trait] (p.83) Coolly brushes aside all of Class A’s criticism; Machida isn’t fazed, having expected it.
[says] (p.83-84) “No, I think that your question was valid, Ichinose-san. Their wariness … If you simply refuse to engage, then I’m unconvinced you can offer anything of value,” Hamaguchi said.
[says] (p.84) “I don’t know specifically why Ichinose-san asked that question. However, this test requires us to come to a solution through discussion. If the students from Class A want to sit in silence, then we’ll have to carry on without you. At the very least, why not tell us what you think we should be discussing?”
[trait] (p.84) The narration: “Hamaguchi was right.” Machida knows it and keeps his arms crossed.
[says] (p.85) “Well, that’s a rather unique idea. How exactly will we come out on top without discussing things, though? Do you think we should let the VIP remain anonymous, and quit while we’re ahead?” Hamaguchi spoke up, looking mildly irritated.
[says] (p.85) “I can’t believe this. If anything, I’m starting to believe that the VIP is someone from Class A. Are you just sharing information with the VIP and taking measures to protect him or her?” Hamaguchi asked.
[says] (p.86) “I do see what you’re saying. But if we don’t know which class the VIP belongs to, the point gap between classes could be widened further. What if the VIP’s identity is extremely biased, and all VIPs are only chosen from one or two classes? That class would get millions of points. Although our class points won’t be affected, I’m sure everyone understands the importance of private points,” responded Hamaguchi.
[trait] (p.86) The private-points argument the narration calls valid — and notes it wasn’t going to work against Katsuragi’s fairness read anyway.
[says] (p.87) “It’s certainly true that the school has made a point of emphasizing its commitment to fairness. If we believe that, then you’d be right,” he said.
[says] (p.89) “We leave everything to you, Ichinose-san,” he replied. (trust from Class B called unshakeable; the other two B students nod)
[says] (p.90) “We agree with Ichinose-san,” said Hamaguchi.
[rel:ichinose-honami] (pp.82–90) The lieutenant — answers for Class B and defers the rest to her.
[rel:machida-kouji] (pp.83–85) The running opponent on introductions and the no-discussion pitch.
