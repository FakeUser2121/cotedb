He … bounced back and immediately tried to enroll in other schools. I did whatever was necessary to crush his attempts to advance. I made him give up. I did the same to Matsuo, too. I tarnished his reputation, leaving him unable to find a job. His son also lost his way, and is now unemployed,”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.61) “He begged me to leave the boy alone, and committed suicide last month by self-immolation.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.61) “Right now, his son’s working part-time, earning enough to live on and nothing else, with no guarantee of a future. No dreams. No hope. His family’s tragedy is your fault. The boy must surely bear a deep grudge against you. Even in death, he won’t forgive you.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.61) “The man who took care of you, who saved you, has died. And you show no reaction whatsoever. If Matsuo could see you now, he’d be full of regret.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.62) “Matsuo’s death has already been recorded. If necessary, I can send for the papers confirming it.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.62) “You’ve changed quite a bit, Kiyotaka.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.62) “What happened that year? What made you so determined to go to this school?”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.63) “I don’t entirely understand, but it seems that I have no choice but to accept. I suppose that temporarily suspending the facility’s activity was a mistake. To think that a plan sixteen years in the making could be ruined in just a year…”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.63) “I understand why you’re here,”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.63) “But if you think the matter is settled, you’re naïve. As with Matsuo’s son, I can force you to quit.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.63) “That’s a statement made without proof.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.63) “There’s no need to bring a bodyguard along to visit a high school.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.64) “Did Matsuo put that idea in your head? Even in death, he’s still impeding me.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.64) “Why would someone like you veer off your path? You were taught from the very beginning that there was no point learning unnecessary things.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.64) “Utter nonsense. The only path in this world is the one I prepared for you. You will one day surpass me, and become the person who guides Japan into the future. Why can’t you understand that?”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.64) “It seems I can’t get through to you.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.64) “The White Room has resumed operations. This time, my plans are perfect. Nothing will get in the way. I’m prepared to make up for lost time,”
[rel:ayanokouji-kiyotaka] (p.55–64) Son across the table; the whole scene is their first face-to-face in a year and a half.
[rel:matsuo] (p.59–64) Dead butler used as leverage; self-immolation last month after the son’s prospects were crushed.
[rel:chabashira-sae] (p.55–56) Bows out with the principal; her nerves framed the visit.
[feat] (p.59–61) Ruined a butler’s reputation, a son’s schooling and a family’s future without raising his voice; treats the suicide as a report.
[trait] (p.55–64) Views his son as property; speaks in corporate and White Room absolutes; admits the sixteen-year plan broke in one year; still claims the single prepared path.

### y1v7 p.65–68 — Ch.3: the ultimatum answered
[scene] (p.65) Gets the free-will refusal, watches Sakayanagi block the expulsion on autonomy and tuition rules, and leaves without a card left.
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.65) “Am I to assume a parent can’t lie to their own child?”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.65) “You have no plans to return?”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.65) “How foolish. This school’s nothing more than a barn full of common rabble. I’m sure that your own class holds many such worthless bottomfeeders with no hope of salvation.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.65) “You think that even incompetent morons can stand toe-to-toe with geniuses?”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.65) “You want to destroy my ideals, then.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.65) “We should end this. We know that, no matter how long we talk, we’ll never agree.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.66) “That’s right. Since it’s what his parent wishes, the school must immediately take appropriate action.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.66) “You’ve changed, too. Where did the old, agreeable you go?”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.67) “You’re free to succeed your father and carry on his wishes. However, if that’s your intention, then why did you allow Kiyotaka to enter this school?”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.67) “Don’t dodge the question. This school’s fundamentally unlike ordinary schools. Kiyotaka should never have been a suitable candidate for admission. I know that the interviews and exam are just for show,”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.67) “Kiyotaka never should’ve been among the possible candidates in the first place. It’s abnormal that you didn’t disqualify him.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.68) “Don’t screw with me,”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.68) “If that’s how things stand, I’ll find another way.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.68) “I understand. I haven’t the slightest intention of putting any pressure on you,”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.68) “In that case, we’re done talking. If you’ll please excuse me.”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.68) “When will we meet again?”
[says:ayanokouji-atsuomi] (p.68) “Coming to a place like this once is quite enough.”
[rel:ayanokouji-kiyotaka] (p.65–68) Free-will answer; parting jab about visiting the school again ignored.
[rel:sakayanagi-arisu] (p.65–68) Chairman Sakayanagi (father) blocks him on autonomy and government fees; nostalgia for the old agreeable days.
[feat] (p.67–68) Falls back to “find another way” and rule-compliant expulsion once the three-way-talk offer lands.
[trait] (p.65–68) Ideologue about genius and rabble; admits no pressure while implying force; leaves rather than argue with the board.
