### Chapter 2: Ibuki Mio has Surprisingly Good Sense (pp. 11–48)
- **Setting:** The dorm between exams, then Keyaki Mall’s fifth floor — the couples-only fortune-teller booth — and finally the elevator on the mall’s external wall, where Ayanokōji and Ibuki are trapped after their reading. (pp. 11–48)
- **Narrative Beats:**
  - Sudou’s call on his tournament rest day: 100,000 points arrive from Ayanokōji, the tournament is reported, and the real objective is an invitation for Horikita; the attempt fails and Sudou’s reply is “Then forget about it.” (pp. 11–17)
  - The fifth floor turns solo students away — guidance for couples only, Ukon-sensei reads for couples — and Ibuki, refused in the next line, pairs up with Ayanokōji; he reads her anxiety as hyper-awareness, and she calls him a future mass murderer. (pp. 18–27)
  - No partner works: Horikita would decline, Kushida is beyond his courage, the couples floor would fluster Sakura, the guys would not pair up — but the next day he and Ibuki happen upon the booth at the exact same time. (pp. 28–29)
  - They queue as a pair; Ibuki’s worry about the island grudge is dismissed on ability-versus-feelings grounds, and the narration concludes Class C is not that different from Class D. (pp. 30–32)
  - The reading: hooded elderly fortune-teller, Basic Plan for both, palm and pattern talk, then the pronouncement that Ayanokōji holds the fate tenchuusatsu — rare, overall outlook bad, no parental blessing, but the rest is up to him; the advice: straight home, no detours, no panic, work together if stuck. (pp. 33–37)
  - They take the detour anyway; the elevator grinds to a halt on the third floor, the emergency line is dead, both phones run out, and the emergency number is scribbled over with magic marker. (pp. 38–41)
  - The air conditioning dies in the summer heat; hatch and button schemes fill half an hour, and the floor-plus-close press moves the car — by coincidence the adults outside had used the remote at the same moment. (pp. 42–47)
  - Katsuragi is the man at the doors: the cut-off call reached him, Sudou “performed brilliantly,” and the friendship with Ayanokōji is treated as already told — the story began a little while ago. (p. 48)
