### Chapter 1: Chabashira Sae’s Soliloquy (pp. 10–12)
- **Setting:** No scene — pure first-person interior monologue, volume cold open. Chabashira narrates; the only other presence is an unnamed "certain young boy" she recalls standing before.
- **Narrative Beats:**
  - Opens with the Wings of Icarus myth as an interpretive frame: Greek mythology "deals abundantly with human frailty" (p.10); Daedalus's wax wings, Icarus's fall.
  - She connects the myth to a boy she stood before "considering what these past few months had brought" — the comparison because "he resembled Icarus" (p.10).
  - Immediately breaks the comparison: the boy "possessed neither the bravery nor the arrogance of Icarus" (p.11).
  - Confesses her provocation as a forced gamble: "I had been pushed into a corner. I had no choice but to do it." — she chose to "incur this boy's divine wrath" and turn his quiet rage toward herself; "The die cannot be returned once cast. The gamble had already begun." (p.11)
  - Volume's second straight non-Ayanokōji cold-open soliloquy (after Sakura in y1v2 ch1), this time the homeroom instructor's.
- **Page map note:** p.12 carries no prose (illustration/section-break page).
- **New named entities:** none — the chapter names only the mythological figures (Daedalus, Icarus, King Minos, Minotaur) inside the retelling.
- **Attribution gaps:** the boy is never named on pp.10–11; he stays unnamed in the records.
