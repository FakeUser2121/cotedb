### Chapter 1: Sakura Airi's Soliloquy (pp. 10–11)
- **Setting:** No scene — pure interior monologue, volume cold open.
- **Narrative Beats:**
  - First-person chapter from Sakura Airi (the only non-Ayanokōji POV chapter header in the volume's structure).
  - She confesses she cannot interact with people and "came up with a solution": adopting a false face to hide her true self.
  - Asks whether everyone else also performs a façade; concludes she'd never know because she can't connect — "Therefore, I remain alone."
  - Ends on the contradiction that drives her arc (p. 10): *"I… I want to connect with someone from the bottom of my heart."* then *"And so I will continue to live quietly, with my eyes downcast. Alone."*

### Chapter 2: The Sudden Beginning of Our Tumultuous Troubles (pp. 12–39)
- **Setting:** June 30 evening (school building, post-brawl) → July 1, Class D: homeroom, lunch, walk to dorms, Ayanokōji's room at night.
- **Narrative Beats:**
  - **Cold open, Sakura's POV (pp. 12–13):** Searching for a selfie spot, Sakura Airi walks on Sudou finishing a one-sided three-on-one beating; she photographs it silently, freezes, and flees before he can spot her ("If I'd lagged a mere two seconds, he probably would've seen me.", p.13). The volume's witness is seeded before the protagonist even knows the fight happened.
  - **2.1 Points day (pp. 15–18):** First class-point payout in Class D's history: 87 points vs Class A's 1004; Horikita reads the across-the-board ~100 gain as mere midterm reward; Hirata translates her cryptic "get something"; the deposit is then announced delayed for all first-years, with Chabashira's ominous "If there are still points left, that is." (p.19).
  - **2.2 Lunch (pp. 19–21):** AK's friends-but-alone paradox in full; Horikita's bento economics (free supermarket ingredients) and her surfboard roast; Kōenji and Sakura as fellow solo eaters.
  - **2.3 After school (pp. 22–26):** Chabashira drags Sudou to the faculty room; Horikita and AK walk home debating whether the point trouble is school-side or student-side; their hate-is-fine honesty about Kushida ("Kushida-san and I are like oil and water.", p.25).
  - **2.4 The crisis conference (pp. 27–38):** Sudou confesses: three Class C boys (Komiya, Kondou — his own basketball clubmates — plus Ishizaki) tried to blackmail him out of his shot at summer-tournament regular; he beat them, they reported first, and he faces a summer suspension plus class-wide point loss unless he produces proof by Tuesday. He felt someone watching (p.34) — the witness problem is live. Kushida drives the plan (get confessions or find the eyewitness), volunteers her friendship with Komiya's group, and pins a dodging AK to participation. AK's balance: 8,320 points (p.27).
  - **Chapter close (p.38):** "No matter how much I thought about it, I couldn't come up with any solutions."
- **New named entities:** Komiya, Kondou, Ishizaki (Class C, basketball), Hondou (Class D, mentioned).

### Chapter 3: Weak Point (pp. 40–65)
- **Setting:** July 2, one school day — homeroom, the cafeteria at lunch, then after school in Class D's classroom, the hallway and the entrance, and the doorway of Class B's classroom.
- **Narrative Beats:**
  - **Homeroom (pp.40–43):** Chabashira announces the Sudou–Class C fight to the whole class, in her "serene" neutrality, and puts out the school's terms: judgment next Tuesday, "taking into account any eyewitnesses and evidence" (p.41), and every teacher will brief their own class, so the affair becomes school-wide property (p.41). She asks for witnesses; nobody raises a hand — including the one person in the room who saw it. Ike's objection is arithmetic ("will we be at zero again this month?", p.41), Kushida then re-tells Sudou's whole account to the room (p.42), Yamauchi's one-sentence doubt about Sudou's junior-high bragging breaks her appeal (p.42–43), and only Hirata standing ("I want to believe him.") with Karuizawa after him turns the class into an investigation team (p.43).
  - **3.1 The cafeteria (pp.44–53):** Horikita refuses flatly — "I've never once considered you a friend. Nothing makes me more uncomfortable than being around someone who doesn't recognize his own stupidity." (p.45) — and the group cannot work out why, because they read her refusal as personal dislike. Kushida puts the question to AK (p.46) and he answers with a guess ("Horikita is smart, right? So I think she probably had some idea.", p.47), while privately holding her read: the school had already told the teachers enough for her to see "the possibility of a happy ending was almost nonexistent" (p.46). The rest of the scene is the boys' romance council — Ike's phone-tracking of Kushida (p.50), the "7 outta 10" ranking talk (p.51), Sudou's Horikita fantasy (p.51), and the girlfriend contest with its "Envy Tax" (p.53) — over which AK keeps noting that Sudou's problem is unresolved (p.53).
  - **3.2 After school (pp.54–63):** the class splits into canvassing teams — Hirata and Karuizawa's "Hero & Gal Team", Kushida's "Beautiful Girl & Entourage Team" (p.54). Kushida drags AK out of the room by asking, chases Horikita down to the entrance, and is refused twice: "I have an important daily routine, so I need my alone time." and "Maybe the Class C students really did start this fight, but Sudou-kun was also one of the perpetrators." (pp.56–57). Horikita then hands the explanation to AK in front of her ("Even though he pretends not to understand what I'm thinking, he probably does.", p.57) and AK gives Kushida the volume's coldest lecture — image-as-evidence, the two-suspects thought experiment, "the less information you have upon which to base your judgment, the more you have to rely on whatever meager information you have at hand" (pp.59–60). Kushida accepts the logic and rejects the conclusion ("I don't agree with abandoning Sudou-kun just to punish him.", p.60), then takes AK's advice on how to help (p.60), overrides his one-by-one plan in favour of asking several people at once (p.62), and leads the group to Class B on his ranking argument (p.61).
  - **3.3 Class B (p.64–65):** the away-classroom visit — Ike and Yamauchi frozen in the doorway, Kushida at home among strangers, Class B's hair and skirts less reserved than AK expected (pp.63–64), and AK's window-side grumble "I wanna go home." answered by Ike's "I think people who participate in clubs are stupid" (p.64). The chapter closes mid-hook: "I waited for a few minutes until I received Kushida's report. I certainly didn't expect what she told me." (p.64).
  - **What this chapter is about, structurally:** it is the volume's pressure test on each character's *stated principle* — Horikita's class-rank logic against Kushida's unconditional friendship against Hirata's faith in classmates — with the actual witness sitting silent in the same building the whole time.
- **Page map note:** pp.49, 58 and 65 carry no prose (illustration/section-break pages); Chapter 4 begins on p.66.
- **New named entities:** none. New system facts: the school phone's registered-friend location tracking (p.50) and the deferred disciplinary judgment dated to next Tuesday (p.41).
- **Threads left open:** who watched AK in the two-thirds-empty classroom (p.62); whether any Class B student saw anything (p.64); Horikita's refusal standing until Tuesday (p.41).
