### Chapter 1: Sakura Airi's Soliloquy (pp. 10–11)
- **Setting:** No scene — pure interior monologue, volume cold open.
- **Narrative Beats:**
  - First-person chapter from Sakura Airi (the only non-Ayanokōji POV chapter header in the volume's structure).
  - She confesses she cannot interact with people and "came up with a solution": adopting a false face to hide her true self.
  - Asks whether everyone else also performs a façade; concludes she'd never know because she can't connect — "Therefore, I remain alone."
  - Ends on the contradiction that drives her arc (p. 10): *"I… I want to connect with someone from the bottom of my heart."* then *"And so I will continue to live quietly, with my eyes downcast. Alone."*

### Chapter 2: The Sudden Beginning of Our Tumultuous Troubles (pp. 12–39)
- **Setting:** June 30 evening (school building, post-brawl) → July 1, Class D: homeroom, lunch, walk to dorms, Ayanokōji's room at night.
- **Narrative Beats:**
  - **Cold open, Sakura's POV (pp. 12–13):** Searching for a selfie spot, Sakura Airi walks on Sudou finishing a one-sided three-on-one beating; she photographs it silently, freezes, and flees before he can spot her ("If I'd lagged a mere two seconds, he probably would've seen me.", p.13). The volume's witness is seeded before the protagonist even knows the fight happened.
  - **2.1 Points day (pp. 15–18):** First class-point payout in Class D's history: 87 points vs Class A's 1004; Horikita reads the across-the-board ~100 gain as mere midterm reward; Hirata translates her cryptic "get something"; the deposit is then announced delayed for all first-years, with Chabashira's ominous "If there are still points left, that is." (p.19).
  - **2.2 Lunch (pp. 19–21):** AK's friends-but-alone paradox in full; Horikita's bento economics (free supermarket ingredients) and her surfboard roast; Kōenji and Sakura as fellow solo eaters.
  - **2.3 After school (pp. 22–26):** Chabashira drags Sudou to the faculty room; Horikita and AK walk home debating whether the point trouble is school-side or student-side; their hate-is-fine honesty about Kushida ("Kushida-san and I are like oil and water.", p.25).
  - **2.4 The crisis conference (pp. 27–38):** Sudou confesses: three Class C boys (Komiya, Kondou — his own basketball clubmates — plus Ishizaki) tried to blackmail him out of his shot at summer-tournament regular; he beat them, they reported first, and he faces a summer suspension plus class-wide point loss unless he produces proof by Tuesday. He felt someone watching (p.34) — the witness problem is live. Kushida drives the plan (get confessions or find the eyewitness), volunteers her friendship with Komiya's group, and pins a dodging AK to participation. AK's balance: 8,320 points (p.27).
  - **Chapter close (p.38):** "No matter how much I thought about it, I couldn't come up with any solutions."
- **New named entities:** Komiya, Kondou, Ishizaki (Class C, basketball), Hondou (Class D, mentioned).

### Chapter 3: Weak Point (pp. 40–65)
- **Setting:** July 2, one school day — homeroom, the cafeteria at lunch, then after school in Class D's classroom, the hallway and the entrance, and the doorway of Class B's classroom.
- **Narrative Beats:**
  - **Homeroom (pp.40–43):** Chabashira announces the Sudou–Class C fight to the whole class, in her "serene" neutrality, and puts out the school's terms: judgment next Tuesday, "taking into account any eyewitnesses and evidence" (p.41), and every teacher will brief their own class, so the affair becomes school-wide property (p.41). She asks for witnesses; nobody raises a hand — including the one person in the room who saw it. Ike's objection is arithmetic ("will we be at zero again this month?", p.41), Kushida then re-tells Sudou's whole account to the room (p.42), Yamauchi's one-sentence doubt about Sudou's junior-high bragging breaks her appeal (p.42–43), and only Hirata standing ("I want to believe him.") with Karuizawa after him turns the class into an investigation team (p.43).
  - **3.1 The cafeteria (pp.44–53):** Horikita refuses flatly — "I've never once considered you a friend. Nothing makes me more uncomfortable than being around someone who doesn't recognize his own stupidity." (p.45) — and the group cannot work out why, because they read her refusal as personal dislike. Kushida puts the question to AK (p.46) and he answers with a guess ("Horikita is smart, right? So I think she probably had some idea.", p.47), while privately holding her read: the school had already told the teachers enough for her to see "the possibility of a happy ending was almost nonexistent" (p.46). The rest of the scene is the boys' romance council — Ike's phone-tracking of Kushida (p.50), the "7 outta 10" ranking talk (p.51), Sudou's Horikita fantasy (p.51), and the girlfriend contest with its "Envy Tax" (p.53) — over which AK keeps noting that Sudou's problem is unresolved (p.53).
  - **3.2 After school (pp.54–63):** the class splits into canvassing teams — Hirata and Karuizawa's "Hero & Gal Team", Kushida's "Beautiful Girl & Entourage Team" (p.54). Kushida drags AK out of the room by asking, chases Horikita down to the entrance, and is refused twice: "I have an important daily routine, so I need my alone time." and "Maybe the Class C students really did start this fight, but Sudou-kun was also one of the perpetrators." (pp.56–57). Horikita then hands the explanation to AK in front of her ("Even though he pretends not to understand what I'm thinking, he probably does.", p.57) and AK gives Kushida the volume's coldest lecture — image-as-evidence, the two-suspects thought experiment, "the less information you have upon which to base your judgment, the more you have to rely on whatever meager information you have at hand" (pp.59–60). Kushida accepts the logic and rejects the conclusion ("I don't agree with abandoning Sudou-kun just to punish him.", p.60), then takes AK's advice on how to help (p.60), overrides his one-by-one plan in favour of asking several people at once (p.62), and leads the group to Class B on his ranking argument (p.61).
  - **3.3 Class B (p.64–65):** the away-classroom visit — Ike and Yamauchi frozen in the doorway, Kushida at home among strangers, Class B's hair and skirts less reserved than AK expected (pp.63–64), and AK's window-side grumble "I wanna go home." answered by Ike's "I think people who participate in clubs are stupid" (p.64). The chapter closes mid-hook: "I waited for a few minutes until I received Kushida's report. I certainly didn't expect what she told me." (p.64).
  - **What this chapter is about, structurally:** it is the volume's pressure test on each character's *stated principle* — Horikita's class-rank logic against Kushida's unconditional friendship against Hirata's faith in classmates — with the actual witness sitting silent in the same building the whole time.
- **Page map note:** pp.49, 58 and 65 carry no prose (illustration/section-break pages); Chapter 4 begins on p.66.
- **New named entities:** none. New system facts: the school phone's registered-friend location tracking (p.50) and the deferred disciplinary judgment dated to next Tuesday (p.41).
- **Threads left open:** who watched AK in the two-thirds-empty classroom (p.62); whether any Class B student saw anything (p.64); Horikita's refusal standing until Tuesday (p.41).

### Chapter 4: An Unexpected Witness (pp. 66–151)
- **Setting:** the days between the July 2 announcement and the following Sunday afternoon — Class D's classroom and its hallway, the doorway of Class B, the corridor and stairwell of the special building after school, Ayanokōji's dorm room and the dorm lobby, the route to school, the spot behind the gymnasium, the tree-lined pathway, and a shopping mall with a school-licensed electronics store. Disciplinary judgment has been promised for Tuesday and does not arrive inside this chapter; the mall trip is dated by the line "On Sunday afternoon, I went to the shopping mall to meet Kushida and fulfill my promise" (p.132), and the love letter is timed for "Friday evening at 4:00 PM, behind the gymnasium" (p.123).
- **Narrative Beats:**
  - **4.0 The class-change audience (pp.66–74):** the canvassing of Class B pays out in the worst possible currency — a homeroom discussion of whether a student can buy their way into a better class. The volume's point economics are dumped on the class in one sitting: 20,000,000 points as the price of a class change, a private-point ceiling of 3.6–4 million, the story of a twelve-million-point fraudster who was expelled, club and philanthropic activity as sources of class points, and the school's test-point shop (pp.68–72). Chabashira glances at the two students who have actually bought a test point (p.68); the narrator's verdict is that "our homeroom teacher was being extremely and deliberately mean" (p.71). Ayanokōji moves Horikita not with feeling but with arithmetic — "Doesn’t this mean there’s some value in saving Sudou?" (p.72) — and stops the instant she concedes (p.73).
  - **4.1 Horikita names the witness (pp.75–82):** while the class still believes it has no lead, Horikita produces one from an observation nobody else logged — when Kushida announced she was looking for a witness, Sakura looked down (p.77). The room cannot place Sakura; Sudō misnames her, Yamauchi denies ever having confessed to her and then retracts the denial in the same breath (p.79–82), and the narrator admits he "could barely match Sakura’s name to a face" (p.82). Ike is headlocked for describing her body out loud. The chapter's plan is set by the one person who can reach her: "Well, I’ll try to talk to her myself tomorrow," Kushida says (p.82).
  - **4.2–4.3 The approach fails in public (pp.83–96):** Hirata agrees to greet Sakura but refuses to be the one who pressures her (p.83–84), Karuizawa is ruled out in advance as too blunt for her (p.83), and Kushida's attempt collapses in front of the whole class — the camera is knocked over, Sakura apologises and flees (p.86–90). Ayanokōji alone reads it as a flight response, citing cultural anthropologist Edward Hall's intimate space (p.88), and notices she concealed her dominant hand (p.88). Kushida then asks him publicly whether he is Sudō's ally (p.91); Horikita writes Sudō off aloud, the class's last organised defence of him dies in an argument with Kōenji, a desk is kicked apart, and Hirata physically stops him (p.92–93). Ike gives up on the case at p.91; the narrator discovers the classroom's hidden cameras at p.95, and Horikita asks him to walk back with her at p.96.
  - **4.4 The special building, and Class B's offer (pp.97–107):** at the scene of the fight the narrator tells Horikita "Sudou was the first friend that I made here. I want to help him." (p.97) and privately states the case's actual state — "Our only proof was Sudou’s word. We were completely in the dark." (p.100). Sakura passes them and neither of them can make her testify; he lets her go (p.102). Ichinose Honami of Class B arrives, hears the whole account from a boy she barely knows (p.104), and offers help on the ground that "If the party that lied wins, that’d set a bad precedent" (p.106). Horikita decides, not him: "I didn’t really have the power to decide in the first place; that was Horikita’s job." (p.106). The information gap becomes his stated grievance against the school (p.107).
  - **4.5–4.6 Horikita in his room, then the dorm lobby (pp.108–115):** she follows him home, calls coming there "a shameful act in itself. A disgrace." (p.111), checks his room for cameras, and delivers the volume's most dangerous read of him — "You’re hiding in plain sight. You disgust me more than anyone else." (p.112). In the dorm lobby the next morning, Ichinose greets him by name: the contact exchange (p.109), the discovery that Class B invented its own offices (class representative, vice representative, secretary) while Class D has none (p.114–115), and the report that Class A's homeroom teacher gets a special bonus (p.108).
  - **4.6–4.7 Walking to school, and the confession behind the gymnasium (pp.116–127):** Ichinose and the narrator walk to school, and the chapter stops being about Sudō for eleven pages. She volunteers Class B's figures ("We have about 600 points, so our classes are around 350 points apart.", p.117), expects a hidden summer task bigger than either exam (p.117), and argues out loud that the four classes were not sorted by ability alone — "I think that good students and good athletes were placed into Class D for a reason, as a countermeasure." (p.120). Then she asks whether a girl has ever confessed to him (p.121), and after class makes him wait behind the gymnasium as a fake boyfriend so a Class B girl named Chihiro will be hurt less by rejection (p.124). He refuses to be the lie: "I’m just a friend." (p.126), lectures both of them on what a confession deserves, walks away without waiting for the answer, and she goes back and tells the truth anyway (p.127).
  - **4.8 Kushida's phone call (pp.128–131):** she calls him late, first of her own accord, to take responsibility for Sakura's broken camera and to borrow him for the repair errand (p.128–129); she carries Horikita's conclusion — "Horikita-san seems convinced that Sakura saw everything." (p.129) — and the class's whole estimate of itself: "Class D was able to come together, thanks to you and Hirata." (p.130). He asks whether her room is on the ninth floor (p.130) and the call dies into a silence she starts three sentences inside and finishes with nothing: "No, it’s nothing." (p.131).
  - **4.9–4.10 The mall, the repair counter, the witness (pp.132–146):** she tests whether he has been excluded from his own friend group — "I’m talking about Ike-kun and the others going to that café, of course." (p.133) — then Sakura appears in a hat and a surgical mask, already sitting unnoticed on the same bench (p.136). At the counter Sakura freezes in "blatant fear and revulsion" (p.138); the clerk's interest is tolerated, and the narrator sees what the two girls do not: "the clerk had unsettling eyes" (p.140). He takes the pen, writes his own name and dorm room on the repair form, and daring the clerk to object (p.140) — the repair is quoted at two weeks (p.140). He then phones Sotomura, "the Professor", from the shop floor about a device he never names, priced against a laptop another student bought for 80,000 points, and books the trip as a "preliminary inspection" because he does not have enough points to buy anything (p.143–144). Kushida asks whether they have met before, asks to see Sakura without her glasses, is refused, and lets it go (p.144). She then declines the thanks, asks Sakura to stop speaking so formally, and is answered by a girl who agrees to try (p.145). The chapter's turn arrives as a gift offered for a day of errands: "I-I may be able to help you with Sudou-kun’s case." — "In her own words, Sakura told us that she was the witness." (p.146).
  - **4.11 Three calls, none of them finished (pp.147–151):** that night the narrator and Kushida reconstruct Sakura's armour — the fake glasses, deduced from the absence of lens distortion, and his conclusion that "she probably wears them because she doesn’t want others to see her true self." (p.148). Sakura calls him instead of her (p.148–149), says thank you, asks "What were you…thinking about?" in a question nobody can answer, and hangs up with "I’m sorry, it’s nothing. Goodnight." (p.149). Kushida confirms she heard nothing. The volume's last page of Chapter 4 is the narrator admitting that his puzzle pieces are "too small, and too few", and deciding he cannot approach Sakura without making her stand out (p.150).
  - **What this chapter is about, structurally:** the case is solved by everyone except the people whose job it was — Horikita is right about the witness and cannot reach her, Kushida reaches her but cannot extract the testimony in a classroom, Hirata refuses to ask at all, and the two students nobody credits (the narrator, and a girl the class cannot picture) close it in a shopping mall. The chapter's second half then refuses to be about Sudō at all: it spends eleven pages on a confession Class D never hears about, and ends on a witness who has promised to testify and a boy who cannot work out why she telephoned him.
- **Page map note:** pp.87, 134 and 151 carry no prose (illustration/section-break pages); p.113 carries only its closing two lines. Chapter 5 begins on p.152.
- **New named entities:** Ichinose Honami is formally named and given her first name (p.109); Hoshinomiya Chie, Class B's homeroom teacher, is described through Ichinose (p.108); Chihiro, an unsorted Class B girl who confesses to Ichinose (p.125–127); Sotomura, "the Professor", is called by name and re-enters as the volume's technical expert (p.143); Yukimura is named once, in the narrator's list of Class D's misfits (p.119); Higashiyama-sensei is remembered as the P.E. instructor who promised 5000 points for the best time in their first pool class (p.71); the school-licensed mall stores and their clerk enter as locations and a minor antagonist (p.138–141).
- **New system facts:** the class-change price and its ceiling, the twelve-million-point expulsion, club and philanthropic activity as class-point sources, the test-point shop and its verification (p.68–72); classroom cameras as the grading instrument, with none in the hallways (p.95); the homeroom-teacher bonus rumour (p.108); the self-made Class B offices against Class D's absence of them (p.114–115); the school-linked electronics store, its warranty and repair turnaround (p.138–140); and the first hard consumer price a student states, 80,000 points for a laptop (p.143).
- **Threads left open:** Tuesday's judgment, still not delivered (p.41, carried forward); what Sakura wanted to say on the phone (p.149); whether Kushida's interrupted sentence and the ninth-floor question are connected (p.130–131); who Chihiro is to Class B's balance of power (p.127); the unnamed device being price-checked at the mall (p.143–144); Ike's café gathering that the narrator was not told about (p.133); and the camera itself, out for a two-week repair that this chapter does not wait for (p.140).
- **Attribution gaps in the translation, recorded rather than guessed:** an untagged modest reply in the p.129–130 phone exchange ("I’m just doing what I normally do. It wasn’t anything special.") is not assigned to either speaker; the question about whether Sakura photographs people (p.142) is untagged; the first exchange of the p.147 night call is untagged on both sides; the reply "You kind of look suspicious…" (p.136) sits between two named speakers with no tag of its own; and in the p.48–53 cafeteria scene and the p.90–96 classroom exchange, several lines have no speaker named and are absent from these records rather than attributed.
