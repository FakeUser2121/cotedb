### Chapter 1: Sudou Ken’s Soliloquy (pp. 11–12)
- **Setting:** No scene — first-person soliloquy, volume cold open. Sudou narrates alone; no other character is present and no school business is named.
- **Narrative Beats:**
  - Opens on self-assessment: “To be completely honest, I ain’t really a capable person.” — his mother abandoned the family to go into the nightclub business, and he decided to get stronger. (p.11)
  - The father’s hunched back and janitor’s toil sickened him: “Being the idiot I am, I decided to give up on studyin’ and go into sports.” (p.11)
  - Sports path: solo sports first — tennis and pingpong, good but never top-tier — then basketball, which he got into seriously despite not being a team player; he kept improving and was recommended to a high school with one of the best basketball teams in the entire country. (p.11)
  - The recommendation was pulled after he got in some trouble, and the setback becomes his self-read: “That was when I really understood the painful truth: I was trash, born from trash.” (p.11)
  - The school choice: “That was why I chose this school.” — a dream school that cost no money and could guarantee even his future. (p.11)
  - Fourth consecutive non-Ayanokōji cold-open soliloquy (Sakura in y1v2, Chabashira in y1v3, Karuizawa in y1v4).

### Chapter 2: The School Sports Festival Begins (pp. 13–38)
- **Setting:** Post-summer review at the crowded café Pallet (back table), then Class 1-D’s afternoon homeroom, the joint Red Team assembly in Gymnasium 1, and Ayanokōji’s walk back to the dorm.
- **Narrative Beats:**
  - The zodiac debrief (pp.13–15): at Pallet, Horikita, Karuizawa, Hirata and Ayanokōji compare notes on the cruise-ship VIP exam — the rabbit group read alphabetically made Ayanokōji the VIP, the dragon group’s fifth name was Kushida’s — and Horikita offers the Class C theory: a dissatisfied student in Ryuuen’s class must have tossed the wrong answer in defiance. Hirata proposes roping Kushida in as an ally; Horikita refuses outright. Karuizawa now calls Hirata Yousuke-kun; the no-presence banter ends with Horikita demanding to know if she’s taken for a fool.
  - Chabashira’s briefing (pp.16–25): the school sports festival returns, with heavy physical education from September to early October. Red Team = Classes A and D, White Team = Classes B and C, one team per colour across all grade levels. Individual events score 15/12/10/8 (fifth place −1, one more per place below), recommended events 50/30/15/10 (fifth −2, two more below), a team win 500, the relay three times the recommended values. The handout deducts 100 class points from the first-, second- and third-year batches of the two classes on the losing team; grade ranking adds 50 to each grade’s first-place class, nothing for second, subtracts 50 for third and 100 for fourth; Chabashira stresses that points move class-by-class, and a team loss plus fourth place costs 200 in total. Individual winners choose 5000/3000/1000 private points or 3/2/1 written-test points (non-transferable); last place loses 1000 private points or one test point; the overall MVP takes 100,000 private points, each grade’s top three 10,000 each; the ten lowest scorers per year lose ten points on the next written exam. Thirteen events, a consensus participation table with a fixed deadline (random assignment if missed), substitutes at 100,000 private points each, health calls left to the student — and the winning team’s pay: “Their reward will be not having any points deducted.” (p.18)
  - The huddle (pp.23–25): Sudou, Ike and Yamauchi pull Horikita into the free homeroom period — Sudou wants every recommended event, Ike wants a personal shot at points, Horikita counters that stamina isn’t unlimited; no decisions.
  - The assembly (pp.26–34): Fujimaki takes Red Team command instead of the student council president; Katsuragi and Hirata agree to keep out of each other’s way; Ryuuen walks Class C out of Ichinose’s cooperation talk; Sakayanagi Arisu is introduced — disabled, cane, silver hair, Class A’s other leader with nearly all the followers — and Katsuragi warns Horikita off Ryuuen. Ayanokōji then argues the festival is not a “special exam” (the school never used the term), that preparation beats loophole-hunting, and that Ryuuen likely already has tactics in place — “You’re not thinking big enough,” he tells Horikita.
  - The gate (pp.35–38): alone after class with a file on his phone — whatever it showed, no traps seem necessary — Ayanokōji runs into Chabashira hosing the grounds; she warns that without results soon she’ll stop covering for him, and he answers that he’s free to decide his own path.
