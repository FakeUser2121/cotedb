### Prologue: The Monologue of Ichinose Honami (pp. 6–11)
- **Setting:** Ichinose Honami’s first-person retrospective; a one-on-one interview in the student council room on a day off, after she found the letter calling her a criminal.
- **Narrative beats:**
  - p.6: Ichinose never saw herself as good or bad; comfortable elementary and middle school; by third year of middle school she was student council president and offered a private high school scholarship; then one unforgivable mistake — her mother collapsed from illness and her little sister was hurt and left a shell of herself.
  - p.7: She wasted half her third year as a shut-in for half a year; learning about this school made her resolve to put an end to that and bring back their smiles; she swore an oath to face her own answer; at school she found a letter and froze — “‘Ichinose Honami is a criminal.’” Classmates turned to her with curious looks; the words did not change however many times she read them; cut to the student council room interview with Vice President Nagumo.
  - p.8: President Horikita had interviewed Ichinose and rejected her student-council application; Nagumo approached her for three reasons — Class B like himself, outstanding academic ability, and extremely good-looking (the last is what he chiefly seeks); the important question is whether she is worth keeping at his side as personal property; he cites her middle-school presidency and her entrance-exam results via Hoshinomiya-sensei; she accepts the praise but cannot relax.
  - p.9: Nagumo lies that Horikita rejected her out of hand for not being Class A and that the man obsesses over status; the narration states Horikita assesses talent from D or A alike; Ichinose asks whether she must promote to Class A; Nagumo says even promotion may not gain Horikita’s acknowledgment and that she was never considered up to par from enrollment; her smile vanishes; she stammers that he is also Class B yet vice president.
  - p.10: Nagumo would love her as personal property; he claims he confessed the full truth behind his own Class B assignment when applying to the council and that it resulted in his position; he refuses to answer what that confession was; he presses why a student with her grades, interpersonal skills and middle-school presidency is in Class B; Ichinose cannot hide her agitation; the hypothesis is built from information he obtained from her homeroom teacher.
  - p.11: “Someone who is unable to grab hold of this opportunity has no place in the student council.”; Ichinose harbors a secret — half a year shut in her room floods back; “What I say here-” / “It won’t leave this room, of course. Your secret is our secret.”; she had held the past without telling a soul; because she lost the trust of others she needs to trust in others; she opens up about everything — her own ‘mistake’.

### Chapter 1: The Student Council President’s Intentions (pp. 14–25)
- **Setting:** Early February after the training camp, student council room — Sakayanagi Arisu (Class 1-A) visits second-year Class A president Nagumo Miyabi; she leaves and meets Kushida Kikyō outside.
- **Narrative beats:**
  - p.14: The room has become flashy and formal — wallpaper changed, Nagumo’s personal belongings moved in bulk; Sakayanagi reads it as a symbol of his might; Nagumo asks whether Horikita-senpai recommended her for the council; she says she was not invited and is not a fit; he says he just doesn’t have a good eye for that sort of stuff.
  - p.15: Sakayanagi asks what would earn his seal of approval; he says appropriate talent and invites her to his side; she refuses — two leaders in one organization would humiliate the seniors; Nagumo does not grow angry and names both her and Ryuuen as interesting first years; narration: no student considers making the council their enemy except those like Sakayanagi and Ryuuen.
  - p.16: Nagumo is setting aside time for her and mentions another ‘1st year’ has an appointment; Sakayanagi states she will launch an attack against student-council member Ichinose Honami-san of Class B and that it may get stormy; Nagumo has heard this before from their previous meeting alone.
  - p.17: “I don’t recall giving you permission to ‘go that far’, Sakayanagi.”; Nagumo’s smile vanishes for the first time; she says she only had permission to bully Ichinose and intends to play rough; “Honami is my personal property I plan on loving. I only gave you permission to weaken her a bit.”; Sakayanagi asks whether he minds if Ichinose ends up expelled; “You’re a bold woman. Aren’t you afraid of me?” / “It’s just in my nature.”
  - p.18: Sakayanagi could have acted without asking; she still came for permission — meaning she does not wish to make him an enemy; Nagumo says the information he gave her is proving useful and the student council will turn a blind eye to her actions; she catches the word ‘too’; he says there is no duplicity and asks what she is planning; she leaves it at that; narration: Nagumo is not the least bit trustworthy and will throw away someone who could become an asset.
  - p.19: She asks about using violence as a drastic measure; Nagumo does not dislike violence, fights are normal, and unlike Horikita-senpai he will not crack down — a skirmish he would laugh off; she asks whether he would have sentenced the Class D–Class C first-year fight (Sudou and Ishizaki’s groups) differently from the previous president; he will not push them to expulsion and would suspend the parties involved.
  - p.20: Sakayanagi declines an escort and says fighting with the pieces she holds is her modus operandi; she wanted to know how far she can take it until it is no longer safe; she is satisfied; then raises the topic of a student purchasing private points from about-to-graduate third years — a strategy using what the school collects pre-graduation as post-graduation currency, “a surefire way to graduate from Class A.”
  - p.21: It came up during Kōenji and Nagumo’s conversation at the training camp — information only the boys overheard; private points are collected at graduation and worthless after, so a third year may entrust them to an underclassman; Nagumo realized Kōenji moved early; Sakayanagi asks why he boldly declared this to a first year — intending to alter the limiting rule; “Kōenji seems to be the only one in possession of an amount greater than what the school permits after all. It’s a form of rule violation.”
  - p.22: Nagumo never liked the school’s rule that allows saving up 20 million points to rise to Class A and would like to revise the system itself — though repeal would not apply to the first years; no student has independently saved 20 million so far; it is meaningless to save up as a class; spy strategies are not realistic because Class A privilege turns spies traitor; there is no merit in knocking down a strong class.
  - p.23: Trust as a weapon — betrayal by someone regarded as certain not to betray causes critical damage; Sakayanagi says Nagumo was too quick to throw away the trust he established; he answers while holding back laughter: he lost Horikita-senpai’s trust and the third-year Class A students’ trust, but nothing changed for the second years and other third years, and the first years will understand right away; narration: even breaking the rules he established with Horikita Manabu had been planned from the beginning, perhaps a consensus the second years reached beforehand.
  - p.24: Nagumo asks about Ayanokōji; “Ayanokōji…? I am somewhat familiar with that name. He’s a 1st year, is he not?”; Sakayanagi offers to investigate him; he says he said something unnecessary and to forget it; she leaves and runs into Kushida Kikyō of Class C — “Hello, Sakayanagi-san.”; Kushida says she was thinking of applying for the student council and asks if Sakayanagi is the same.
  - p.25: Sakayanagi excuses herself; she felt doubtful Kushida would want to join the council at a time like this, though an honor student aiming for the council would not be out of place; the girls are aware of Nagumo’s actions during the special exam; there has not been a single bad rumor about Kushida — “Fufu. It’s exactly that sort of person who’d unexpectedly turn villainous though.”; at the very least Sakayanagi does not believe she is purely good.
