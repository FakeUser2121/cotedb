      Arc

      y1v3

      • Situation: Debut as Class C's self-declared tyrant, summoning Horikita to his territory.
      • Knowledge state: Already tracking which Class D student moves behind the scenes.
      • Changes: Opens the hunt for the 'puppet master' — treats the whole year as a game board with everyone as a target.
      • Why it matters: Establishes his mode: predation as entertainment.
      • Evidence: y1v3 p.241 · y1v3 p.108

      y1v4

      • Situation: Island and VIP tests just ended; he debriefs Horikita one-on-one.
      • Knowledge state: Reads Kushida's identity from body language alone; knows Class D had outside help.
      • Changes: Admits shock ('The results of the test shocked me'), pitches a class-A-crushing alliance, and declares everyone in Horikita's orbit a target.
      • Why it matters: First evidence he respects outcomes over ego — and first exposure of his lie-reading.
      • Evidence: y1v4 p.74 · y1v4 p.239 · y1v4 p.240

      y1v5

      • Situation: Sports festival: Kushida leaked Class D's participation table; Kinoshita 'accident' escalates.
      • Knowledge state: Holds the leak, the match rigging, and the extortion leverage.
      • Changes: Demands Horikita kneel or pay a million points; 'I wanted to crush you. That was reason enough.'
      • Why it matters: Peak confidence — and the moment the anonymous recording first cuts him off.
      • Evidence: y1v5 p.155 · y1v5 p.198 · y1v5 p.199

      y1v6

      • Situation: Final exams: he runs the phone-check inquisition to find the traitor.
      • Knowledge state: The X recording proves someone inside his class feeds Horikita protection.
      • Changes: Frames and breaks Kushida with planted evidence ('Evidence trumps theory'), escalates to all-out war on Class D.
      • Why it matters: His surveillance style is rule-bound empiricism, not random cruelty — the method that later fails against X.
      • Evidence: y1v6 p.63 · y1v6 p.67 · y1v6 p.202

      y1v7

      • Situation: School ship: he corners Karuizawa to unmask X; Ayanokouji walks in and reveals himself.
      • Knowledge state: Learns X's identity the hardest possible way — and that Ayanokouji's power was never in doubt.
      • Changes: Beaten in his own stronghold; accepts responsibility ('I take responsibility for everything') and the expulsion that follows without appealing.
      • Why it matters: The fall is the hinge of his whole series arc.
      • Evidence: y1v7 p.171 · y1v7 p.183 · y1v7 p.193

      y1v7.5

      • Situation: Expelled, rebuilding from outside the school's walls.
      • Knowledge state: Still wired in: Kushida contacts him, he tracks the Nagumo situation.
      • Changes: 'Save it. I've always been alone' — the lone-wolf self-image stated plainly while he re-plans the board.
      • Why it matters: Post-fall he trades terror for leverage: same appetite, no institutional power behind it.
      • Evidence: y1v7.5 p.79 · y1v7.5 p.89

      y2v6

      • Situation: Returned and negotiating an alliance contract with Ayanokouji against Sakayanagi.
      • Knowledge state: Accepts that the old hierarchy inverted: the man who beat him now sets terms.
      • Changes: 'You're the man who beat me and shut me up' (y2v2) hardens into a signed contract; he agrees to hold Sakayanagi as his side's contribution.
      • Why it matters: Respect earned through defeat — the first relation in the series he submits to voluntarily.
      • Evidence: y2v2 p.305 · y2v6 p.208 · y2v6 p.216

      y2v12

      • Situation: End-of-year exam concludes his long revenge line against Sakayanagi.
      • Knowledge state: He has ground the distance down for a full year.
      • Changes: '...I've finally closed the gap' — the post-expulsion rebuild declared complete.
      • Why it matters: Fall (y1v7) → gap-closed (y2v12) is the cleanest two-point arc any main character gets.
      • Evidence: y2v12 p.196 · y2v12 p.42

      y3v1

      • Situation: Now a working senior figure in the class game — listened to, critiqued, recruiting.
      • Knowledge state: Fully integrated into cooperative play he once only raided.
      • Changes: 'I'll listen. Why do you think that?' — the tyrant asks for reasoning; peers evaluate his leadership.
      • Why it matters: Authority replaced the terror he used to rule with.
      • Evidence: y3v1 p.140 · y3v1 p.191

      Psychology & competing readings

      • His violence is procedural, not chaotic: every campaign has rules, evidence, and a stated price (confidence 0.85)
        • Evidence: y1v6 p.64 · y1v6 p.67 · y1v5 p.155
        • Reading: Directly competes with the 'sadist brute' surface reading: he confiscates phones by protocol, offers kneel-or-points choices, and reasons from evidence ('Evidence trumps theory').
      • He and Ayanokouji share methods but not minds — the series' explicit mirror pair (confidence 0.8)
        • Evidence: y1v7.5 p.81 · y1v7 p.183
        • Reading: Ryuuen states it himself: 'similar methods, but fundamentally different thought processes.' His engine is amusement; Ayanokouji's is ends. Competing nuance: after the contract (y2v6) the distinction blurs into instrumental partnership.
      • He governs by appetite — fun is the sovereign value, points only instrumental (confidence 0.8)
        • Evidence: y1v7 p.176 · y2v12 p.42 · y1v4 p.74
        • Reading: Competing reading: the 'fun' is partly persona — y1v6's rule-bound inquisition and y2v6's contract discipline show a calculating operator wearing amusement as armour. The text keeps both live.
      • The y1v7 defeat made him better — humility he never had came through losing (confidence 0.75)
        • Evidence: y1v7 p.193 · y2v2 p.305 · y2v12 p.196
        • Reading: Counter: he was already outcome-respectful before the fall (y1v4 'shocked me'); the beatdown supplied the personal motivation, not the trait. Both readings fit the corpus.
      • Post-expulsion, ruling by terror became impossible, so he rebuilt on pure leverage (confidence 0.7)
        • Evidence: y1v7.5 p.89 · y1v8 p.208 · y2v6 p.211
        • Reading: Supported by the mercenary pricing ('make it a million') and contract diplomacy replacing school-sanctioned hierarchy. Competing: he keeps the terror as brand — Ishizaki and Ibuki still fear him.
