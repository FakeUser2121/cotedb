Cruise Ship Exam

special

Rules

Second week of the first-year cruise trip: the liner becomes a twelve-sign zodiac test. Students form zodiac-named groups (rabbit, dragon, ...) each with a hidden VIP identity puzzle and a traitor embedded in the group; rules are walked through in the private post-trip briefing (

y1v4 p.34
I believe you were told punctuality was key, and yet you’re late. Hurry and take your seat,
Okay.
Year 1, Volume 4, page 34
) and the group/VIP logic is re-derived over the map at
y1v5 p.13-14
The students’ surnames were the key to figuring out the VIPs’ identities. They were assigned in the order of the animals of the zodiac.
The rabbit is fourth in the zodiac. If you put the names of the students in the rabbit group in alphabetical order, it would be Ayanokouji-kun, Ichinose-san, Ibuki-san, and then Karuizawa-san,
Year 1, Volume 5, page 13
.

Points

Group-level scores plus traitor sabotage decide outcomes; the ship results are accounted together with the island week (

y1v4.5 p.69
But didn’t you just say you had a packed schedule?
Where were you planning to go? I don’t mind changing my schedule to accommodate yours,
Year 1, Volume 4.5, page 69
: 'results from the island test, and the test on the ship').

Conditions

Groups must complete the week's tasks while neutralizing their traitor; Koenji unilaterally ended his group's participation (

y1v4 p.125
You’re joking, right?
Unfortunately, it’s the truth. Kouenji just ended the test for his group,
Year 1, Volume 4, page 125
).

Organizer

School-run special exam staged aboard the luxury liner during the trip.

Notes

I suppose I understand his desperation,

) rather than restating rules. Forms 'zodiac test' (y1v7, y2v9) and 'zodiac game' (y1v7) added after pack reads; 'double-question' (y1v4 chapter title) folded in here.
