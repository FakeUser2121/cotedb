What the text says

  1. Rule Complete institutional isolation: ANHS is an enclosed boarding campus. Students are prohibited from leaving the grounds or contacting outside individuals (including family and friends) without administrative permission. Y1V1 p.30–31
  2. Rule Point currency parity: 1 Private Point = 1 Japanese Yen (1 pt = ¥1). All on-campus transactions (cafeteria, convenience stores, mall, equipment, services) are conducted exclusively with points via digital mobile terminals. Y1V1 p.31–32
  3. Rule Monthly point grant: homeroom teacher Chabashira announces that 100,000 points are deposited into each student's account on the first of every month, ostensibly reflecting their merit and evaluation for passing the entrance exam. Y1V1 p.32
  4. Rule Transferability & anti-extortion: points may be freely transferred between students via terminals. Coercion, extortion, and theft of points are strictly prohibited and monitored by school authorities. Y1V1 p.32
  5. Rule Non-convertible & graduation expiration: points cannot be converted into real-world cash, and all unspent points revert to the school upon graduation. Y1V1 p.32
  6. Rule Dormitory utility policy: dormitories (single rooms, keycard access) do not charge or deduct points for water, gas, or electricity consumption. Y1V1 p.52
  7. Rule The zero-point safety valve: the school store features a dedicated lower-shelf section of **entirely free goods**: Free basic sanitary items, toilet paper, notebooks, pencils, and emergency food rations. Allocation rule: students are permitted to take up to one package per month free of charge. Institutional deduction: the school designed this infrastructure from inception anticipating that certain students would exhaust or lose all their private points, ensuring basic survival without cash or points. Y1V1 p.106–109
  8. Rule The S-System evaluation deduction: the monthly deposit is directly tied to the collective evaluation of the class. On May 1st, Class D receives **0 Private Points** due to April disciplinary infractions: **98 absences and late arrivals** **391 incidences of talking or mobile phone usage in class** Total penalty completely wipes out the baseline 100,000 point monthly allowance. Y1V1 p.120–122
  9. Rule Initial grade-wide Class Point standings (May 1st, 2015): **Class A:** 940 points (94,000 Private Points/student) **Class B:** 650 points (65,000 Private Points/student) **Class C:** 490 points (49,000 Private Points/student) **Class D:** 0 points (0 Private Points/student) Y1V1 p.125
  10. Rule Proprietary evaluation secrecy: the school refuses to disclose the exact formula or weighted rubric used for point deductions and rewards, comparing it to corporate employee evaluations. Y1V1 p.124–125
  11. Rule The zero-point trap (the ratchet baseline): stopping negative behavior (tardiness, talking) merely halts further deductions; it does **not** grant positive points. Once a class hits 0 points, it remains at 0 points the next month unless affirmative merit points are actively earned. Y1V1 p.125
  12. Rule Meritocratic class sorting: students are sorted upon entrance into four tiers based on overall competence. Class A represents the most capable elite; Class D is the bottom depository for failures (chabashira: "You're defective. This is just the result of you being defective."). Y1V1 p.126
  13. Rule Dynamic class promotion: class letters are not fixed. If Class D accumulates more points than Class C, Class D immediately becomes Class C and vice versa. Y1V1 p.127
  14. Rule Zero-point survival guarantee: students with 0 points do not starve; housing in dormitories and basic food rations remain guaranteed by the school. Y1V1 p.127
  15. Rule Red-mark instant expulsion: receiving a single failing score (red mark) on any midterm or final examination results in **immediate, irreversible expulsion** from the school. Y1V1 p.128–129
  16. Rule Class A monopoly on privilege: the school's renowned guarantee of **100% employment and college matriculation** applies **strictly and exclusively to graduates of Class A**. Classes B, C, and D receive no placement assistance from the school and are left entirely to their own devices. Y1V1 p.129
  17. Rule Terminal point transfer mechanism: private points can be transferred bilaterally between students directly through their school-issued smartphones by entering the recipient's phone number. Y1V1 p.138–139
  18. Rule Midterm pass threshold: the exact pass line is **32 points** — scoring at least 32 is safe, anything below is a red mark (→ immediate expulsion). Sudou asks for the exact cutoff; Ike confirms it to him. Y1V1 p.173
  19. Rule High marks feed class assessment: per Hirata's announcement, strong midterm scores should improve the class's overall evaluation, on top of preventing expulsion. Y1V1 p.152
  20. Rule Study-group scoring policy: Horikita sets the target at **50 points for everyone**, refusing to coach them to merely skate by at the 32-point threshold ("Aiming to just skate by is dangerous"). Y1V1 p.173–174
  21. Rule The dead-weight calculus: expelled failures raise the class average test scores without further effort; Horikita argues that dumping the failures leaves only the better students, making a higher class rank simpler to attain — the position she defends to Ayanokōji in their night debate and concedes is "basically correct" only after he proves her blind to others' unique strengths. Y1V1 p.178, 197–199
  22. Rule Past exam papers are openly tradeable: no school rule bars studying from old test problems; point transfers are logged but legal, and barter among students (even across years) is routine enough that a third-year negotiates without surprise. Y1V1 p.230–232
  23. Trade Ayanokōji buys a third-year Class D student's first-year midterm answer sheet (plus the post-admission mock answers as a bonus): opening offer 10,000 → seller asks 30,000 → AK shows 20,000 → settles at **15,000 points**, transferred in advance (transfer logs serve as mutual anti-fraud insurance). Y1V1 p.230–231
  24. Rule Zero-point students are the natural sellers: excluded from cafés, they eat the **free vegetable meal set**, making cash points the ideal lever (AK targets his stakeout specifically by watching who buys the free set). Y1V1 p.230
