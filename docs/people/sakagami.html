Vol. 2Ch.6 §6.1: the attack on the witnessp.199–203
13 entries · 12 quoted
  • Spoken

    "I thought I’d heard something or other about a witness, but you’re a Class D student?" p.199

  • Spoken

    "Apparently she didn’t witness anything. More of this would just be a waste of our time." p.199

  • Spoken

    "I want to speed this along. If we waste time, my students will suffer. These students are the joyful heart of their class, so I’ve no doubt that their many friends are worried about them. Also, they’re striving to improve their basketball skills, and we’re depriving them of valuable practice time. As a teacher, I can’t overlook this." p.199

  • Spoken

    "Excuse me, but may I say something?" p.202

  • Spoken

    "Normally, teachers are asked to speak as little as possible, but this situation is just too pitiful. Student council president, do you mind?" p.202

  • Spoken

    "In regards to what you’ve said, Sakura-kun, I do not necessarily doubt you. However, I have one thing to ask. You’ve stepped forward to testify as a witness, but you were rather late in doing so. May I ask why? I would think if you really did see something, you would have come forward much earlier." p.202

  • Spoken

    "Why didn’t you want to get involved?" p.202

  • Spoken

    "I see. I understand that. However, I’d like to say something else. You’re not good at talking with others, and yet when the week was nearly out you stepped forward as a witness. Doesn’t that seem rather odd? To my mind, it seems Class D secretly put a story together and are having you act as a false witness to give phony testimony." p.202

  • Spoken

    "No matter how poor your communication skills might be, I can see you haven’t testified with much confidence. Is that because you’re tormented with guilt, because you know what you’re saying is actually a lie?" p.202

  • Spoken

    "I’m not blaming you. You were probably forced to lie for your class’s sake, to save Sudou-kun. Weren’t you? If you come forward and confess honestly to us now, you won’t be punished." p.202

  • Spoken

    "I don’t think so. There are excellent students in Class D, students such as yourself, Horikita-san. By setting up a person like Sakura-san as your witness, it would establish a sense of realism that you yourself could not." p.203

  • Trait

    The cross-examination is built to make the witness doubt herself rather than to test her account; the narrator's read is that "Sakagami-sensei probably didn't actually believe this." p.202–203

  • Spoken

    "Please, let’s not force this situation to continue. If there really was evidence, you would have presented it at an earlier stage—" p.203

Vol. 2Ch.6 §6.1: the photographs, and the compromisep.204–205
11 entries · 8 quoted
  • Spoken

    "What did you use to take these photos?" p.204

  • Spoken

    "You can alter the date rather easily with a digital camera, though. If you manipulated these photos on a computer, you could effectively set them at the time and date of the incident. This are inadequate evidence." p.204

  • Caveat

    The run-together "This are inadequate evidence" is kept as printed. p.204

  • Caveat

    The page calls him "Sakagamisensei’s" once, in the run-together form: "Sakagamisensei’s expression stiffened for the first time." p.203–204

  • Spoken

    "Th-this?!" p.204

  • Spoken

    "I see. Well, you do seem to be telling the truth about witnessing the incident. That much I simply must accept. However, I can’t ascertain how the situation started from this picture. This doesn’t prove that you saw the entire incident." p.205

  • Spoken

    "So, what do you think, Chabashira-sensei? Why not look for a compromise here?" p.205

  • Spoken

    "I’m convinced that Sudou-kun lied in his testimony." p.205

  • Spoken

    "No matter how long we go back and forth, we’ll never reach an agreement. We won’t change our testimony, and your side won’t give up or admit that you conspired with the witness. In other words, you won’t stop. It’ll be an endless cycle of saying that the other side is lying. In addition, the picture is too inconclusive to be considered definitive proof. Therefore, I suggest we compromise. I do think that the students from Class C are responsible for some of the blame here. There were three students up against Sudou, and one of them has a history of fighting, which is a problem. So how about two weeks of suspension for Sudou-kun, and one week of suspension for my students? What do you think of that? The weight of the punishment is different, of course, but I think that matches the difference in sustained injuries." p.205

  • Spoken

    "Chabashira-sensei. What do you think?" p.205

  • Trait

    The proposal is a settlement that still assigns Sudō the larger share; the narrator calls it "asking for less than half" of a month and therefore "a considerable concession" for Class C. p.205
