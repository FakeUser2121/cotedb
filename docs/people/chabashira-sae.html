16 entries · 7 quoted
  • Post

    Class 1-D classroom, entrance day morning. The first bell rings. Chabashira enters briskly. Early thirties, hair pulled back into a ponytail, sharply dressed in a tailored business suit, exuding strict professionalism with zero warmth.

  • What happens

    Distributes the official student handbook and guidelines without smiling. p.29–30

  • Spoken

    “Ahem. Good morning to you, students. I’m the instructor for Class D. My name is Chiyabashira Sae. I usually teach Japanese history. However, at this school, we do not change classrooms for each grade. For the next three years, I will be acting as your homeroom teacher, so I hope to get to know all of you. It’s a pleasure to meet you.” p.30

  • Spoken

    “The entrance ceremony will be in the gymnasium one hour from now, but first, I will distribute written materials with information about this school’s special rules. I will also hand out the admissions guide.” p.30

  • Rule

    Institutional isolation: complete boarding school structure. Students are required to live in campus dormitories and are strictly prohibited from leaving campus or contacting anyone in the outside world (including family and friends) without explicit administrative approval. p.30–31

  • Rule

    S-System economy: points as universal currency. p.31–32

  • Spoken

    “I will now hand out your student ID cards. By using your card, you can access any of the facilities on campus, purchase goods from the store, and so on. It acts like a credit card. However, it is imperative that you pay attention to the points that you spend. At this school, you can use your points to buy anything. Anything located on the school premises is available for purchase.” p.30

  • Spoken

    “Your student cards can be used simply by swiping them through the machine scanner. The method is simple, so you shouldn’t get confused. Points are automatically deposited into your account on the first of every month. You should all have received 100,000 points already. Keep in mind that one point is worth one yen. No further explanation should be necessary.” p.30–31

  • What happens

    Hands out campus student ID cards that act as currency cards (S-System); students confirm their initial balance of 100,000 points. p.30

  • Spoken

    “Shocked by the amount of points you’ve been given? This school evaluates its students’ talents. Everyone here has passed the entrance examination, which itself speaks to your value and potential. The amount you’ve received reflects the evaluation of your worth. You can use your points without restraint.” p.31

  • Spoken

    “After graduation, however, all of your points return to the school. Because it’s impossible to exchange your points for cash, there’s no advantage to saving them. Once points have been deposited into your account, it’s up to you how to spend them. Do as you like.” p.31

  • Spoken

    “In the event that you don’t want to spend your points, you may transfer them to someone else. However, extorting money from your peers is not allowed. This school monitors bullying very carefully.” p.31

  • What happens

    Immediately packs up her materials, delivers a perfunctory closing, and walks out the door, abandoning the class to celebrate their windfall. p.32

  • Trait

    Calculated omission: speaks with legalistic, literal truth while deliberately concealing the hidden trap—never mentions Class Points, monthly evaluation deductions, absenteeism penalties, or the true meaning of Class D. Entices students into financial complacency. p.30–32

  • Voice

    Flat, dispassionate, strictly informative, formal school administrative tone. p.30–32

  • Spoken

    “So speaks the accused, but I wouldn’t say that you have a high level of credibility right now. If there is an eyewitness, as Sudou seems to believe, then the situation may change. If anyone here witnessed the fight, please raise your hand.” p.41

  • Scene

    "Chabashira-sensei continued talking in a robotic, flat voice. No students raised their hands in response to her question." p.41

  • Spoken

    “Too bad, Sudou. It appears there are no witnesses in this class.” p.41

  • Spoken

    “In order to check for witnesses, every teacher will inform their classes of the details of this incident.” p.41

  • Spoken

    “Anyway, that is all. We’ll make our final judgment next Tuesday, taking into account any eyewitnesses and evidence. With that, let’s end homeroom for the day.” p.41

  • Trait

    She destroys Sudou's containment plan as a side effect of doing her job neutrally: no editorialising, no protection, and the school-wide notification she volunteers is the step that turns a class matter into a cohort matter. AK: "The school probably had no say in the matter." p.40–41

  • Rule

    The disciplinary process stated on the page: accusation and defence both recorded, decision held pending eyewitness or evidence, judgment date fixed (next Tuesday), and every class informed so witnesses can be found outside Class D. p.41

  • Scene

    She leaves and "Sudou followed right after her" — AK's read: he stayed out of the room rather than lose his temper at his classmates. p.41

  • Scene

  • Spoken

    "A witness? For Sudou’s case?" p.163

  • Spoken

    "I understand what you’re said. However, I can’t simply accept what you’re telling me," p.163

    "I’m speaking to Sakura right now." p.164

    Her interrogation of a frightened witness reduces the girl physically: "Sakura hunched and shrank away, much like a frog cornered by a snake." The narrator adds that she "should have understood what kind of girl Sakura was" and that speaking up at all "had made great progress" — the method is exact and costly. p.164

    "I see. So, then you mustered up the courage to come forward?" p.164

  • Spoken

    "I see. Well if you are a witness as you say, naturally I’m obligated to relay that information to the school. However, while the school will listen to the whole story, Sudou may not be declared innocent." p.164

    "Are you really the witness, Sakura? That’s what I’m getting at. I think that it may be a lie made up by Class D because the students are afraid of receiving a negative evaluation." p.164

Considering that the witness is from Class D, it’s doubly suspicious. Any reasonable person would have doubts. Don’t you think so? Conveniently, a student from the same class happened to be in an infrequently visited building and happened to witness the entire event?" p.164

    "However, a witness is a witness. I cannot determine if she’s lying, so for the time being, I will accept her testimony. So, Sakura, I will ask for you to join us on the day of deliberation. I understand that you do not like to associate with others, but can you do this?" p.165
