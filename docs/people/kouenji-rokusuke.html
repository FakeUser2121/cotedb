Vol. 1Bus priority seat debate: absolute egoism
Vol. 1 p.20–22
That’s a really crazy question, lady,
Why should I offer up my seat? There’s no reason for me to do so.
Year 1, Volume 1, page 20
9 entries · 3 quoted
  • Scene

    Crowded bus on entrance morning. Kōenji sits with his legs crossed in the designated priority seating. Well-built athletic frame, blond hair, dressed in the ANHS uniform.

  • What happens

    Ignores the wobbling elderly woman standing near him. When Kushida approaches and politely asks him to give up his seat, he smirks and rebuffs her without shifting an inch. p.20

  • Spoken

    “Why should I offer up my seat? There’s no reason for me to do so.” p.20

  • Spoken

    “I don’t understand. Priority seats are just that: priority seats. I have no legal obligation to move. Since I’m currently occupying this seat, I should be the one who determines whether or not I move. Am I supposed to give up my seat just because I’m young? Ha! That reasoning is nonsense.” p.20

  • What happens

    Flashes a pointlessly vigorous smile, slides his earphones in, and begins listening to cacophonous music; his smug, self-important attitude remains fixed as the office lady clenches her teeth. p.21

  • Spoken

    “A contribution to society, you say? Well, that is a rather interesting opinion. It’s certainly true that offering up one’s seat to the elderly could be viewed in such a positive light. Unfortunately, I have no interest in contributing to society. I care only for my own satisfaction. Oh, and one more thing. You’re asking me, the one in the priority seat, to give up his spot, but couldn’t you simply ask one of the other people seated on this crowded bus? If you truly cared for the elderly, then something like priority seating would be a rather trivial concern, wouldn’t you agree?” p.21

  • Trait

    Flawless aristocratic arrogance, unshakeable rationalized selfishness, completely immune to public shaming, peer pressure, or conventional morality; sharp philosophical debater who exposes the hypocrisy of the crowd. p.20–22

  • Voice

    Theatrical, high-handed, grandiloquent aristocratic rhetoric. p.21

  • Relationship

    Complete dismissal of her moral appeal. — Kushida Kikyō p.20–22

Vol. 1Pool class: the 23.22-second swim feat
Vol. 1 p.86–88
He’s surprisingly fast,
Sensei, what was his time?
Year 1, Volume 1, page 86
9 entries · 1 quoted
  • Scene

    Campus indoor heated swimming pool. Co-ed physical education class.

  • What happens

    Walks to the starting block wearing tight black speedo briefs, completely unconcerned as classmates stare and girls look away. Takes an athlete's flawless starting stance on the block. p.86–87

  • Feat

    Physical conditioning: possesses an extraordinarily sculpted, muscular physique clearly superior to varsity athlete Sudō Ken. p.87

  • Feat

    Dives into the pool with textbook perfection; swims with explosive speed and aggressive form. p.87

  • Feat

    Clocked at **23.22 seconds** in the 50m sprint. The PE instructor looks at his stopwatch twice in stunned disbelief. p.87

  • What happens

    Climbs out of the pool completely unbothered, showing zero shortness of breath as if he hadn't exerted himself at all. Smirks and sweeps his wet hair back. p.87

  • Spoken

    “My abdominal muscles, back muscles, and psoas major muscle seem to be in good shape, as usual. Not a bad performance.” p.87

  • What happens

    Races Sudō in the final heat; crushes Sudō by a massive 5-meter lead with effortless ease. p.88

  • Trait

    Elite athletic capability coupled with clinical anatomical self-awareness; views peak physical performance as natural entitlement. p.87

Vol. 1Deducing zero points & supreme corporate indifference
Vol. 1 p.121
Dumb? What?
Sit down, Hondou. I’ll explain once more,
Year 1, Volume 1, page 121
, 130
10 entries · 3 quoted
  • Scene

    Classroom 1-D, May 1st morning homeroom.

  • What happens

    Props his feet up on his desk, laughs boisterously, and points smugly toward Hondou and Chabashira. p.121

  • Spoken

    “Ha ha ha! I see. So, it’s like that then, teacher? I think I’ve solved the mystery… It’s simple. We’re in Class D, so we didn’t receive a single point.” p.121

  • What happens

    Chuckles boldly and counters Hondou’s claim about 100,000 points every month. p.121

  • Spoken

    “I don’t remember hearing that, though. Do you?” p.121

  • Feat

    Instant analytical deduction: decodes the S-System's harsh reality and deduces Class D's zero-point allocation before anyone else in the room. p.121

  • What happens

    Scoffs in amusement when Yukimura panics over losing guaranteed university and career placement. p.130

  • Spoken

    “Besides, I don’t care in the slightest if the school does or doesn’t assist me to higher education or the workforce. It’s been decided that I will lead the Kouenji conglomerate group. Whether I’m in Class D or Class A is a trivial matter.” p.130

  • Backstory

    Absolute socioeconomic safety: sole heir to the Kouenji Conglomerate Group; his career and life trajectory are completely pre-determined regardless of graduation status. p.130

  • Trait

    Razor-sharp intellect masked behind flamboyant vanity; impervious to institutional pressure because he operates outside ordinary societal incentives. p.121, 130
