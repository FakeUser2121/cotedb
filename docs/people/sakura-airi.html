Vol. 1Campus store debut: the broken camerap.110–111
5 entries
  • Scene

    Campus convenience/electronics store, late April.

  • What happens

    A quiet, bespectacled girl standing alone by the digital camera display rack. Wears glasses with large frames, long hair draped forward to obscure her facial features, wearing the ANHS uniform. p.110–111

  • What happens

    Holds a compact digital camera with a damaged/broken casing in both hands, staring at the replacement models with intense anxiety. p.110–111

  • What happens

    When Ayanokōji steps into the aisle nearby, she flinches in alarm, avoids eye contact completely, clutches her broken camera tightly to her chest, and hurriedly flees down the aisle. p.111

  • Trait

    Pathologically shy, hyper-sensitive to the presence of others, deeply attached to digital photography. p.110–111

Vol. 2Ch.1 "Sakura Airi's Soliloquy": the volume opens in her POVp.10
9 entries · 5 quoted
  • Backstory

    Whole first-person monologue chapter — the only non-Ayanokōji POV chapter opening in Vol. 2; foreshadows her role as "An Unexpected Witness" (Ch.4). p.10

  • Inner voice

    "I don't know how to interact with other people. I'm bad at talking while making eye contact. I'm horrible with crowds. I don't remember how or when I became so hopeless." p.10

  • Inner voice

    "However, one thing I do know for certain is that a person cannot live entirely in isolation. No matter how much I may love my solitude, I can't remain totally by myself. So, I came up with a solution. I would adopt a false face and hide my true self. Then, I wouldn't be completely honest, but I could be a version of myself. I could continue to live in this dark, lonely world." p.10

  • Trait

    Self-aware about her condition; frames the false face as a deliberate "solution," not an accident — same masking logic she later applies to photography. p.10

  • Inner voice

    "Is everyone else putting on a façade, just like me? Or do most people show their true selves to the outside world? Since I can't connect with people, I suppose there's no way for me to find out the answer. Therefore, I remain alone." p.10

  • Inner voice

    "I'm all right by myself. I'm all right with being alone." p.10

  • Inner voice

    "I… I want to connect with someone from the bottom of my heart." p.10

  • Voice

    Her interior voice: short declarative confessions, self-questioning, closing cadence "And so I will continue to live quietly, with my eyes downcast. Alone." p.10

  • Trait

    Paradox locked in at volume start: wants a heart-to-heart connection while believing connection is impossible for her — the engine of her Vol. 2 arc. p.10

  • Inner voice

  • Feat

  • Inner voice

    Panic freeze: "After taking the pictures, I asked myself what I was doing. I couldn’t think clearly in my panicked state... My legs didn’t obey my command to move, like I was paralyzed." p.12

    Sudou, sensing movement, calls "Is someone there?" toward her hiding spot; she escapes by seconds: "I’d successfully escaped by a hair’s breadth. If I’d lagged a mere two seconds, he probably would’ve seen me." p.13

  • Trait

    Document-first, confront-never instinct: she photographs the fight but flees rather than intervene or report; self-protection through invisibility. p.12–13

  • Relationship

Vol. 2Ch.3: the witness sits in the room and says nothingp.40–65
7 entries
  • Scene

    Chabashira puts the ask to the classroom: "If anyone here witnessed the fight, please raise your hand." — "No students raised their hands in response to her question." p.41

  • Caveat

    This is inference, stated as inference: Sakura was in that homeroom (pp.40–43 is Class D's classroom, and she is a Class D student), but Chapter 3 never singles her out, never names her, and never says whether she looked up. All this page can assert is that no hand went up. p.41

  • Scene

    Kushida re-asks the same question in a form a witness could answer privately — "If anyone knows someone who saw what happened, whether it’s someone in class, a friend, or an upperclassman, please tell me. You can contact me at any time." — the one line in the chapter that would have been safe for her to answer, and she does not. p.42

  • Position

    She holds the only neutral visual record of pp.12–13 — silent-camera photographs she has not shown anyone — and Chapter 3 closes with it still hidden. p.40–65

  • Limit

    The window that her silence consumes is fixed and public: "We’ll make our final judgment next Tuesday, taking into account any eyewitnesses and evidence," and "In order to check for witnesses, every teacher will inform their classes of the details of this incident." The school is now actively canvassing every class in the year for her. p.41

  • Caveat

    AK, in a classroom with "about one third of our class remained in the room": "Suddenly, I felt a sensation, like someone was watching me. I turned around." The text never says who watched him and never resolves it inside the chapter. Do not read it as Sakura — it is an unanswered beat, not a link. p.62

  • Trait

    Her Chapter 1 creed ("And so I will continue to live quietly, with my eyes downcast. Alone.") is what the whole chapter quietly costs Sudou: invisibility works, and it works on the class's behalf too. p.40–65

  • Spoken

    Ike, asked to describe her, offers one fact: "Would it help if I said she has the biggest boobs in class? Her boobs are insanely huge." — and Yamauchi converts it into a stereotype on the spot: "Oh, she’s that plain girl with glasses, right?" p.79–80

  • Scene

    Kushida defends her, in the only defence she gets in the chapter: "It’s not good to remember people based on something like that, Ikekun! That’s pathetic." p.80

  • Position

    Her phone goes unanswered when Kushida calls: "She listened to her phone ring for about 20 seconds, but then she shook her head and hung up." — AK's reading: "Sakura might just be pretending to be out, too." p.80

  • Spoken

    Kushida's account of what Sakura actually gave her: "She gave me her contact information, but she doesn’t know me very well. She might be confused if I tried to call her." p.81

  • Feat

    Her invisibility is total enough to make the room's memory unreliable: Sudou misnames her on the spot — "this Sakura, or Kokura, or whoever" — and Yamauchi disowns having ever confessed to her ("It wasn’t Sakura; it was a girl from the next class over. Not a gloomy and ugly girl like Sakura."), then drops the subject mid-sentence by claiming a message. p.78, p.82

  • Trait

    Even the class's most charitable description is an apology for her: "It’s certainly true that Sakura was plain, but she wasn’t ugly. I’d never gotten a good look at her face before, but she seemed to have very nice features. Even so, I couldn’t say that with complete confidence, probably because Sakura had so little presence." p.82

  • Scene

    The plan the chapter ends on is to let one girl approach her alone: "Well, I’ll try to talk to her myself tomorrow. I think she might be on her guard if a lot of people approach her," Kushida said — and AK's gloss: "If Kushida couldn’t manage it, then I doubted anyone else would be able to get through to Sakura." p.82

  • Relationship

    The only Class D student who ever looked at her closely is the one who does not like being looked at; Horikita's read of Sakura's downcast eyes is the chapter's hinge and Sakura never learns it happened. — Horikita Suzune p.77

  • Relationship

    Kushida holds her contact number, her seating position and the class's only defence of her, and still cannot reach her by phone. — Kushida Kikyō p.80–82

  • Caveat

    All of this is Class D talking about her in a room she is not in. Chapter 4 gives her no line of her own until her own section later in the chapter; nothing here is her choice. p.77–82
