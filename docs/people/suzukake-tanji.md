#### p.105–109 — Suzukake's fear-based education wins the exam

Conducts the exam for the children he raised; his group shows no reaction even to wrong answers.

- **Spoken** (p.105) — "I made it my first task to instill children with a mature mind. Even if they can't solve the problems, I've made sure they approach them calmly and objectively. Those who can't do this are punished without mercy."
- **Spoken** (p.105) — "No, from newborns. And I don't want you to call it corporal punishment, Souya. This is my education."
- **Spoken** (p.106) — "Education—a process where they are punished in various ways, causing the children to become fearful. Physical pain, mental anguish, and anything effective works. If you push fear far enough, eventually it will disappear."
- **Spoken** (p.107) — "My education goes far beyond the ordinary person's learning abilities."
- **Spoken** (p.108) — "I completely eliminated them. I believed that by prohibiting communication skills to develop through dialogue, it would be possible to raise the floor of human potential."
- **Spoken** (p.108) — "I believe it's possible to create the strongest humans this way."
- **Trait** (p.105–109) — Ruthless and fearless in research — punishes from newborns, eliminates emotions entirely, and aims to "raise the floor of human potential" to create the strongest humans.
- **Feat** (p.104–108) — His three-year-old children outperform the other groups — calm under wrong answers and flashy music, adept at piano, concentration "close to that of adults."
- **Relationship · Ayanokōji Kiyotaka** (p.108–109) — Is appointed leader for the second generation; Atsuomi values his willingness to go "all the way."
- **Relationship · Sakayanagi Narimori** (p.106–109) — His methods draw Sakayanagi's disgust; Atsuomi tells him not to interfere with an outsider's opinion.
- **Relationship · Sōya** (p.105–106) — Clashes with him over the "corporal punishment" charge.

#### p.242–246 — Suzukake's recorded reflections (March 11th)

Records a video on his phone, March 11th, on the day the White Room is to be halted.

- **Narration** (p.242) — "TODAY IS MARCH 11th. Recorded by Suzukake Tanji."
- **Narration** (p.242) — "For a long time, I've been in charge of the education in this White Room."
- **Narration** (p.242) — "However, the White Room will be halted for a while starting today."
- **Narration** (p.243) — "That child's… Ayanokouji Kiyotaka's presence is significant."
- **Narration** (p.243) — "Truly a mutation."
- **Narration** (p.245) — "That child currently shows no upper limit to his talent."
- **Narration** (p.246) — "I wonder if that child I created… will grasp happiness at his dying moment…"
- **Detail** (p.242) — The White Room halted due to Naoe's maneuvering against Atsuomi; Suzukake takes the "extended vacation" positively.
- **Detail** (p.242) — He introduced a communication curriculum from the fifth generation onward after realizing children seek communication even in a closed environment — it generated emotions but lowered the ability-improvement rate.
- **Numbers** (p.242–243) — Curriculum levels: fourth applied to fifth generation, fifth to sixth, sixth to seventh (all dropped out), then a mix of fourth/fifth from the eighth. By the eighth year five children remained of the original seventy-four — a >93% dropout rate; first–third generation average 27%, fifth onward 30%.
- **Inference, not stated** (p.243–245) — The cup/bottle-cap metaphor: an ordinary educator's talent is a bottle cap, White Room educators a cup; children's limits scale to their educator's talent. True geniuses exceed the cup (bottle-sized talent), but people with both exceptional talent and teaching skill are virtually nonexistent; the White Room can make the top 10% but perhaps not the top 0.01%.
- **Trait** (p.243–246) — Still believes punishment should be executed and emotions kept to a minimum; regards Kiyotaka as a unique mutation, neither purely nature nor nurture; is driven crazy by not being able to prove why he survived without dropping out; doubts he is a true genius due to his lacking emotions and common knowledge; refuses to leave the final question of whether Kiyotaka will grasp happiness on record.
- **Relationship · Ayanokōji Kiyotaka** (p.243–246) — His most significant subject — a mutation with no upper limit; wonders if placing him as an instructor could raise the next generation's ceiling to the bottle's size.
