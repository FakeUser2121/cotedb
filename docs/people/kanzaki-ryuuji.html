  • "The fact remains that he hit the other students. Your opponents made a concession because of corroboration from the witness and her evidence. You should’ve accepted the compromise."p.214

6 more notes
  • Caveat He is present as Ichinose's second rather than as a party to the case.p.214
  • Caveat The narrator records his position first through disagreement: "Kanzaki in particular insisted that we’d made the wrong choice."p.214
  • Spoken "He might not just be sent back to square one. It might be worse, actually. If the school knew both sides shared the responsibility, they’d take that into account when assigning punishments. However, if Sudou’s share of the blame increases tomorrow, it’ll be bad news."p.215
  • Trait His argument is institutional, not moral: a shared-fault settlement caps the loss, while a retrial can increase Sudou's share of blame.p.215
  • Spoken "We’re in too deep now. Besides, it’s like we said before. We can’t forgive liars."p.215
  • Trait He loses the argument and then works the losing side anyway; the narrator's verdict is "These were really good people."p.215
