Year 1, Volume 1

Vol. 1Club Fair debut: absolute presencep.66–68
10 entries · 3 quoted
  • Post

    Student council — Student Council President; takes the stage at the club fair. p.66–68

  • Scene

    School gymnasium club fair. Representatives from various athletic and cultural clubs deliver lively pitches. Student council president Horikita Manabu steps onto the stage.

  • What happens

    Slender build, sharp analytical gaze behind glasses, neatly trimmed black hair. Merely by stepping up to the microphone, his sheer aura and commanding presence reduce over a hundred chattering first-year students to dead, breathless silence without him saying a single word or raising a hand. p.66–67

  • Spoken

    “I’m the student council president. My name is Horikita Manabu.” p.67

  • Spoken

    “The student council is looking to recruit potential candidates among the first-year students to replace the graduating third years. Although no special qualifications are required for candidacy, we humbly ask that those considering application not be involved in other club activities. We generally do not accept students involved elsewhere.” p.67

  • Spoken

    “Furthermore, we in the student council do not wish to appoint anyone who possesses a naive outlook. Not only would such a person not be elected, he or she would sully the sanctity of this school. It is the student council’s right and duty to enforce and amend the rules, but the school expects more than that. We gladly welcome those of you who understand this.” p.67–68

  • What happens

    Delivers his speech in a quiet, eloquent, razor-sharp monotone without pausing once. Immediately hops off the stage and walks out of the gymnasium, leaving the room paralyzed with intimidation. p.68

  • Feat

    Unmatched psychological dominance and institutional authority; commands immediate, involuntary deference from the entire freshman class. p.66–68

  • Voice

    Austere, aristocratic, intensely disciplined, zero warmth or theatricality; speaks with absolute executive authority. p.67–68

  • Rule

    Student Council membership condition: absolute commitment; members are prohibited from participating in any other school club. p.67

Vol. 1Night confrontation, martial arts brawl, & sizing up Ayanokōjip.192–196
23 entries · 12 quoted
  • Scene

    Dark pathway outside student dormitories, late night.

  • What happens

    Confronts his younger sister Suzune in the dead of night; displays chilling, emotionless detachment, treating her like an insignificant nuisance. p.192–193

  • Spoken

    “Suzune. I didn’t think you’d follow me this far.” p.192

  • Spoken

    “I heard you were placed in Class D. I suppose nothing has really changed in the last three years. You’ve always been fixated on following me, and as a result you don’t notice your own flaws. Choosing to come to this school was a mistake.” p.192

  • Spoken

    “It’s pointless. You will never reach Class A. In fact, your class will fall apart soon enough. Things at this school aren’t as simple as you think.” p.192

  • What happens

    Grabs Suzune roughly by the wrist and slams her back against the rough concrete wall when she insists she will reach Class A. p.193

  • Spoken

    “I told you, it’s pointless. You really are a disobedient little sister.” p.193

  • Spoken

    “No matter how I try to avoid you, the fact remains that you’re my little sister. If people around here learned the truth, I would be humiliated. Leave this school immediately.” p.193

  • Spoken

    “How incredibly stupid. Do you want to relive the pain of the past?” p.193

  • Spoken

    “You possess neither the abilities nor the qualities needed to reach Class A. Get that through your head.” p.193

  • What happens

    Prepares to violently slam Suzune onto the concrete ground, but his right wrist is suddenly intercepted and gripped from behind by Ayanokōji. p.193–194

  • Spoken

    “What? You… Eavesdropping is not an admirable quality.” p.193

  • What happens

    Instantly unleashes a vicious martial combination the second Ayanokōji lets go: throws a blinding backhand strike aimed directly at AK’s face, followed immediately by a sharp kick targeting an unguarded opening, and reaches out with an open hand to execute a throw. p.194

  • Feat

    Master martial artist: holds **5th dan in karate** and **4th dan in aikido**; strikes with enough lethal force and speed to knock an opponent unconscious in a single blow. p.194, 196

  • What happens

    Exhales deeply in slight confusion when Ayanokōji evades all strikes and slaps his throw hand away. p.196

  • Spoken

    “Good reflexes. I didn’t imagine you could evade all of my blows so quickly. Also, you seemed to understand quite well what I was trying to do. Have you been taught?” p.196

  • What happens

    Directly penetrates Ayanokōji's facade when AK claims he only learned piano and calligraphy. Delivers a biting philosophical diagnosis to Suzune and a parting assessment to AK. p.196

  • Spoken

    “You’re in Class D, too, aren’t you? What a unique boy, Suzune.” p.196

  • Spoken

    “Suzune, is this boy your friend? I’m honestly surprised.” p.196

  • Spoken

    “You continue to confuse independence with solitude. And you, Ayanokouji. With you around, things might get interesting.” p.196

  • Trait

    Uncompromising perfectionist; views weakness and familial dependency with icy disgust; highly perceptive judge of martial and psychological capability. p.192–196

  • Relationship

    Older brother; regards her as a humiliating, inferior shadow desperately chasing him; demands she withdraw from school. — Horikita Suzune p.192–193

  • Relationship

    Immediately recognizes an extraordinary individual capable of reacting to and neutralizing high-dan martial techniques. — Ayanokōji Kiyotaka p.193–196

Vol. 2Ch.6 §6.1: the president at the head of the tablep.193–196
6 entries · 3 quoted
  • Scene

    The student council room on the fourth floor; he sits in the back and does not look at his sister as she enters. p.193

  • Spoken

    "Because I am quite busy, there are certain agenda topics upon which I will defer. As a general rule, though, I would prefer to attend to these issues, as I’m entrusted to lead this student council." p.193

  • Caveat

    Chabashira’s probe about whether his presence is chance does not move him at all; the narrator notes his record — Class A on entry, student council secretary, president by December of his first year on an overwhelming vote. p.194

  • Spoken

    "It would seem there’s no objection, given the arguments we’ve heard thus far." p.196

  • Spoken

    "Regardless which party called the other over, the fact remains that it was a one-sided fight between Sudou and the other students. We can clearly see that from the injuries they sustained. We have no choice but to come up with a conclusion based on that." p.196

  • Trait

    He decides the case on visible injuries and closes the evidentiary question before Class D has finished speaking; the narrator describes his intervention as wanting "to draw a conclusion as soon as possible." p.196

Vol. 2Ch.6 §6.1: the retrial and the expulsion warningp.208–209
9 entries · 6 quoted
  • Spoken

    "So then…what do you mean?" p.208

  • Caveat

    It is the first time he looks directly at his sister in the hearing. p.208

  • Spoken

    "That’s enough. Continuing this discussion would just be a waste of time." p.208

  • Spoken

    "What I’ve learned today is that each side has an exact opposite claim. In that case, one side is propagating an extremely malicious falsehood." p.208

  • Spoken

    "I’ll ask you, Class C. Have you lied to us today?" p.208

  • Spoken

    "Well, what about you, Class D?" p.209

  • Spoken

    "Then we will regroup here for a retrial tomorrow at 4:00 PM. If by then it hasn’t been clearly established which party has lied, or in the event no one admits that they were at fault, we will pass judgment based on the evidence we’ve collected thus far. Of course, in that case we may have to consider the possibility of expulsion from this school. That is all." p.209

  • Rule

    The retrial is set for 4:00 PM the next day, and if neither side is proven to have lied or admits fault, judgment falls back on the collected evidence with expulsion as a possible outcome. p.208–209

  • Caveat

    The escalation is deliberate: the charge shifts from a fight to which class lied to the school. p.209

However, please keep in mind that how much we value the evidence is determined by how much we trust in its believability. No matter what you do, the evidence’s legitimacy is reduced because you are a student from Class D. No matter how detailed your account, we cannot accept it as one hundred percent true." p.211

  • Relationship

    The corridor exchange is the longest conversation they have had; he tests whether the narrator has a plan and accepts the answer that Suzune will supply it. — Ayanokōji Kiyotaka p.210–212
