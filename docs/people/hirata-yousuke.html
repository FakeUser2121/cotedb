Vol. 1Classroom Day 1: initiating class introductionsp.32–34
7 entries · 2 quoted
  • Scene

    Class 1-D classroom after Chabashira abruptly leaves. Students are in stunned disarray celebrating their 100,000-point deposits without knowing what to do next.

  • What happens

    Stands up with calm confidence, smiling warmly to draw the room's attention. p.32–33

  • Spoken

    “Everyone, can you please listen to me for a moment?” p.32

  • Spoken

    “My name’s Hirata Yousuke. Back in junior high, lots of people called me Yousuke. Feel free to use my first name! I guess my hobby is sports in general, but I especially like soccer. I’m planning on playing soccer here, too. Nice to meet you!” p.33

  • What happens

    Effortlessly breaks the ice; commands widespread admiration; girls in class immediately swoon. Proposes orderly self-introductions starting from the front row so classmates can learn each other's names before ceremony. p.33

  • Trait

    Natural leader, charismatic, genuinely kind, self-effacing, proactive; instinctively steps forward to build group cohesion. p.32–34

  • Voice

    Warm, gentle, inviting, devoid of ego or condescension. p.33

Vol. 1Standing up for the class & the podium appealp.121–125, 131–137
29 entries · 15 quoted
  • Scene

    Classroom 1-D, May 1st morning homeroom and subsequent class chaos.

  • What happens

    Takes the initiative on behalf of the bewildered class to question Chabashira regarding the missing points. p.121–122

  • Spoken

    “Sensei, may I please ask you a question? I’m afraid I still don’t understand… Can you please tell us why we didn’t receive any points? We won’t completely understand otherwise.” p.121–122

  • What happens

    Politely challenges Chabashira’s omission of point deduction rules. p.124

  • Spoken

    “Chiyabashira-sensei. I do not recall hearing you explain that to us before… Of course. There was never any talk about reducing our points. Had that been explained beforehand, I’m sure we would have avoided being late or talking during class.” p.124

  • Spoken

    “Well then, could you at least explain in detail how points are added or deducted? We can keep that in mind for future reference.” p.124

  • What happens

    Expression darkens as Chabashira reveals that merely eliminating infractions will still leave them at zero points next month. p.125

  • What happens

    When the classroom descends into panic during the break, Hirata steps in to soothe tempers. Yukimura grabs his collar in a rage; Hirata remains completely non-violent. p.131

  • Spoken

    “I understand that you’re all confused right now, but everyone needs to calm down… Even if I was, isn’t it better for us to band together so we can turn things around?… I understand. However, sitting here whining won’t help us right now.” p.131

  • What happens

    After Kushida defuses Yukimura, Hirata takes accountability gracefully. p.132

  • Spoken

    “It’s all right. I should have chosen my words a little more carefully.” p.132

  • What happens

    Takes the teacher’s podium before next period to deliver an impassioned call for class unity. p.133–134

  • Spoken

    “Attention, everyone. Before class begins, I want you to listen seriously for a moment. Especially you, Sudou-kun… We didn’t get any points this month. This is a serious problem, and one that will have an enormous impact on our daily lives moving forward. It’s impossible for us to make it to graduation with zero points, right?” p.133–134

  • Spoken

    “Of course. Therefore, we must earn points next month. To do that, we all need to cooperate with each other. So, please, take care not to be late to class or to talk during the lecture. Also, the use of cell phones during class is prohibited, of course.” p.134

  • Spoken

    “As long as we continue talking during class and being late, our points won’t increase for sure. Although we can’t go below zero points, disruption will, without a doubt, count as strikes against us.” p.134

  • What happens

    When Sudō aggressively rejects his appeal, Hirata bows his head with courteous humility rather than getting angry. p.134

  • Spoken

    “I don’t think that there’s anything particularly wrong with what you said, Sudou-kun. I apologize if I made you feel uncomfortable.” (bows his head politely toward the disgruntled Sudou) p.134

  • Spoken

    “However, Sudou-kun, it’s a fact that unless we all cooperate, we won’t get any more points.” p.134

  • What happens

    Steps down from the podium and stops directly in front of Horikita and Ayanokōji’s desks to personally recruit them into a collective study and improvement effort. p.135

  • Spoken

    “Horikita-san, Ayanokouji-kun, do you have a moment? I want to speak with you about how we can increase our points. I’d like you to join me.” p.135

  • Spoken

    “This is the first trial we’re facing together as a united Class D. So then—” p.135

  • What happens

    When Horikita rejects him outright (“I refuse. I won’t participate.”), Hirata accepts her rebuff with a self-deprecating wry smile rather than anger. p.135

  • Spoken

    “I…I see. I’m sorry. If you change your mind, I’d love for you to join us.” p.135

  • Spoken

    “What about you, Ayanokouji-kun?” p.136

  • Spoken

    “No, I’m sorry for bothering you. If you change your mind, please let me know.” p.136

  • Trait

    Quintessential servant-leader; absorbs class hostility without retaliation; persistent dedication to total class unity even when facing toxic rebuffs. p.121–137

  • Relationship

    Patiently mediates Sudō's outbursts without malice. — Sudō Ken p.134

  • Relationship

    Tries earnestly to draw her into the fold; bears her scorn with diplomatic grace. — Horikita Suzune p.135–136

  • Relationship

    Values his quiet presence; extends an open-door invitation without coercion. — Ayanokōji Kiyotaka p.135, 137

Vol. 1The midterm study group announcementp.152–156
8 entries · 3 quoted
  • Scene

    Classroom 1-D, lunchtime.

  • What happens

    Takes the floor when everyone rises for lunch and announces his own study group project, staring directly at Sudou during his grand speech. p.152

  • Spoken

    “Chiyabashira-sensei mentioned that the midterm is coming up soon. Remember that if you fail, you’ll be expelled. Therefore, I think it would be a good idea to form a study group.” p.152

  • Spoken

    “If you neglect your studies, you’ll get a failing grade and be expelled on the spot. I want to avoid that. However, studying won’t just prevent expulsion; it may also help earn points. If we receive high marks, our class’s assessment should improve as a result. I asked some of the students who scored high on the test to help prepare a study plan. So, I would like people who are anxious to come join our group. Everyone is welcome, of course.” p.152

  • Spoken

    “Starting today at five o’clock, we will plan to study in this classroom for two hours a day until the test. If you’d like to join us, please come whenever you like. Of course, I don’t mind if you need to leave partway. That’s all I have to say.” p.153

  • What happens

    Several failing students immediately get up and join; Sudou, Ike, and Yamauchi notably stay seated — the three he most wanted (especially Sudou). p.153

  • Trait

    Proactive crisis leadership paired with personal charisma; his group becomes the default destination for the class's struggling students. p.152–153

  • Relationship

    Relationship rocky since Sudou stomped all over his introduction game; targets Sudou with a direct stare during the speech, but Sudou averts his gaze in a Tch. — Sudō Ken p.152–153

11 entries · 8 quoted
  • Spoken

    "Good morning, Ayanokouji-kun." — "Hirata called to me, his expression as fresh and friendly as usual. The sweet yet subtle fragrance of flowers always emanated from him." p.83

  • Spoken

    Why he does not go to the witness himself: "Me? No. I’m going to greet her, that’s all. I’ve wanted to talk to her since she’s always by herself, but as a guy I don’t want to come off as forceful or anything. Also, if I asked Karuizawa-san to talk with her, that might cause a few problems." p.83

    "I think that for the time being, we’ll just wait for more information from Kushida-san." — the hero's plan is to let someone else do the asking. p.83

  • Spoken

    His reason for briefing AK at all: "Not for any particular reason. I guess it’s because you have a connection to Horikita-san. She doesn’t talk to anyone except for you, so I thought you’d pass it along." — AK: "There was no real reason for him to tell me any of this. I wasn’t really part of the team." p.84

  • Scene

    He invites AK into his own social life, girlfriend included: "Oh, that reminds me. If you’re up for it, we should hang out sometime soon. What do you think?" and "Are you fine with my girlfriend coming, too?" — and reads AK's refusal when AK himself cannot: "I’m sorry. Do you not really want to?" p.84

    When Sudou kicks a desk apart and walks on Kōenji, he is the only one who moves: "He was the only boy able to move in this awful situation. My heart pounded in my chest." p.92

    "All right, that’s enough. Calm down, you two," — and he puts the blame on both sides in front of the class: "Sudou-kun. You certainly are part of the problem here. But Kouenji-kun, you’re in the wrong, too." p.92

    "Stop it already. I don’t want to see my friends fight…" is Kushida's; his is the follow-up that picks a side for the record: "It’s just like Kushida says. I don’t know about Kouenji-kun, but I’m your ally, Sudou-kun." — AK: "Hirata was way too cool. It would’ve been fitting if he changed his name to 'Hero.'" p.93

    His last line to Sudou is the school's argument, not his own feeling: "I’m going to stop this. Sudou-kun, you should act more mature. If you cause another huge disturbance, the school’s evaluation of you will only worsen. Right?" p.93

    He never asks the witness anything, never challenges Horikita's write-off of Sudou, and stops a fight by citing the school's evaluation — leadership that avoids being the one who pressures anyone. p.83–93
