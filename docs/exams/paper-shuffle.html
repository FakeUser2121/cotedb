      Paper Shuffle

      special · generated view (source: cote_kb.db; regenerate with python3 kb/build_exam_dossiers.py)

      27 mentions · 7 vols · 1 runs · 1 claims

      Rules

      Named in the y1v6 chapter title 'The Paper Shuffle' (p.30): the first-year second-semester final pairs students so results interlock — two examinees cover eight subjects (16 scores), a 43.75 average per subject needed against a ~700-point overall line (y1v6 p.36). Later specials reuse the pairing logic: the Event Selection decides opponents 'similar to how it was during the Paper Shuffle exam' (y1v11 p.28).

      Points

      Paired finals shift class standing through the combined scores; the format is cited as last year's contrast when standardized school-designed finals return (y2v8 p.12).

      Conditions

      The midterm/final expulsion rule still applies (restated y1v6 p.30); unexcused absence scores zero on all missed tests (y1v6 p.36).

      Organizer

      School-run written final; Chabashira announces pairings and thresholds.

      Notes

      27 mentions / 7 vols (y1v6-y2v8). Named at y1v6 p.30 and remembered as the exam Horikita's strategy turned around (comeback narration y1v7 p.19-22), plus a mechanics recap at y2v1 p.42-43 when a later volume re-explains it.

      Runs

      y1v6 p.30 (run #1, conf 0.95)

      • Chronology: First-year first-semester final: pairs are fixed by a mock quiz (y1v6 p.35), then the two-day paired written exam runs under the Paper Shuffle name (chapter 3, y1v6 p.30).
      • Participants: First-year students in fixed pairs; Chabashira Sae (explains the rules); Hirata, Horikita, Yukimura and Sudou among the pairs.
      • Outcome: Eight subjects, 400 questions; a pair under sixty in any subject, or under the ~700-point overall bar (43.75 average across sixteen scores), loses both members — 'one or two pairs are usually expelled… most of them are from Class D' (y1v6 p.36). The result narrows the gap to Class C (y1v7 p.19).
