      Arc

      y1v1

      • Situation: School's most social girl systematically befriends everyone while engineering Horikita's isolation.
      • Knowledge state: Already knows Ayanokouji from junior high and is probing how much Horikita remembers.
      • Changes: Recruits Ayanokouji as a Horikita-convergence tool; drops the first genuine mask slip in private ('I really hate you').
      • Why it matters: Establishes the two-layer voice the whole series runs on: the public warmth, the private war.
      • Evidence: y1v1 p.35 · y1v1 p.91 · y1v1 p.238

      y1v2

      • Situation: Sudou trial: she plays the tireless class organizer.
      • Knowledge state: Running reconnaissance on who knows what about Sakura and the witness chain.
      • Changes: Deepens the helper persona — networks Class B, manages Sudou's image — while quietly mapping everyone's positions.
      • Why it matters: Peak visible usefulness: the mask earns her the class's trust she intends to weaponize.
      • Evidence: y1v2 p.31 · y1v2 p.36 · y1v2 p.163

      y1v5

      • Situation: Sports festival leak traced back toward her; Ayanokouji forces the private confrontation.
      • Knowledge state: Exposed enough to pivot: she shows her hand deliberately.
      • Changes: Declares the expulsion campaign against Horikita — and then against Ayanokouji too; admits the Ryuuen question-swap deal was her own gamble.
      • Why it matters: The mask comes off as a choice, not an accident: 'I will have the school expel you... deal with the devil'.
      • Evidence: y1v5 p.174 · y1v5 p.175 · y1v5 p.196

      y1v6

      • Situation: Final-exam question sabotage exposed; the bet with Horikita settles.
      • Knowledge state: Her junior-high destruction and her addiction to being valued are now spoken aloud to both witnesses.
      • Changes: Confesses the mechanism ('This is what I am'), loses the blazer-swap trap, and accepts a non-aggression pact she refuses to turn into cooperation.
      • Why it matters: The confession reframes her from schemer to addict: visibility is the drug, sabotage the supply.
      • Evidence: y1v6 p.125 · y1v6 p.197 · y1v6 p.199

      y1v8

      • Situation: Threat maintenance while Horikita's position hardens.
      • Knowledge state: Still believes exposure equals destruction — of the class as well as herself.
      • Changes: Restates the doctrine in plain terms: leak anything and she will 'destroy the entire class, just like I did back in junior high'.
      • Why it matters: Mutually assured destruction becomes her governing policy.
      • Evidence: y1v8 p.214 · y1v8 p.215

      y1v9

      • Situation: Pact period: an uneasy working channel opens.
      • Knowledge state: Accepts the frame where she neither antagonizes Ayanokouji nor gets full trust.
      • Changes: Joins the agreement with a single strict condition — don't make her his enemy — and keeps feeding useful information.
      • Why it matters: Managed coexistence begins; hostility goes underground but never ends.
      • Evidence: y1v9 p.195 · y1v9 p.197 · y1v9 p.200

      y2v5

      • Situation: Horikita's direct confrontation re-opens the old wound in public.
      • Knowledge state: Cornered by the recording/proof line of attack.
      • Changes: Snaps ('That's enough!!!') — the composed mask failing under sustained pressure for the first time since y1v6.
      • Why it matters: Shows the pact did not heal anything; it only stabilized the war.
      • Evidence: y2v5 p.199 · y2v5 p.201

      y2v7

      • Situation: Maid-café arc: she admits the relationship with Horikita in one honest half-sentence.
      • Knowledge state: Still embedded in Class D's social fabric, now with visible ambivalence.
      • Changes: 'Half glad we were classmates and half pissed off' — resentment and attachment finally co-exist in the same breath.
      • Why it matters: The series' clearest statement that her war with Horikita is personal, not purely instrumental.
      • Evidence: y2v7 p.158

      Psychology & competing readings

      • Her need to be valued is an addiction that explains both the sunshine persona and the sabotage (confidence 0.85)
        • Evidence: y1v1 p.185 · y1v6 p.125 · y1v6 p.126
        • Reading: The text states it outright ('I think that I'm probably addicted to that feeling'). Competing nuance: whether the addiction or the trauma came first — junior-high destruction is implied as the wound, the addiction as the scar tissue.
      • She is the series' clearest case of popularity as a pathology, not a gift (confidence 0.8)
        • Evidence: y1v6 p.126 · y1v1 p.185 · y1v1 p.101
        • Reading: No serious competing reading of the mechanism; only of culpability — addiction mitigates, the sabotage actions indict.
      • She would genuinely burn the class to protect her secret — the threat is not a bluff (confidence 0.75)
        • Evidence: y1v5 p.196 · y1v8 p.214
        • Reading: Counter-reading: she already had leverage and chose containment (the y1v9 pact) over destruction; the threats may be calibrated deterrence rather than standing intent. Her y1v6 confession to three witnesses weakens the pure-burn reading.
      • The Horikita obsession is personal (witnessed popularity vs. outsider) rather than strategic (confidence 0.7)
        • Evidence: y1v5 p.174 · y1v6 p.197 · y2v7 p.158
        • Reading: Competing: Horikita is also the one person whose knowledge threatens her — hate and threat are inseparable. The corpus supports motive entanglement, not a clean single cause.
      • Her later 'I always care about others' posture is sincere re-integration, not another mask (confidence 0.5)
        • Evidence: y2v7 p.158 · y3v1 p.25
        • Reading: Contested: y3v1 ('Ayanokouji-kun is much more important than you two think') reads protective, yet she has every reason to keep him close as a watched variable. Corpus does not settle whether the thaw is real.
