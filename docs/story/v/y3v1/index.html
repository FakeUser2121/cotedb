Coverage notes (Year 3, Vol. 1)
Ch.1: Confusion (pp. 13–28) — depth read

Setting. Class 3-A classroom, first morning of the third year, after the opening ceremony.

Beats

  • pp.13–15: Horikita notices Ayanokōji is absent; Sudō speculates he is feigning sickness over the Karuizawa breakup, speaking from his own heartbreak experience. Horikita observes Karuizawa sits listless by the window, the emotional distance between the two now obvious.
  • pp.16–18: Chabashira arrives pale and unfocused, not responding to Hirata or Kikuchi; she eventually says a student "used their Private Points to exercise a certain right."
  • pp.18–21: She reveals Ayanokōji exercised a class transfer, moving from Class 3-A to Class 3-C. Students check the OAA: his name is gone from 3-A and present in 3-C. Horikita's heartbeat races; she cannot accept it.
  • pp.21–25: Matsushita is visibly flustered. The class speculates — 20 million points, embarrassment over the breakup, betrayal, or being bought in as Sakayanagi's replacement in the fallen Class C.
  • pp.25–28: Kushida stays calm and tells Ike and Shinohara that Ayanokōji is far more important than they think, having contributed unseen, and passes the point to Horikita, who agrees.

Page-map. pp.22–23 carry no prose. Chapter 2 begins p.29.

Open threads. Why Ayanokōji transferred; who paid the 20 million points; whether Class C bought him as a replacement for Sakayanagi.

Attribution gaps. none in this chapter.

Ch.2: Confirmation (pp. 29–39) — depth read

Setting. Class 3-A classroom, the hallways, and a café at Keyaki Mall, on the last day before formal classes.

Beats

  • pp.29–30: Horikita, still in disbelief, resolves to confirm Ayanokōji's true intentions directly; Hirata, Sudō and others notice her leaving.
  • pp.30–33: She goes to the former Class A (now Class C) room; Tsukasaki says Ayanokōji left for Keyaki Mall. Matsushita catches up and insists on coming, wanting to verify the transfer herself. Horikita speculates he may have gone in alone due to some danger in Sakayanagi's former class, and wonders if she was simply unreliable to him.
  • pp.34–39: At the café they find Ayanokōji with Hashimoto, Morishita and Ichinose. Hashimoto blocks access, calling Ayanokōji their "leader candidate." Ayanokōji calmly exposes that Matsushita was a schemer who had secretly cooperated with him (including during Maezono's expulsion), shakes Horikita's confidence by noting the others were not surprised by his transfer, and refuses to explain his reasons. Horikita leaves, accepting he has become her enemy.

Open threads. Ayanokōji's real motive; his prior cooperation with Matsushita; his "leader candidate" status in Class C.

Attribution gaps. none in this chapter.

Ch.3: The Commencement of a One-Year Period (pp. 40–74) — depth read

Setting. Class 3-C classroom and homeroom; a café at Keyaki Mall; the dorm lobby and the area behind the dorm.

Beats (half 1, pp.40–58)

  • p.40: Flashback — after the opening ceremony Ayanokōji submitted his transfer notice to the chairman, with Mashima verifying the twenty million points and their source.
  • pp.40–44: Introduces himself to Class C, saying he cannot replace Sakayanagi but can help if the will to fight remains. Hashimoto applauds; most students are cold. Morishita arranges his seat in front of hers, citing unfamiliarity and the need for surveillance of a former enemy; Sugio eagerly swaps. She teases him about "making a hundred friends" and "onigiri with a hundred people."
  • pp.45–48: Hashimoto proposes a welcome party; Yoshida, Machida and others refuse, saying Ayanokōji must prove his worth first and might be a spy. Morishita tells him bluntly he is "spectacularly unpopular" — "leftover merchandise."
  • pp.49–58: Ayanokōji, Hashimoto, Morishita and Ichinose discuss the C–D alliance. Ichinose insists on maintaining cooperation until the last possible moment; she trusts Ayanokōji without a contract.

Beats (half 2, pp.59–74)

  • pp.59–65: The alliance logic is debated — doubled information, pooled Private Points, no binding contract as an advantage. Ayanokōji explains he delayed the transfer until just after the ceremony to maximise the disturbance in Class A; his stated aim is to lead Class C to Class A, and he wants Horikita to grow, trusting her classmates will help her recover.
  • pp.66–68: Hashimoto notes the backlash risk if Ayanokōji acts without class recognition; Morishita points out Hashimoto would itself have opposed the plan had he not brought Ayanokōji in.
  • pp.69–74: Hirata pulls Ayanokōji aside outside the dorm; Sudō, Ike, Keisei and Akito join. They demand a reason. Ayanokōji says he transferred to help the struggling Class C in exchange for points, "solely for my own benefit." Sudō insists he is important to the class; Hirata accepts it is his choice. They leave, still friends.

Open threads. The C–D alliance (kept secret from Class A); class recognition of Ayanokōji as leader; Hirata's unresolved distrust and his unusual use of "Kiyotaka."

Attribution gap. p.74 TL note — Hirata calls him "Kiyotaka," which the translation flags as intentional/not a translator error, reason unknown.

Ch.4: An Onlooker's Perspective (pp. 75–97) — depth read

Setting. Class 3-C classroom early morning; Keyaki Mall/gym on Saturday; a café conversation among Ichinose and Amasawa.

Beats (half 1, pp.75–89)

  • pp.75–84: Ayanokōji arrives 30 minutes early to observe Class C and finds Shiraishi already there. They talk; she presses him on why he transferred and says most of the class is skeptical of his impact. Yoshida arrives and, after Shiraishi needles him, offers to help Ayanokōji fit in. Shiraishi volunteers to introduce him to friends on a day off. She states bluntly that Yoshida likes her and that she would never choose him.
  • pp.85–87: At Keyaki Mall, Hōsen approaches Ayanokōji, asking about the transfer and hunting for a "punching bag"; he delegates class leadership to Nanase and says he only cares about money. Chabashira intervenes.
  • pp.87–89: Chabashira admits she followed him from the dorm and asks whether he transferred because of her trouble with Hoshinomiya. He says he had already planned to transfer before that issue; it is for his own sake, and he refuses to disclose the true task.

Beats (half 2, pp.90–97)

  • pp.90–93: Ichinose, parted from Ayanokōji at the gym, meets Amasawa. Amasawa probes about Ayanokōji, professes to love him, and escalates to asking whether Ichinose has slept with him — trying to see her agitation and warning her not to get too close.
  • pp.94–97: Ichinose calmly deflects with a cake metaphor and then counters by mentioning the "White Room," deducing Amasawa's origin from her reaction. Amasawa, flustered, realises she was the one teased and revises her view of Ichinose — concluding Ayanokōji changed her, but through her own strength.

Open threads. Shiraishi's promise to introduce Ayanokōji to friends; Amasawa's White Room connection; Chabashira's suspicion.

Attribution gap. none. Two TL notes (p.84 "outsiders have a better grasp"; p.97 White Room in katakana).

Ch.5: Convolution (pp. 98–145) — depth read

Setting. Class 3-A classroom; Class 3-C classroom; Keyaki Mall café; the bench outside the dorms; Horikita's room; the karaoke room.

Beats (part 1, pp.98–110). A week in, the class is adapting to Ayanokōji's absence; Horikita is not. She spirals with guilt and self-doubt, wondering if she could have kept him. Chabashira announces the "Comprehensive Academic Ability Exam: Full-Class and Small-Group Battles" against Class 3-D (Ichinose) in two weeks — 100 questions across 7 subjects; a full-class battle (2 wins) plus five small-group battles (1 win each), with purchasable penalty points (50,000 each). Horikita realizes Class A is short five students after the transfer. In Class C, Hashimoto pushes to leave the exam to Ayanokōji; Shimazaki resists; Sanada brokers a compromise — entrust it to him and judge by whether his penalty-point predictions are right.

Beats (part 2, pp.111–122). At the café, Ayanokōji, Hashimoto and Morishita strategize. Morishita questions bringing Yamamura in; Ayanokōji says he told her to keep her distance. They debate penalty-point targeting — hitting even two of the opponents' five small-group participants would prove his read. At the mall, Ishizaki angrily demands why Ayanokōji transferred; Ryūen intervenes and tells Ayanokōji he welcomes the chance to defeat him now that he leads Class C directly, then warns him Ishizaki will come around. Morishita notes Ryūen is aiming to win.

Beats (part 3, pp.126–135). Kushida, isolated since her true nature was exposed, finds Horikita waiting on a bench "by coincidence" and needles her. She admits her own position is worse — without Ayanokōji the class facade may collapse and he knows her secret — but says she must pretend to be calm. She demands Horikita deal with Ibuki and win the exam as class leader. Horikita, alone in her room, breaks down crying, replaying the transfer and wishing she had stopped him.

Beats (part 4/5, pp.136–145). Ryūen gathers Ishizaki, Albert, Katsuragi and Ibuki in the karaoke room to plan; Katsuragi notes this is the exam Class D (their rival class) would struggle with most. (Continues into Ch.6.)

Open threads. Whether Ayanokōji's penalty predictions will hold; Class C's acceptance of him as leader; Ryūen's intent to beat him; Kushida's fear of exposure; Horikita's breakdown.

Attribution gap. none. TL note on "light" (p.125) and the exam rules are printed in full on pp.100–102.

Ch.6: School Life in Class C (pp. 146–162) — depth read

Setting. In front of the dormitory on a Sunday morning; a karaoke room at Keyaki Mall; the Class 3-C classroom.

Beats

  • pp.146–148: Ayanokōji meets Yoshida, who has been waiting an hour to see Shiraishi; Ayanokōji confirms Yoshida likes her. He asks Yoshida for the class's honest evaluation of Hashimoto. Nishikawa Ryouko arrives instead of Shiraishi — she is Shiraishi's friend and teases Yoshida mercilessly.
  • pp.148–151: Nishikawa warns them about Shiraishi's "Hundred-Man Slayer" nickname and reputation; she whispers to Ayanokōji that Shiraishi might make him "the 101st," but he refuses, reasoning it would cause rumors harmful to his position in Class C.
  • pp.152–154: At karaoke, Shiraishi sits very close to Ayanokōji, touches his hand and exchanges contact details, treating him like an "interesting experimental subject" — her eyes show curiosity, not romance.
  • pp.155–162: In class, Morishita rubs eraser crumbs and throws them at Ayanokōji's head. Shiraishi tells him the timing so he catches her red-handed. Morishita justifies it as "Jeanne d'Arc" defeating evil; Shiraishi watches cheerfully.

Open threads. Shiraishi's true interest in Ayanokōji (as a specimen); Morishita's persistent teasing; Nishikawa's hostility after her offer was refused.

Attribution gap. none. TL note (p.154) about "Shiraishi"/"Nishikawa" in the raw.

Ch.7: Ayanokouji's Defeat (pp. 163–193) — depth read

Setting. The school path/bench before school; Class 3-B's classroom before the exam; Class 3-D's homeroom for results; Class 3-C's classroom after the exam.

Beats (part 1, pp.163–183)

  • pp.163–173: Karuizawa, still heartbroken, arrives early and sees Ayanokōji on a bench. Ichinose approaches and the two talk; Karuizawa asks whether she and Ayanokōji are dating. Ichinose denies it but admits her feelings are "kinda like that," says she gives without expecting return, and reveals Ayanokōji is waiting to meet her. She tells Karuizawa she cannot help her — a new, selective kindness. Karuizawa hides in the bushes, crushed.
  • pp.174–180: Class B is tense before the exam; Ryūen has not named the small-group participants. (Horikita's class discovers Kōenji was targeted via a suspected leak.)
  • pp.181–183: Horikita apologizes to her class; Hirata calms them, saying they must fight with the hand they are dealt.

Beats (part 3, pp.184–188). Results in Class D: Class D beat Class A; Class C beat Class B. Full Class A 2633 vs D 2712; Class B 2327 vs C 2880. Ayanokōji scored 100 but took 100 penalty points and lost his small-group bout to Ishizaki; Class C got 6 wins, 1 loss. Ichinose's class bought 1.25 million points in penalties. Ichinose texts Ayanokōji, who credits her courage in trusting his advice.

Beats (part 4, pp.189–193). In Class C, Shimazaki confronts Ayanokōji — he missed all five penalty predictions despite the win. Ryūen and his group barge in; Ryūen shoves Sawada and Hashimoto aside. The class's verdict hangs. Then Shimazaki reveals he has come to acknowledge Ayanokōji as leader anyway, because Ryūen's class didn't take the exam seriously (naming predictable participants), so there was nothing to "see through." Ayanokōji explains the real point: predicting participants is worthless if the opponent isn't serious.

Open threads. Ryūen's deliberate "gift" of a near-complete victory; whether Class C fully accepts Ayanokōji.

Attribution gap. none. TL note (p.190) on "shoot him."

Ch.8: Enemies and Allies (pp. 203–224) — depth read

Setting. Class 3-A's empty classroom at dusk; the gravel path outside the dorm; a bench; a spot by the railing at dusk.

Beats (pp.203–215). Horikita sits alone in the empty classroom, blaming herself for Class A's loss and feeling she has become useless. Outside, Ibuki suddenly kicks her in the back from behind — calling her pathetic because Ayanokōji's absence has made her so — and walks off. Karuizawa, who saw it from a bench, helps her up and cleans her uniform. The two talk: Horikita admits she was only strong because Ayanokōji supported her; Karuizawa reveals she still loves him and tells Horikita the secret of her bullying past and how he saved her from darkness. They realize they both fell for the same difficult person, laugh, and resolve to make him regret leaving — Horikita finally takes a step forward.

Beats (pp.216–224). After a welcome party for Ayanokōji in Class C, he meets Kushida by the railing. He proposes she act as an insider — feeding him information about Class A in exchange for Private Points — and threatens to expose her true nature (which other classes don't fully know). Kushida refuses for now but does not outright reject. He explains how he and Ichinose coordinated (her gathering intel on who Hirata called and Kōenji's weakness) to target the right students. He promises to prepare Private Points for her later.

Open threads. Kushida's answer to the betrayal offer; Class A's recovery under Hirata; Horikita's resolve to make Ayanokōji "turn his head."

Attribution gap. none.

Epilogue: What Awaits Ahead (pp. 225–247) — depth read

Setting. The school hallway and route to the dorm; behind the dorm (garbage disposal area); the student council room area; the Author's Afterword (pp.248+).

Beats (pp.225–234)

  • pp.225–228: On the way back from school, Ayanokōji encounters Utomiya and Tsubaki. Tsubaki's question about whom he'd like to meet after graduation ("someone other than family") triggers a memory of a White Room girl named Yuki — prompted by the wordplay on "Yuki-Tsubaki"/snow. He decides not to seek her out.
  • pp.229–234: Kitou Hayato calls him behind the dorm and declares he does not recognize him as leader. Kitou explains he followed Sakayanagi only because she had the best chance of leading Class A, and now challenges Ayanokōji to prove his strength. Ayanokōji also rebukes him for not protecting Sawada when Ryūen barged in. In a brief fight Ayanokōji easily outclasses Kitou (palm to the abdomen, ear-slap, kick) but leaves him his dignity; Kitou agrees to follow if Ayanokōji leads Class C toward Class A.

Beats (pp.237–247)

  • Morishita drags Hashimoto (and an uninvited Shiraishi) to check on Horikita at the student council room. They speculate Ayanokōji is a ruthless "ally of convenience" who uses everyone; Morishita warns Shiraishi she will get burned, but Shiraishi says that is why it is fine.
  • They eavesdrop on Horikita and Nanase; Nanase asks about Ayanokōji. After Horikita leaves, Nanase takes a call from "Tsukishiro-san," mentions monitoring Ayanokōji for another year, Ishigami Kyou, and a noteworthy first-year — then reveals she has two phones (against school rules). She detects the three eavesdroppers and names them.

Open threads. Tsukishiro's continued plot; Nanase's role as monitor and her second phone; the first-year she mentions; the White Room girl "Yuki"; Kitou's conditional loyalty.

Attribution gap. none. TL notes on "Yuki-Tsubaki"/snow (p.228), the proverb (p.247), and "koi" wordplay (p.247).
