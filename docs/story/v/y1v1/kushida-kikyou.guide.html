Kushida Kikyō

Kushida Kikyō is the social idol of Class 1-D in Classroom of the Elite — the sunshine classmate who befriends everyone, de-escalates every fight, and lends points without hesitation. Beneath that persona, revealed only on a locked rooftop, lies a venomous hatred of Horikita Suzune and an absolute compulsion to be liked by every person she meets, enforced if necessary by fabricated assault evidence. Year 1 sets her as Ayanokōji Kiyotaka's uneasy secret-keeper and a full partner in the Class A project — smiling the entire time.

Spoiler scope: this page covers Year 1, Volume 1 only — nothing from later volumes.

Personality & voice

Kushida's default register is warm, earnest, and pitched to disarm: "It's okay to go slowly. Don't rush" to a panicking classmate (p.34), instant handshakes, remembered self-introductions, and a standing promise to "invite you again" even after being told not to (p.89). She weaponizes the same skill set for intelligence work — her Horikita probes are always framed as friendly worry (p.56–57), and her gift for reading rooms lets her de-escalate a collar-grab in seconds with both hands around the aggressor's fist (p.131–132).

Beneath it runs two hard drives:

1. The compulsion. One holdout invalidates the whole project: "if I can't become friends with one girl in my class, then that means I'll never achieve my goal…" (p.101). Her self-worth is a percentage, and Horikita is the missing point. 2. The mask. On the locked rooftop she drops to a harsh, guttural voice — "It'd be better if she just died…" (p.182) — and when caught, escalates instantly to a manufactured sexual-assault frame: fingerprints pressed onto fabric, an unwashed uniform held for the police (p.183–185). "I absolutely despise gloomy, ordinary guys like you" is delivered with the same smile that later says "I really hate you" (p.238).

Her self-diagnosis is the most honest line she speaks: "That's what I want out of life, though. That way, my existence has meaning" (p.185).

Story summary — Year 1, Volume 1

The idol installs (Ch. 1–4). Bus confrontation with Kōenji establishes the public Kushida (under the never-named uniformed girl identification carried as an inference note); Day 1 she rescues Inogashira's intro and immediately crowns herself class friend-collector (p.34–35). On Day 2 she intercepts Ayanokōji — the boy who gave the lamest self-introduction — and runs friendly reconnaissance on Horikita, who refused her contact-info request (p.56–57). At the pool she's the first to see through Ayanokōji's build (p.88).

The café trap and the compulsion (Ch. 5). She engineers the Palate ambush with Ayanokōji; Horikita dismantles it in seconds and exits on the nine-years-alone bombshell. Kushida collapses into her seat, snaps back to a beaming smile in a split second, and admits the project's true stakes: a single unbeaten girl in the class would mean her entire goal of universal friendship has failed (p.99–101).

The campaigner (Ch. 6–7). Group outing glue work, gentle fact-checking of Yamauchi's "APEC" boast (p.118), fearless mediation of Yukimura's attack on Hirata (p.131–132), and instant 2,000-point charity to Karuizawa (p.138). Chiyabashira's "defective" verdict moves her to genuine tears-adjacent advocacy: "It's cruel to expel someone for getting a bad grade" (p.163). She recruits the failing trio for the study group, secures Ayanokōji's number, asks if he and Horikita are dating, and insists on joining the group herself — vetoed by Horikita (p.165–168).

The rooftop (Ch. 8). Alone on the locked rooftop she vents pure homicidal loathing of Horikita; Ayanokōji's kicked door catches her mid-scream. The mask that follows pins him to a wall and constructs the blackmail frame on the spot — "I would tell everyone that you raped me" … "Your fingerprints are on my clothes. That's evidence of my claim" (p.183) — before extracting his sworn oath, snapping back to warmth, and filing him as the only boy Horikita opens up to (p.185–186). The volume's central secret triangle is born.

The partner (Ch. 9–12). Horikita's lunch invitation converts her to the Class A project — "I want to work on getting into Class A with you" (p.205) — and the three-way handshake seals it (p.206). She stress-tests the alliance over lunch ("If Horikita and I happen to clash…," p.216), relays the girls' hot-guy rankings as social currency (p.215), and takes the podium the day before the exam to distribute the old-test sheets under the third-year cover story (p.236). Horikita forces the rooftop question into daylight: "Ha ha, you got me… That's right. I really hate you" (p.238) — accepted as working terms. On results day she shares a relief glance with Ayanokōji (p.246), and at the victory party she reveals the Class A project to the trio, rebuilding every hope Horikita knocks down, and closes the recruits with "a strange bond of mutual trust forms between people who share a secret" (p.235, applied to Ayanokōji earlier — her standing framing of the group).

Trivia

  • The uniformed bus champion who confronts Kōenji is never named in Volume 1; her identification as Kushida is an inference, resting on her later knowledge of the gate conversation (p.57).
  • The rooftop blackmailer and the podium savior are the same afternoon's character: she distributes the old-test salvation two days after engineering the assault frame.
  • Claims junior-high swimming weakness, then posts the second-fastest time in her heat (~31s, p.79) — the same sandbagging instinct Ayanokōji uses, observed on her.
  • Her smile survives every crisis in the volume: rejection, blackmail collapse, and a confessed hatred, all reset within a second.
