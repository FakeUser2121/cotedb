Chapter 1: The Structure of Japanese Society p.14–15

Setting. In-story year 2015. Pre-school philosophical framing / direct reader address.

Narrative beats

  • Ayanokōji poses humanity's fundamental dilemma: "Are people equal or not?"
  • Rebuts modern social doctrines of surface equality: abilities inherently differ, meaning roles must differ.
  • References Fukuzawa Yukichi's Gakumon no Susume: heaven sets no man above another at birth, yet education immediately creates an inevitable hierarchy of status and position.
  • Core mission established: equality is a fiction, but humanity cannot simply surrender to cruel inequality based on instinct. Ayanokōji seeks a new answer to this eternal question.
  • Reflects on completing compulsory education, entering high school, and having felt only relief at being free of obligation without considering the future.
Chapter 2: Welcome to the School Life of Your Dreams p.16–53

Setting. Present day late May (classroom coercion framing) → Flashback to April entrance day (bus, classroom 1-D, convenience store, dorm room 401).

Narrative beats

  • Present Day Coercion (pp. 16–18): Horikita wakes Ayanokōji with a 3-second countdown threat, demanding his assistance after class. Denies stabbing him with a compass earlier on strict lack-of-evidence grounds.
  • The Bus Conflict (pp. 19–25): On the entrance morning bus, Kushida Kikyō challenges Kōenji Rokusuke to give up his priority seat for a standing elderly woman. Kōenji delivers a solipsistic aristocratic refusal (caring only for his own satisfaction). Kushida boldly appeals to the entire bus, compelling a working woman to yield. Ayanokōji and Horikita observe in stone-faced silence, mutually agreeing that yielding is pointless.
  • Gate Confrontation (p. 25): Horikita intercepts Ayanokōji at the natural rock school gate;
    establishes that her actions stem from rational conviction rather than mere trouble-avoidance;
    declares she does not want to spend time around him.
  • Classroom 1-D &
    Seating (pp. 26–29): Ayanokōji is seated in the rear window seat;
    Horikita sits to his left. After he opens with small talk about friends, she refuses even the name exchange ("Do you mind if I refuse?", "I disagree."), then sighs and introduces herself solely to repay the social debt — and instantly verdicts his self-introduction ("Spoken just like someone who avoids trouble. I don’t think I could ever like such a person").
  • Homeroom Orientation &
    Point Trap (pp. 29–32): Homeroom teacher Chabashira Sae introduces herself, outlines complete campus isolation, and announces the S-System: digital terminals issued with an initial grant of 100,000 Private Points (¥100,000) per student, promising equal monthly deposits while deliberately concealing behavioral evaluation and Class Point mechanics.
  • Self-Introductions (pp. 32–40): Hirata takes proactive leadership to guide introductions. Kushida gently calms a paralyzed Inogashira Kokoro, then announces her goal to befriend everyone. Sudō aggressively refuses to introduce himself and walks out. Ike introduces himself hunting for a girlfriend. Kōenji puts his legs on his desk and warns he will punish "ugly things."
    Ayanokōji completely freezes and bombs his introduction in dead silence. Horikita walks out alone.
  • Convenience Store Altercation (pp. 41–51): Two second-year upperclassmen bump into Sudō, knock his ramen, and erupt in mocking laughter when they learn he is in Class D, calling him "defective"
    and warning he will soon be in hell. Sudō storms off leaving his mess. Ayanokōji notices two security cameras above the entrance and cleans up the mess to avoid trouble. Horikita scolds him for being a pushover.
  • Dormitory Freedom (pp. 52–53): Ayanokōji receives Room 401. Celebrates absolute freedom from the outside world: family and past acquaintances cannot contact him. Resolves to begin anew and enjoy high school anonymously.

Narrative beats

Ayanokōji solves them with ease while targeting an average score.
  • Free Supplies &

  • Keyaki Mall Outing &
    The Spending Abyss (pp. 112–119): Kushida takes Ayanokōji to Keyaki Mall with Hirata, Karuizawa, Ike, and Yamauchi. Karuizawa buys 30,000 points of designer clothes while Hirata carries her bags. Yamauchi claims he scored 900 on the "APEC"
    before Kushida gently corrects him that he means the TOEIC. The chapter ends on the last day of April with Class D completely unprepared for what comes next.
Chapter 7: Classroom of the Elite p.120–151

Setting. Classroom 1-D, faculty guidance office, school stairwell. May 1st.

Narrative beats

  • The May 1st Point Revelation (pp. 120–127): On May 1st, Class D receives 0 Private Points. When Hondou and the class express confusion, Kōenji arrogantly deduces the truth: Class D received nothing. Chabashira confirms his deduction and drops the hammer: Class D accumulated 98 absences/lates and 391 talking/cellphone infractions during April, wiping out their entire 100,000-point grant. She unfurls the grade-wide standings: Class A (940), Class B (650), Class C (490), Class D (0). She formally introduces the S-System: students are sorted by competence, and Class D is "akin to the last bastion for failures… You’re defective. This is just the result of you being defective."
    She claps mockingly at their ruinous month of indulgence and warns that stopping infractions will not increase points—next month will still be zero unless they actively earn positive points.
  • The Red-Mark Expulsion Rule &
    Class A Monopoly (pp. 127–130): Chabashira posts the results of the April diagnostic short test. Seven students failed (Sudō worst with 14, Ike with 24;
    class average 65). She reveals the school's absolute ironclad rule: a single failing score (red mark) on midterms or finals results in immediate expulsion. Furthermore, she shatters their illusions by revealing that the school's advertised 100% employment and college matriculation rate applies strictly and exclusively to Class A graduates;
    Classes B, C, and D receive zero institutional backing. Kōenji scoffs at the panic, noting his future as head of the Kōenji Conglomerate is already assured regardless of class ranking.
  • Classroom Chaos &
    Failed Leadership (pp. 131–139): Class 1-D descends into panic. An enraged Yukimura grabs Hirata by the collar, but Kushida physically steps between them, envelops Yukimura's fist, and restores calm. Hirata takes the teacher's podium to appeal for class unity, no tardiness, and no talking;
    Sudō reacts aggressively, refusing to be ordered around, and storms out of the room. Hirata approaches Horikita and Ayanokōji to invite them into a study and strategy circle, but Horikita rejects him without a flicker of interest ("I refuse. I won’t participate."). During lunch break, Yamauchi desperately tries to pawn his game console to Ayanokōji for 20,000 points (AK offers 1,000), while Karuizawa casually hits up Kushida for 2,000 points, which Kushida gives without hesitation.
  • The Faculty Guidance Trap &
    Entrance Exam Secret (pp. 139–148): Outside the faculty office, Class 1-B homeroom teacher Hoshinomiya Chie flirtatiously teases Ayanokōji until Chabashira smacks her with a clipboard. Chabashira ushers Ayanokōji into the guidance office kitchenette, ordering him behind closed doors under threat of expulsion, then admits Horikita. Horikita fiercely protests her placement in Class D, citing her near-perfect entrance exam (3rd highest in the entire first-year grade) and high interview evaluation. Chabashira dismantles her academic vanity, pointing out that academic merit alone does not define superiority. Chabashira then opens the kitchenette door, reveals Ayanokōji, and produces his entrance examination sheets: exactly 50 points out of 100 across all five subjects (Japanese, Math, English, Science, Social Studies), plus 50 on the short test. Chabashira reveals AK solved a 3%-success-rate math problem using a complex formula while botching a 76%-success problem, proving intentional score manipulation. She warns Horikita that Ayanokōji may be more intelligent than she is, and warns AK he could be sent to "Class E"
    (Expelled).
  • The Stairway Confrontation &
    The Pawn Offer (pp. 148–151): Horikita confronts Ayanokōji on the stairs, grilling him about his Class A ambitions and his true abilities. When AK tells her not to act so proud, she chops him in the ribs. Horikita identifies the three immediate crises: tardiness, talking, and the upcoming midterms. She demands his help;
    when Ayanokōji flatly refuses, she simply ignores him ("I heard the voice inside your head. You said you'd help"). When AK suggests she recruit Hirata, Horikita dismisses him — "Unfortunately, no one else in Class D is nearly as easy to manipulate as you."
    — then drops her iconic shogi analogy: "Right now, I don’t need a gold or silver general. I want a pawn."
    She dismisses his continued refusal and tells him to contact her once he has given it thought.
Chapter 8: The Association of Failures p.152–202

Setting. Classroom 1-D, cafeteria, dorm rooms, school library, locked rooftop, dormitory grounds at night. Mid-to-late May.

Narrative beats

  • Compass Wake-Up &
    The Lunch Deal (pp. 152–157): Horikita stabs Ayanokōji's arm with her drawing compass to stop him dozing off, then flatly denies it ("When did I stab with you a compass needle, Ayanokouji-kun?"). At lunch she negotiates his cooperation: he must gather the failing students (Sudō, Ike, Yamauchi) for a rival study group while she tutors. She plays dirty with the Kushida café grudge ("I said that to Kushida-san. I don’t remember saying that to you") and hands him her phone number and email — effectively holding a gun to his head ("No, not ‘almost.’ I really am threatening you").
  • Recruitment War (pp. 157–166): Sudō refuses outright ("Sounds fishy;
    I’ll pass. I’ll be fine cramming for the test the night before");
    Ike waves him off ("A high school student’s free time is precious") while texting girls. Ayanokōji enlists Kushida instead — she eagerly accepts as "ambassador of goodwill,"
    insists on participating herself, extracts his phone number, and flips all three holdouts via text within hours. His own idea of a lure is a joke she shoots down: "If they get a perfect score, you agree to be their girlfriend or something"
    — "Okay, how about a kiss? If they get a perfect score, you give them a kiss."
    — "So, you really do want to die?"
  • Hirata's Study Group &
    The Kushida Veto (pp. 152–153, 167–169): Hirata independently announces a 5-o'clock classroom study group (Sudō, Ike, Yamauchi stay seated anyway). That night, Horikita calls furious that Kushida was enlisted without permission and refuses to let her join — "I will not allow her into our study group. I refuse to budge on this"
    — then hangs up on him.
  • The Library Session &
    Collapse (pp. 171–180): The rival group convenes at the library's far table: Horikita, Ayanokōji, Kushida, plus Sudō, Ike, Yamauchi, and stowaway Okitani (dragged in by Kushida). The pass line is clarified — 32 points — while Horikita demands everyone aim for 50. Instant catastrophe: Sudou throws his pencil and quits ("Stop. I’m done. This isn’t going to work"), provokes Horikita with anti-academic defiance, and seizes her by the collar until Kushida grabs his arm. Ike quits next ("You might be smart, Horikita-san, but that doesn’t mean you can act like you’re better than us"), taking Yamauchi and Okitani with him. Horikita coldly concludes it is "better to get rid of the dead weight";
    she accuses Kushida of faking her concern;
    Kushida, hurt, leaves resolved to save them herself. Ayanokōji and Horikita remain alone in the oppressive silence.
  • Rooftop Unmasking &
    Blackmail (pp. 181–186): Ayanokōji chases the unanswered-calling Kushida across campus to the locked rooftop, where he discovers the sweet girl persona is a mask: she viciously vents her hatred of Horikita ("It’d be better if she just died…"). Spotted, he is pinned against a hallway wall by her forearm and threatened: “If you tell anyone what you just heard, I won’t forgive you.” The mutual-secret standoff instantly rebalances their relationship.
  • The Bullying Chat (pp. 189–191): In the Class D group chat, Satou (invited by Ike) joins and the boys plan payback against Horikita — hiding her shoes, making her cry. Ayanokōji stays neutral, warns that school punishment would follow, and shuts the chat down with "If Kushida heard about this, she’d probably hate you. Lol."
    He closes his phone, recognizing he is drifting past a situation he should be at the center of.
  • The Night Assault &
    The Devil's Handshake (pp. 192–202): Unable to sleep, Ayanokōji intercepts Horikita's nighttime ambush of her brother Manabu, catching the president's thrown strike mid-air. After the beating she demands his real motives;
    they debate merit, equality, and her dismissiveness of others'
    strengths. She concedes his argument ("You’ve persuaded me;
    I have to concede that point") and seals the pact — she will look after Sudō and the others, but purely as strategy for Class A — while Ayanokōji's narration admits he has "made a deal with the devil."
Chapter 9: The Failures Mobilize Once Again p.203–235

Setting. Classroom 1-D, Palate Café, school library, faculty room, cafeteria. ~6 weeks into school (mid-May).

Narrative beats

  • The Reunion Campaign (pp. 203–211): A month and a half in, Horikita reactivates Ayanokōji — tearing up her absurd written contract ("You agreed to be my workhorse until we reach Class A") — and personally recruits Kushida over lunch at Palate, winning her with raw honesty ("No. This is for me."). Kushida doesn't stop at the study group: she demands a seat in the wider Class A project, which Horikita conditionally grants. Back at school, the boys reassemble: Sudou demands a sincere apology for the library humiliation and gets the opposite — "I hate you, Sudou-kun"
    followed by "our mutual abhorrence is trivial right now. I will teach you for my own sake."
    Horikita unveils her in-class study plan (weaponize the six wasted hours;
    10-minute break tutoring;
    memorize, don't take notes), but Sudou still walks. Ayanokōji detonates the contingency: he publicly offers Kushida a date to anyone scoring 50 — Ike bids 51, Yamauchi 52, and Kushida tops it with a date for the highest score. Sudou folds ("guess I don't have a choice").
  • The New Routine &
    The Ally Test (pp. 212–217): The Idiot Trio actually studies during class;
    Sudou fights sleep for his pro-basketball dream. The group relocates its 20-minute lunch sessions to the library — officially for concentration, really to dodge Hirata's rival group. Kushida ambushes Ayanokōji for lunch, waves Horikita off, and drops her roster knowledge: he's 5th in the girls'
    hot-guy rankings (Satonaka A #1, Hirata #2), high in gloominess, and "there's something missing, some element of attractiveness."
    The lunch's real purpose surfaces — "If Horikita and I happen to clash, I hope that you'll cooperate with me"
    — plus her doctrine: "First, we'd need to build a relationship of mutual trust."
  • The Class C Confrontation (pp. 219–224): Ike's Francis Bacon triumph is cut short when Yamawaki and his Class C friends mock Class D as "defective product[s]"
    and let slip that the test's material isn't what Class D was studying. Sudou seizes Yamawaki by the collar;
    only the unexpected intervention of Ichinose Honami (Class B) — calm logic plus a school-report threat — de-escalates it. Ayanokōji notes she managed what Horikita's bluntness could not.
  • The Faculty Room (pp. 225–228): Chabashira admits the test topics "changed last Friday"
    and she "must've forgotten to inform you,"
    handing over revised page numbers with zero contrition;
    no other teacher reacts — Ayanokōji smells design. Horikita rationally withdraws ("staying would be a waste of time"). Ayanokōji tasks Kushida with spreading the corrected scope to all of Class D. The humiliation backfires productively: Sudou bows and suspends club activities for the final week, Ike and Yamauchi follow, and Kushida rallies fists.
  • The Old-Test Operation (pp. 229–235): Suspecting the real game, Ayanokōji stakes out the cafeteria's free-meal third-year (a zero-point Class D upperclassman) and buys his first-year midterm papers: 10,000 offered → 15,000 paid (bonus: last year's mock answers). Verification lands: every word of the mock test is identical across years. The plan: withhold the papers from the trio (don't kill their motivation), then release them online the day before the exam to force a class-wide overnight cram — enough for universal passes, not perfect scores. He reads the deeper design: Chabashira's early certainty that "there was a way for us to pass this test"
    was pointing at this institutional escape hatch all along — and it explains how Sudou, Ike, and Yamauchi were admitted at all. Kushida fronts the cover story — he asks her to say she got the papers "from a third-year student you’re close to"
    so he stays invisible: "Don't you feel that a strange bond of mutual trust forms between people who share a secret?"
Chapter 10: Midterm Exam p.236–245

Setting. Classroom 1-D. Thursday (distribution), then exam day (Friday).

Narrative beats

  • The Eve: Old Tests Go Wide (pp. 236–237): Kushida takes the podium and hands out Ayanokōji's printed old-test sheets under the cover story ("I got them from a third-year student last night"), framing them as near-certain predictions. Ecstasy: Ike hugs his copy, Yamauchi laugh-complains that studying was pointless, Sudou quietly thanks her. Ike rallies secrecy from the other classes. Horikita gives Kushida rare genuine praise — including recognizing that announcing it after class was exactly right, lest premature leaks kill motivation.
  • "I really hate you"
    (p. 238): As Kushida leaves, Horikita demands the one confirmation their partnership requires: "You hate me, don't you?"
    — reading through the deflection ("You're not answering my question because it's true. Right?"). With her smile unwavering, Kushida answers: "Ha ha, you got me… That's right. I really hate you."
    Horikita declines the reason entirely: "Knowing that is good enough. I can continue working with you without hesitation."
  • Exam Day (pp. 240–243): Full attendance — "That's the first hurdle for you leftovers"
    — and Chabashira dangles the reward: clear this midterm and the July final with no expulsions and the class earns "a dream vacation on an island."
    First papers confirm the jackpot: the questions match the old exam;
    Horikita's lessons had predicted the Japanese and chemistry content too. At the break, confidence runs hot (Ike: "I might get 120 points") — until Sudou is found sweating over English, having slept through its old-test review: "Everything but English. I fell asleep partway through."
    Ten minutes of cramming, magic-spell letters, then an English test he can only survive one remembered answer at a time.
  • The Apology (pp. 244–245): Afterward, Sudou is gutted — "I did everything I could, but I have no idea how well I did."
    Horikita steps in front of him: no corners cut, head held high — then amends her old mockery of his pro-basketball dream, having researched how brutally rocky that road actually is: "Someone who doesn't understand how difficult, how arduous basketball is has no right to dismiss that dream as foolish."
    She bows: "I'm sorry for what I said back then."
    The class is stunned;
    Sudou stares after her through the door, hand over his heart: "O-oh, no… I… I think I might be in love with Horikita…"
Chapter 11: The Beginning p.246–255

Setting. Classroom 1-D → first-floor hallway outside the faculty room.

Narrative beats

  • The Red Line (pp. 246–248): Results day opens triumphant — more than ten perfect scores, Sudou 60×4 with 39 in English — until Chabashira draws a red line above his name: "You failed, Sudou."
    The bombshell: this midterm's pass mark was 40, not 32 ("you were just one point short"), computed as the class average (79.6) ÷ 2 (39.8), rounded up. Expulsion processing begins coldly (withdrawal form, legal guardian). Hirata — the student Sudou most despises — is first to demand an audit;
    there are no grading mistakes. Some students are silently relieved the "nuisance"
    is leaving.
  • The Sacrifice Exposed (pp. 249–250): Horikita raises her hand voluntarily for the first time all year and springs a decimal-trap: last test's average (64.4 ÷ 2 = 32.2) was set at 32, so this one should be 39, not 40. The class then understands what her score sheet means: perfect in four subjects, a deliberate 51 in English — she had sabotaged her own exam to drag the English average down and lift Sudou over the line. Chabashira demolishes the trap (the rule is rounding, not decimal-dropping — "Well, too bad.") and leaves. Horikita's parting words to a shattered Sudou: "I'm sorry. I should have tried to lower my score just a little more"
    … "Don't misunderstand. I did this for my own sake."
  • The Point Purchase (pp. 250–255): Ayanokōji chases Chabashira down with the fairness question — "Do you think that today's Japanese society is fair?"
    — gets "The world isn't fair, not even the slightest bit,"
    and presses the Class D scope-change grievance. She calls it a microcosm of society and blocks formal appeal: expulsion "cannot be overturned at this stage"
    — wording AK instantly mines. Then his play: he produces his student ID and asks to buy one test point for Sudou. Chabashira howls with laughter — "I never imagined you'd try to buy points"
    — and names the exceptional price: 100,000 points. Horikita steps out of hiding: "I'll pay, too."
    Chabashira takes both IDs, collects the combined 100,000, and cancels the expulsion. She then confirms she's known about the old-test operation all along, praises it as "one correct solution,"
    and asks Horikita if she grasps how talented Ayanokōji is — Horikita's verdict: "I think you're disagreeable."
  • Defective, Not Trash (pp. 254–255): Chabashira notes no Class D student has ever been promoted and that the school will "coldly toss you aside."
    Horikita answers without flinching: "maybe the students in Class D are defective. However, that doesn't mean they're trash"
    — because "with repairs, a defective product may become a superior article."
    Chabashira's faint smile: "I look forward to seeing what you do next."
    Back in the hallway, Horikita chops Ayanokōji in the side "For whatever"
    — and he chases after her.
Chapter 12: Victory Celebration p.256–262

Setting. Ayanokōji's dorm room, evening.

Narrative beats

  • The Gathering (pp. 256–257): The former "association of failures"
    reconvenes to celebrate that no one was expelled — Ike toasting with juice, Horikita sitting outside the circle reading (her presence itself a milestone). Yamauchi retcons his own expulsion risk into a boast: "I could have gotten a perfect score if I were serious about it. Really."
    Kushida credits Horikita's tutoring, who deflects with "I did it for my own sake."
    Sudou, post-apology, openly calls her "a surprisingly good person."
    The Class D secret — that a test point was bought for 100,000 points — stays buried under "Hmm, I don't really remember."
  • Class A Pitch (pp. 257–261): Horikita steers the party to business: final exam harder, points nonexistent (Class D lowest average, free-offerings survival for the broke pair), and the need for an ally network. Kushida reveals the Class A project to Ike, Yamauchi, and Sudou and recruits them as helpers;
    a proverb volley (Kushida: "A single arrow is easily broken, but not ten in a bundle";
    Horikita: "even if you bundled these ten together, they'd still break";
    Kushida: "Three heads are better than one!";
    Ayanokōji: three combined test scores "one normal person's grade") ends in a truce and all three enlist — Ike and Yamauchi "Gladly!", Sudou grudgingly.
  • Ability Is Paramount (pp. 261–262): Bickering with Sudou (annoying-girl / not-cute / headlock horseplay), then Horikita's hard terms: "In this school, ability is paramount… you can't do so half-heartedly. Otherwise you'll be a burden."
    Ayanokōji closes alone — chest tightening at the words, the goal of Class A now shared with an unshakeable partner: "…I wouldn't mind seeing Horikita smile."
Postscript p.263–264

Thesis statement. academics aren't everything — sports, drawing (illustrator), comedy (entertainer); "a near-infinite variety of callings and professions" suit different people. Adult regrets ("If only I'd done this") drive the theme.
