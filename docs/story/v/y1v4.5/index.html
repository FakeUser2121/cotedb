Chapter 1: Summer Vacation is Nearly Over p.10

Setting. No scene — the collection’s one-page first-person frame, outside the story: no character is present and no school business is named.

Narrative beats

  • Opens on the Sazae-san syndrome — Sunday-evening dread of Monday — and the students who wish summer would last;
    the narrator refuses it: the free years of life are the school years, and most of a life is spent sacrificing for society. (p.10)
  • Names the shape of the book: “This is a selection of vignettes about students wavering in that space between childhood and adulthood.” (p.10)

About these pages. The printed footer reads “Page 1” although the page marker reads p.10; the front matter carries the table of contents and galleries. The translator's watermark line sits at the foot of the page and is not story text.

New names. none — the page names no one (Sazae-san names the TV cartoon of the opening, not a character).

Unattributed lines. the frame’s “I” prints no name; read as the series narrator and filed under Ayanokōji, following the first volume’s opening monologue.

Chapter 2: Ibuki Mio has Surprisingly Good Sense p.11–48

Setting. The dorm between exams, then Keyaki Mall’s fifth floor — the couples-only fortune-teller booth — and finally the elevator on the mall’s external wall, where Ayanokōji and Ibuki are trapped after their reading. (pp. 11–48)

Narrative beats

  • Sudou’s call on his tournament rest day: 100,000 points arrive from Ayanokōji, the tournament is reported, and the real objective is an invitation for Horikita;
    the attempt fails and Sudou’s reply is “Then forget about it.” (pp. 11–17)
  • The fifth floor turns solo students away — guidance for couples only, Ukon-sensei reads for couples — and Ibuki, refused in the next line, pairs up with Ayanokōji;
    he reads her anxiety as hyper-awareness, and she calls him a future mass murderer. (pp. 18–27)
  • No partner works: Horikita would decline, Kushida is beyond his courage, the couples floor would fluster Sakura, the guys would not pair up — but the next day he and Ibuki happen upon the booth at the exact same time. (pp. 28–29)
  • They queue as a pair;
    Ibuki’s worry about the island grudge is dismissed on ability-versus-feelings grounds, and the narration concludes Class C is not that different from Class D. (pp. 30–32)
  • The reading: hooded elderly fortune-teller, Basic Plan for both, palm and pattern talk, then the pronouncement that Ayanokōji holds the fate tenchuusatsu — rare, overall outlook bad, no parental blessing, but the rest is up to him;
    the advice: straight home, no detours, no panic, work together if stuck. (pp. 33–37)
  • They take the detour anyway;
    the elevator grinds to a halt on the third floor, the emergency line is dead, both phones run out, and the emergency number is scribbled over with magic marker. (pp. 38–41)
  • The air conditioning dies in the summer heat;
    hatch and button schemes fill half an hour, and the floor-plus-close press moves the car — by coincidence the adults outside had used the remote at the same moment. (pp. 42–47)
  • Katsuragi is the man at the doors: the cut-off call reached him, Sudou “performed brilliantly,” and the friendship with Ayanokōji is treated as already told — the story began a little while ago. (p. 48)

About these pages. Section markers 2.1 (p.18), 2.2 (p.28), 2.3 (p.29), 2.4 (p.33), 2.5 (p.38); p.22 is a blank page; printed page numbers run nine behind the citations used here (p.11 is printed as page 2); the translator's watermark sits at every page foot.

New names. Ukon — the booth’s fortune-teller, named only by the line attendant at Keyaki Mall (p.20); the roster row was already present and the file opens with this chapter. The hooded tent reader (pp. 33–37) is read as the same person: the booth has one fortune-teller.

Unattributed lines. none — every spoken line names its speaker or the group speaking; unnamed on the page: the “three idiots” of p.41 and the two adults at the doors (p. 47).

Chapter 3: Katsuragi Kouhei is Surprisingly Troubled p.49–107

Setting. Summer vacation one week before the elevator incident, running to the August 29 birthday: Keyaki Mall's girls' shop and chocolate shelves, Ayanokōji's room, the dorm crossroads, the bench outside, the Student Council Room, and the dorm lobby. The closing section (pp. 105–106) is told from Katsuragi's viewpoint. (pp. 49–107)

Narrative beats

  • Kushida's group-chat invite for Inogashira's birthday;
    the class is broke until the September 1 deposit, so the boys pool 500 points for one gift. (pp. 49–50)
  • Shopping trip: Ike blows up at Shinohara over the orange-character keychain, and Katsuragi is spotted buying chocolate with an August 29 birthday card. (pp. 51–59)
  • In Ayanokōji's room the birthday list matches nobody;
    Kushida supplies Horikita's February 15, Sudou plans interference, Kushida bans it, and the investigation lands on Ayanokōji. (pp. 60–66)
  • The crossroads stakeout is intercepted by Student Council President Horikita Manabu and Secretary Tachibana: the island leader-name leak, the sister rejection, and a vice-president offer with a warning about Nagumo — all refused. (pp. 67–73)
  • Tailing Katsuragi explains the uniform (school entry), the chocolate-shelf cleanup talk raises the outside-contact disadvantage, and “It’s personal” keeps the recipient secret. (pp. 74–78)
  • The report finds no matching girl;
    the boys flip to a self-gift theory and then to buying Katsuragi a present themselves, doubling Ayanokōji's expenses. (pp. 79–82)
  • Kushida joins the run;
    Ayanokōji breaks off for the bench talk with Horikita — the Ryuuen loss, falling to rock bottom, and the island contradiction he cannot explain. (pp. 83–87)
  • The birthday ambush turns into a student council visit: the package request is refused, points cannot buy explicit rules, and Katsuragi's twin sister — an orphan's one birthday — is revealed;
    Ayanokōji's quiet-overlook pitch earns a free hand from Manabu. (pp. 88–94)
  • Lobby defeat, then the room plan: a club-tournament courier, Sudou recruited for 100,000 points (20,000 advance, 80,000 after, routed through Ayanokōji), lunchbox transport and a hidden phone for the proof video;
    the run succeeds and Manabu's possible hand stays open. (pp. 95–104)
  • From Katsuragi's side: Ike and Yamauchi hit his floor with party poppers and Kushida's melted ice cream cake. (pp. 105–106)

Chapter 4: Dangers Lurk in Everyday Life p.108–131

Setting. The evening of the dormitory water outage, 6:00 P.M. past midnight: Ayanokōji's room, Horikita's flat on the thirteenth floor, the corridor and emergency stairs, the first-floor lobby and elevator, with Keyaki Mall named as the unreachable goal. (pp. 108–131)

Narrative beats

  • A water-department failure cuts the dormitory until early morning;
    Horikita's two unanswered calls turn a quiet dinner into a worry. (pp. 108–111)
  • The 9:00 P.M. turtle parable ends with a request Ayanokōji cannot refuse: come to her room, past nine, on the girls'
    floor. (pp. 112–114)
  • Her right hand has been stuck inside a small water bottle for over two hours;
    soap and water are the only way out, and every local source is dry — cafeteria, vending machines, Ike and Sudou refused, Hirata unreachable. (pp. 115–120)
  • The Kushida grudge surfaces with the ship-exam recap: Outcome #1's numbers, the VIP leak, “I assume she’s guilty,” and Ayanokōji's counter that responsibility runs both ways. (pp. 120–122)
  • Disguise and exit: handkerchief, bath towel, going as they are;
    the lobby monitor rules out the elevator, so the stairs — until Maezono's door opens and Kushida is suddenly in the hallway with water to spare. (pp. 123–127)
  • Forced back to the elevator, Ayanokōji stands in front of the camera as cover;
    the fifth-floor stop brings Kouenji aboard, and he never once leaves the mirror. (pp. 128–130)
  • Sakura's late reply finally brings water;
    the bottle comes off, gratitude is extracted, and Ayanokōji traps his own hand testing the same bottle. (pp. 130–131)

About these pages. Section markers 4.1 (p.110), 4.2 (p.112), 4.3 (p.115), 4.4 (p.119), 4.5 (p.128), 4.6 (p.131); p.117 is a blank page; printed page numbers run nine behind the citations used here (p.108 is printed as page 99).

New names. Maezono — Class D neighbour three rooms from the stairwell, first on the page in this volume; Sakura's research keeps her awake and away from her phone; the cafeteria lady is unnamed.

New rules and facts. outage rules — two liters of water from the cafeteria, Keyaki Mall free water, no bottling to take home, one flush per toilet (p.108); the lobby monitor and the locked east stairwell (pp.124, 126); the Outcome #1 recap — 500,000 points per member, 1,000,000 to the VIP, no class points (p.121).

Unattributed lines. none — every spoken line names its speaker; unnamed on the page: Ayanokōji's friends out for karaoke (p.123), the lobby students (p.128), and the voice on the stairs (p.128).

Chapter 5: A Day of Girl Troubles and Disaster: A Devil Smiles Like an Angel p.132–152

Setting. The last days of summer vacation: Ayanokōji's dorm room in the morning and at midnight, the agreed spot behind the school building, and the tree-lined corner where the rain catches them. (pp. 132–152)

Narrative beats

  • Yamauchi bursts in with a printed love letter to Sakura and drafts Ayanokōji to proofread and deliver it;
    the tribute line and the scary font go, the Kokugikan sumo final stays as a probable lie. (pp. 132–135)
  • The reverse-comparison plan: Sakura is lured out by Kushida, told nothing, and left to think Ayanokōji is the one confessing. (pp. 136–137)
  • The handover: the anonymous letter, Sakura's panic, the Horikita worry, and the refusal to read it together — she leaves to read alone. (pp. 137–143)
  • The midnight call: nerves about tomorrow, the responsibility talk, the dating question that perks up at zero experience, and sleep at last. (pp. 144–146)
  • The answer in the rain: soaked to the skin, Sakura turns Yamauchi down in person while Ayanokōji watches the first confession he has ever seen;
    Yamauchi leaves relieved, matured, and lending a phone. (pp. 147–152)
