Chapter One: Sae Chabashira's Monologue p.7–8

Setting. POV frame — Chabashira's recurring nightmare of the graduation special exam her third-year class (Class B) took 11 years ago; the events end the same way every time.

Narrative beats

  • The nightmare and its invariant shape (p.7): the events of "that day"
    — her class had momentum, "well within reach of Class A"
    (p.7). By third year, "the number of classmates who had left me had swelled to six"
    (p.7), yet she had accumulated class points in her final year without a single expulsion, believing she could graduate from Class A "without having to leave anyone behind"
    (p.7).
  • The nightmare's climax (p.7): "The scene changes and I am screaming in class."
    Her best friend, Chie, approaches with an angry expression and grabs her chest;
    she screams in agony. "The class, which had been united as one, collapsed in an instant."
    Then: "It's... over."
    — printed as his line ("He muttered, his face full of resignation and realization."
    p.7).
  • The ending (p.8): "He had been my companion for the past three years"
    — irreplaceable friend, irreplaceable person, in the vein of "a bit of a jerk, but he was serious, kind, and more reliable than anyone else."
    He reached out his hand to her "under the evening sky, somewhat shyly."
    Holding back tears, she said, "I look forward to working with you..."
    — "The relationship between the two of us ended just as it had begun."
    (p.8)

New names. none. "Chie" (her best friend) is printed without a surname; no roster id is confirmed by these pages.

Unattributed lines. none. "It's... over." is tagged "He muttered" (p.7) — the he-companion; who exactly "he" is (the classmate whose expulsion ended the relationship) is not named on these pages.

Translation errors, kept as printed. none observed.

Open threads. identity of the "he" companion and of Chie; the exam at the heart of the nightmare (the Unanimous Voting Exam of Ch. Four/Eight/Nine); the 100 vs 50 class-point decision hinted by "well within reach of Class A."

Chapter Three p.29–75

Setting. ANHS, the day before and the weeks leading into the Unanimous Voting Exam — homeroom, the walk-home, the classroom, his room, a first-year dormitory room; afterword material begins the same evening as the announcement (p.29 → evening calls).

Narrative beats

  • The announcement (p.29–44): Chabashira announces a surprise special exam for the second-years only, tomorrow — the "Unanimous Voting Exam": 5 questions, up to 4 options each;
    39 students vote anonymously on tablets;
    not unanimous → 10 minutes free discussion → revote;
    5 hours, 50 class points for clearing, −300 for failing;
    protection points temporarily disabled (expulsion unappealable except by paying 20,000,000 private points);
    binding agreements/money exchanges strictly forbidden;
    thorough school monitoring ("even people like Ryūen would be unlikely to make any overt moves");
    phones collected during the exam.
  • The mechanics taught by example (p.32–42): Example 1 — "You lose 5 class points, but all your classmates gain 10,000 private points": round one 3 in favour / 36 opposed;
    second round 2 / 37 (Ike is curiously baffled). Example 2 — "1 million private points to one person in the class"
    (nomination removes the chosen student and their vote;
    majority nomination, then 38 others vote for/against;
    a unanimous "against"
    removes the candidate and re-picks;
    volunteering allowed first-come-first-served, one candidate per question): Ike is chosen for the demo;
    the class unanimously votes against giving it to him (0 for / 38 against);
    Sudō delivers the verdict. Example 3 — Keyaki Mall facility (4 options;
    restaurants/shops/recreation/medical: 20/4/15/0 first round, 23/2/14/0 second round);
    the school-vote rule: if this class is unanimous on one option and the other three classes are unanimous on another, the three-vote choice wins. Named unanimous win condition: 39 in favour or 39 against clears the question.
  • The practice votes (p.33–35): Ayanokōji deliberately votes 'for'
    both times to prevent an accidental unanimity and teach the class how the exam works;
    Sudō does the same;
    2 in favour in round two. The pair of "who's still voting yes"
    lines printed untagged — attribution gap recorded (see sudou-ken.md).
  • The emergency leader (p.45–46): Kushida proposes a clear leader in case choices don't add up;
    Hirata backs her;
    Horikita accepts the role;
    Airi and Haruka mutter as Kei claims Kiyotaka in the lunchroom ("Sorry, but I'm his girlfriend and I'm his priority, you know?"
    p.46) — the relationship goes fully public in one stroke.
  • The fallout (p.48–55): the pair hits the whole school within hours;
    Ike and Shinohara's own coming-out is treated as expected;
    Haruka confronts Ayanokōji on the walk home — Airi has cried through lunch;
    Haruka agrees to talk to Airi about the maid café once she's more relaxed;
    she calls him out on underestimating the depth of Airi's feelings.
  • Horikita's consultation (p.56–61): she catches him at the dormitory elevator and visits his room (Room 401) — asks about the relationship (he's evasive), then asks for exam advice;
    proposes the pre-agreed-leader strategy;
    rejects Ryūen-style coercion because the vote is anonymous;
    warns him the class is not united (Kushida will "lie about everything";
    Kōenji might vote his own way);

    she exits after writing a mysterious long message to someone.
  • The pre-exam war council (p.62–65): Ayanokōji gathers Yōsuke and Kei in his room;
    rice-or-bread debate;

    he assigns them shadow-support roles for Horikita (secret cues, pre-agreed signs);

  • The Chairman's call (p.66–69): shortly after 10 p.m., Chairman Sakayanagi reveals government officials and their families will attend the sports and cultural festivals — his father may be involved;

    she walks out determined.
