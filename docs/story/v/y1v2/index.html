Chapter 1: Sakura Airi's Soliloquy p.10–11

Setting. No scene — pure interior monologue, volume cold open.

Narrative beats

  • First-person chapter from Sakura Airi (the only non-Ayanokōji POV chapter header in the volume's structure).
  • She confesses she cannot interact with people and "came up with a solution": adopting a false face to hide her true self.
  • Asks whether everyone else also performs a façade;
    concludes she'd never know because she can't connect — "Therefore, I remain alone."
  • Ends on the contradiction that drives her arc (p. 10): "I… I want to connect with someone from the bottom of my heart."
    then "And so I will continue to live quietly, with my eyes downcast. Alone."
Chapter 2: The Sudden Beginning of Our Tumultuous Troubles p.12–39

Setting. June 30 evening (school building, post-brawl) → July 1, Class D: homeroom, lunch, walk to dorms, Ayanokōji's room at night.

Narrative beats

  • Cold open, Sakura's POV (pp. 12–13): Searching for a selfie spot, Sakura Airi walks on Sudou finishing a one-sided three-on-one beating;
    she photographs it silently, freezes, and flees before he can spot her ("If I'd lagged a mere two seconds, he probably would've seen me.", p.13). The volume's witness is seeded before the protagonist even knows the fight happened.
  • 2.1 Points day (pp. 15–18): First class-point payout in Class D's history: 87 points vs Class A's 1004;
    Horikita reads the across-the-board ~100 gain as mere midterm reward;
    Hirata translates her cryptic "get something";
    the deposit is then announced delayed for all first-years, with Chabashira's ominous "If there are still points left, that is."
    (p.19).
  • 2.2 Lunch (pp. 19–21): AK's friends-but-alone paradox in full;
    Horikita's bento economics (free supermarket ingredients) and her surfboard roast;
    Kōenji and Sakura as fellow solo eaters.
  • 2.3 After school (pp. 22–26): Chabashira drags Sudou to the faculty room;
    Horikita and AK walk home debating whether the point trouble is school-side or student-side;
    their hate-is-fine honesty about Kushida ("Kushida-san and I are like oil and water.", p.25).
  • 2.4 The crisis conference (pp. 27–38): Sudou confesses: three Class C boys (Komiya, Kondou — his own basketball clubmates — plus Ishizaki) tried to blackmail him out of his shot at summer-tournament regular;
    he beat them, they reported first, and he faces a summer suspension plus class-wide point loss unless he produces proof by Tuesday. He felt someone watching (p.34) — the witness problem is live. Kushida drives the plan (get confessions or find the eyewitness), volunteers her friendship with Komiya's group, and pins a dodging AK to participation. AK's balance: 8,320 points (p.27).
  • Chapter close (p.38): "No matter how much I thought about it, I couldn't come up with any solutions."

New names. Komiya, Kondou, Ishizaki (Class C, basketball), Hondou (Class D, mentioned).

Chapter 3: Weak Point p.40–65

Setting. July 2, one school day — homeroom, the cafeteria at lunch, then after school in Class D's classroom, the hallway and the entrance, and the doorway of Class B's classroom.

Narrative beats

  • Homeroom (pp.40–43): Chabashira announces the Sudou–Class C fight to the whole class, in her "serene"
    neutrality, and puts out the school's terms: judgment next Tuesday, "taking into account any eyewitnesses and evidence"
    (p.41), and every teacher will brief their own class, so the affair becomes school-wide property (p.41). She asks for witnesses;
    nobody raises a hand — including the one person in the room who saw it. Ike's objection is arithmetic ("will we be at zero again this month?", p.41), Kushida then re-tells Sudou's whole account to the room (p.42), Yamauchi's one-sentence doubt about Sudou's junior-high bragging breaks her appeal (p.42–43), and only Hirata standing ("I want to believe him.") with Karuizawa after him turns the class into an investigation team (p.43).
  • 3.1 The cafeteria (pp.44–53): Horikita refuses flatly — "I've never once considered you a friend. Nothing makes me more uncomfortable than being around someone who doesn't recognize his own stupidity."
    (p.45) — and the group cannot work out why, because they read her refusal as personal dislike. Kushida puts the question to AK (p.46) and he answers with a guess ("Horikita is smart, right? So I think she probably had some idea.", p.47), while privately holding her read: the school had already told the teachers enough for her to see "the possibility of a happy ending was almost nonexistent"
    (p.46). The rest of the scene is the boys'
    romance council — Ike's phone-tracking of Kushida (p.50), the "7 outta 10"
    ranking talk (p.51), Sudou's Horikita fantasy (p.51), and the girlfriend contest with its "Envy Tax"
    (p.53) — over which AK keeps noting that Sudou's problem is unresolved (p.53).
  • 3.2 After school (pp.54–63): the class splits into canvassing teams — Hirata and Karuizawa's "Hero &
    Gal Team", Kushida's "Beautiful Girl &
    Entourage Team"
    (p.54). Kushida drags AK out of the room by asking, chases Horikita down to the entrance, and is refused twice: "I have an important daily routine, so I need my alone time."
    and "Maybe the Class C students really did start this fight, but Sudou-kun was also one of the perpetrators."
    (pp.56–57). Horikita then hands the explanation to AK in front of her ("Even though he pretends not to understand what I'm thinking, he probably does.", p.57) and AK gives Kushida the volume's coldest lecture — image-as-evidence, the two-suspects thought experiment, "the less information you have upon which to base your judgment, the more you have to rely on whatever meager information you have at hand"
    (pp.59–60). Kushida accepts the logic and rejects the conclusion ("I don't agree with abandoning Sudou-kun just to punish him.", p.60), then takes AK's advice on how to help (p.60), overrides his one-by-one plan in favour of asking several people at once (p.62), and leads the group to Class B on his ranking argument (p.61).
  • 3.3 Class B (p.64–65): the away-classroom visit — Ike and Yamauchi frozen in the doorway, Kushida at home among strangers, Class B's hair and skirts less reserved than AK expected (pp.63–64), and AK's window-side grumble "I wanna go home."
    answered by Ike's "I think people who participate in clubs are stupid"
    (p.64). The chapter closes mid-hook: "I waited for a few minutes until I received Kushida's report. I certainly didn't expect what she told me."
    (p.64).
  • The chapter's shape: it is the volume's pressure test on each character's stated principle — Horikita's class-rank logic against Kushida's unconditional friendship against Hirata's faith in classmates — with the actual witness sitting silent in the same building the whole time.

About these pages. Pages 49, 58 and 65 carry no prose (they are illustration or section-break pages). Chapter 4 begins on p.66.

New names. none. New system facts: the school phone's registered-friend location tracking (p.50) and the deferred disciplinary judgment dated to next Tuesday (p.41).

Open threads. who watched AK in the two-thirds-empty classroom (p.62); whether any Class B student saw anything (p.64); Horikita's refusal standing until Tuesday (p.41).

Chapter 4: An Unexpected Witness p.66–151

Setting. the days between the July 2 announcement and the following Sunday afternoon — Class D's classroom and its hallway, the doorway of Class B, the corridor and stairwell of the special building after school, Ayanokōji's dorm room and the dorm lobby, the route to school, the spot behind the gymnasium, the tree-lined pathway, and a shopping mall with a school-licensed electronics store. Disciplinary judgment has been promised for Tuesday and does not arrive inside this chapter; the mall trip is dated by the line "On Sunday afternoon, I went to the shopping mall to meet Kushida and fulfill my promise" (p.132), and the love letter is timed for "Friday evening at 4:00 PM, behind the gymnasium" (p.123).

Narrative beats

  • 4.0 The class-change audience (pp.66–74): the canvassing of Class B pays out in the worst possible currency — a homeroom discussion of whether a student can buy their way into a better class. The volume's point economics are dumped on the class in one sitting: 20,000,000 points as the price of a class change, a private-point ceiling of 3.6–4 million, the story of a twelve-million-point fraudster who was expelled, club and philanthropic activity as sources of class points, and the school's test-point shop (pp.68–72). Chabashira glances at the two students who have actually bought a test point (p.68);
    the narrator's verdict is that "our homeroom teacher was being extremely and deliberately mean"
    (p.71). Ayanokōji moves Horikita not with feeling but with arithmetic — "Doesn’t this mean there’s some value in saving Sudou?"
    (p.72) — and stops the instant she concedes (p.73).
  • 4.1 Horikita names the witness (pp.75–82): while the class still believes it has no lead, Horikita produces one from an observation nobody else logged — when Kushida announced she was looking for a witness, Sakura looked down (p.77). The room cannot place Sakura;
    Sudō misnames her, Yamauchi denies ever having confessed to her and then retracts the denial in the same breath (p.79–82), and the narrator admits he "could barely match Sakura’s name to a face"
    (p.82). Ike is headlocked for describing her body out loud. The chapter's plan is set by the one person who can reach her: "Well, I’ll try to talk to her myself tomorrow,"
    Kushida says (p.82).
  • 4.2–4.3 The approach fails in public (pp.83–96): Hirata agrees to greet Sakura but refuses to be the one who pressures her (p.83–84), Karuizawa is ruled out in advance as too blunt for her (p.83), and Kushida's attempt collapses in front of the whole class — the camera is knocked over, Sakura apologises and flees (p.86–90). Ayanokōji alone reads it as a flight response, citing cultural anthropologist Edward Hall's intimate space (p.88), and notices she concealed her dominant hand (p.88). Kushida then asks him publicly whether he is Sudō's ally (p.91);
    Horikita writes Sudō off aloud, the class's last organised defence of him dies in an argument with Kōenji, a desk is kicked apart, and Hirata physically stops him (p.92–93). Ike gives up on the case at p.91;
    the narrator discovers the classroom's hidden cameras at p.95, and Horikita asks him to walk back with her at p.96.
  • 4.4 The special building, and Class B's offer (pp.97–107): at the scene of the fight the narrator tells Horikita "Sudou was the first friend that I made here. I want to help him."
    (p.97) and privately states the case's actual state — "Our only proof was Sudou’s word. We were completely in the dark."
    (p.100). Sakura passes them and neither of them can make her testify;
    he lets her go (p.102). Ichinose Honami of Class B arrives, hears the whole account from a boy she barely knows (p.104), and offers help on the ground that "If the party that lied wins, that’d set a bad precedent"
    (p.106). Horikita decides, not him: "I didn’t really have the power to decide in the first place;
    that was Horikita’s job."
    (p.106). The information gap becomes his stated grievance against the school (p.107).
  • 4.5–4.6 Horikita in his room, then the dorm lobby (pp.108–115): she follows him home, calls coming there "a shameful act in itself. A disgrace."
    (p.111), checks his room for cameras, and delivers the volume's most dangerous read of him — "You’re hiding in plain sight. You disgust me more than anyone else."
    (p.112). In the dorm lobby the next morning, Ichinose greets him by name: the contact exchange (p.109), the discovery that Class B invented its own offices (class representative, vice representative, secretary) while Class D has none (p.114–115), and the report that Class A's homeroom teacher gets a special bonus (p.108).
  • 4.6–4.7 Walking to school, and the confession behind the gymnasium (pp.116–127): Ichinose and the narrator walk to school, and the chapter stops being about Sudō for eleven pages. She volunteers Class B's figures ("We have about 600 points, so our classes are around 350 points apart.", p.117), expects a hidden summer task bigger than either exam (p.117), and argues out loud that the four classes were not sorted by ability alone — "I think that good students and good athletes were placed into Class D for a reason, as a countermeasure."
    (p.120). Then she asks whether a girl has ever confessed to him (p.121), and after class makes him wait behind the gymnasium as a fake boyfriend so a Class B girl named Chihiro will be hurt less by rejection (p.124). He refuses to be the lie: "I’m just a friend."
    (p.126), lectures both of them on what a confession deserves, walks away without waiting for the answer, and she goes back and tells the truth anyway (p.127).
  • 4.8 Kushida's phone call (pp.128–131): she calls him late, first of her own accord, to take responsibility for Sakura's broken camera and to borrow him for the repair errand (p.128–129);
    she carries Horikita's conclusion — "Horikita-san seems convinced that Sakura saw everything."
    (p.129) — and the class's whole estimate of itself: "Class D was able to come together, thanks to you and Hirata."
    (p.130). He asks whether her room is on the ninth floor (p.130) and the call dies into a silence she starts three sentences inside and finishes with nothing: "No, it’s nothing."
    (p.131).
  • 4.9–4.10 The mall, the repair counter, the witness (pp.132–146): she tests whether he has been excluded from his own friend group — "I’m talking about Ike-kun and the others going to that café, of course."
    (p.133) — then Sakura appears in a hat and a surgical mask, already sitting unnoticed on the same bench (p.136). At the counter Sakura freezes in "blatant fear and revulsion"
    (p.138);
    the clerk's interest is tolerated, and the narrator sees what the two girls do not: "the clerk had unsettling eyes"
    (p.140). He takes the pen, writes his own name and dorm room on the repair form, and daring the clerk to object (p.140) — the repair is quoted at two weeks (p.140). He then phones Sotomura, "the Professor", from the shop floor about a device he never names, priced against a laptop another student bought for 80,000 points, and books the trip as a "preliminary inspection"
    because he does not have enough points to buy anything (p.143–144). Kushida asks whether they have met before, asks to see Sakura without her glasses, is refused, and lets it go (p.144). She then declines the thanks, asks Sakura to stop speaking so formally, and is answered by a girl who agrees to try (p.145). The chapter's turn arrives as a gift offered for a day of errands: "I-I may be able to help you with Sudou-kun’s case."
    — "In her own words, Sakura told us that she was the witness."
    (p.146).
  • 4.11 Three calls, none of them finished (pp.147–151): that night the narrator and Kushida reconstruct Sakura's armour — the fake glasses, deduced from the absence of lens distortion, and his conclusion that "she probably wears them because she doesn’t want others to see her true self."
    (p.148). Sakura calls him instead of her (p.148–149), says thank you, asks "What were you…thinking about?"
    in a question nobody can answer, and hangs up with "I’m sorry, it’s nothing. Goodnight."
    (p.149). Kushida confirms she heard nothing. The volume's last page of Chapter 4 is the narrator admitting that his puzzle pieces are "too small, and too few", and deciding he cannot approach Sakura without making her stand out (p.150).
  • The chapter's shape: the case is solved by everyone except the people whose job it was — Horikita is right about the witness and cannot reach her, Kushida reaches her but cannot extract the testimony in a classroom, Hirata refuses to ask at all, and the two students nobody credits (the narrator, and a girl the class cannot picture) close it in a shopping mall. The chapter's second half then refuses to be about Sudō at all: it spends eleven pages on a confession Class D never hears about, and ends on a witness who has promised to testify and a boy who cannot work out why she telephoned him.

About these pages. Pages 87, 134 and 151 carry no prose (illustration or section-break pages); p.113 carries only its closing two lines. Chapter 5 begins on p.152.

Ichinose Honami is formally named and given her first name (p.109); Hoshinomiya Chie, Class B's homeroom teacher, is described through Ichinose (p.108); Chihiro, an unsorted Class B girl who confesses to Ichinose (p.125–127); Sotomura, "the Professor", is called by name and re-enters as the volume's technical expert (p.143); Yukimura is named once, in the narrator's list of Class D's misfits (p.119); Higashiyama-sensei is remembered as the P.E. instructor who promised 5000 points for the best time in their first pool class (p.71); the school-licensed mall stores and their clerk enter as locations and a minor antagonist (p.138–141).

New rules and facts. the class-change price and its ceiling, the twelve-million-point expulsion, club and philanthropic activity as class-point sources, the test-point shop and its verification (p.68–72); classroom cameras as the grading instrument, with none in the hallways (p.95); the homeroom-teacher bonus rumour (p.108); the self-made Class B offices against Class D's absence of them (p.114–115); the school-linked electronics store, its warranty and repair turnaround (p.138–140); and the first hard consumer price a student states, 80,000 points for a laptop (p.143).

Open threads. Tuesday's judgment, still not delivered (p.41, carried forward); what Sakura wanted to say on the phone (p.149); whether Kushida's interrupted sentence and the ninth-floor question are connected (p.130–131); who Chihiro is to Class B's balance of power (p.127); the unnamed device being price-checked at the mall (p.143–144); Ike's café gathering that the narrator was not told about (p.133); and the camera itself, out for a two-week repair that this chapter does not wait for (p.140).

It wasn’t anything special.") is not assigned to either speaker; the question about whether Sakura photographs people (p.142) is untagged; the first exchange of the p.147 night call is untagged on both sides; the reply "You kind of look suspicious…" (p.136) sits between two named speakers with no tag of its own; and in the p.48–53 cafeteria scene and the p.90–96 classroom exchange, several lines have no speaker named and are absent from these records rather than attributed.

Chapter 5: Each and Every Prediction p.152–189

Setting. the day before the deliberation and the night of it — the school entrance and its bulletin board, Class D's classroom, the faculty room, Ayanokōji's dorm room, Sakura's room on the eleventh floor, the club-activities building, and the dorm's shared spaces. The chapter ends the night before Tuesday's judgment; the hearing itself is not shown.

Narrative beats

  • 5.0 A poster, a message board, a leak (pp.152–158): with one day left, the class is united but lacks "firm, irrefutable evidence"
    (p.152). Two canvasses run in parallel: Kanzaki of Class B pinned a bounty flyer to the bulletin board (p.153), and Ichinose posted to the school homepage's message board (p.153). Both offer points, and the money is Class B's own (p.154). Two replies arrive, and one of them re-frames the fight: Ishizaki was a junior-high brawler, which makes a three-on-one loss suspect rather than heroic (p.156). Kanzaki's reading — that the three let Sudō beat them as a trap — is the chapter's first real theory (p.156). Ayanokōji keeps Sakura's name out of the conversation (p.156), teaches Ichinose how to pay an anonymous informant with a temporary token key (pp.157–158), and catches one unreadable glimpse of her phone that he files away as a future problem (p.158).
  • 5.1 The face Horikita does not intend to make (pp.159–162): Kushida's morning thanks, and Horikita's reaction to it, are the chapter's quiet centre — she denies making any expression while the page says otherwise (pp.159–160), and Kushida reads the same face as exclusion rather than anger (p.162).
  • 5.2 The witness is tested (pp.163–166): in the faculty room, Sakura gives the account in full for the first time (p.163). Chabashira does not welcome it: she asks why the testimony is late, then names the class's motive out loud — "it may be a lie made up by Class D"
    (p.164) — before accepting it anyway and setting the terms: Sakura must attend, Sudō will be told, and only two classmates may sit in (p.165). Horikita claims both seats over Kushida (p.165).
  • 5.3 The strategy lunch (pp.167–168): Sudō's confidence, Horikita's refusal to reassure him, and Ike and Yamauchi fighting over a manga magazine in the middle of it (p.167). Kushida notices something she will not name yet and reaches for her phone (p.168).
  • 5.4 The room on the eleventh floor (pp.169–175): Sakura asks him to come alone, and he rides up past the floor he claimed not to be visiting while Horikita, shopping bags in hand, asks him the question and then withdraws it (pp.170–171). In her room Sakura says she does not have the confidence to testify and considers staying home (p.171). The night's real content is her explanation of why she can talk to him — not his eyes but "the pupils, way back in the eyes"
    (p.172) — and his offer of friendship, which she accepts as if it were a surprise (p.173). Her glasses fall off, and the page confirms what the class had already guessed: there are no lenses (p.174).
  • 5.5 Advice in a club building (pp.176–178): she takes him to the club-activities building, and the advice he gives her is deliberately not encouragement: testify for herself, not for Sudō or Kushida or the class (p.177). Whether it helps is left open (p.178).
  • 5.6 What the class finds (pp.179–185): Kushida identifies Sakura as the gravure idol Shizuku from a blog, and the proof is mundane — a dormitory door in one photograph (p.180). Her theory of the double life is that the idol face is the construction, not the disguise (p.183). Then, at the door, she makes her deferred request explicit — help her, and she will give him "my most precious possession"
    (p.184) — and the narrator's judgment of her hardens (pp.184–185).
  • 5.7 The comments (pp.186–188): that night he reads the blog's history and finds a single commenter escalating for months, including a post from the previous Sunday (pp.186–187). The connection he makes is the electronics-store clerk who took her repair form (p.187). The chapter ends with nothing actionable: the only option is to wait for her to ask for help (p.188).
  • The chapter's shape: the witness found in Chapter 4 becomes a person with a second life in Chapter 5, and every institution around her — teacher, class, school message board — handles her evidence while missing her. The chapter's two most consequential discoveries, the lensless glasses and the stalker, are made by people she did not tell.

About these pages. Pages 155, 161, 182 and 189 carry no prose (illustration or section-break pages); pages 154, 160, 166, 178 and 188 carry only a short closing passage. Chapter 6 begins on p.190.

New names. Kanzaki Ryūji of Class B, introduced by Ichinose, who pinned the bounty flyer (p.153); Shizuku, the gravure-idol name Sakura worked under (p.179); Ishizaki gains a junior-high history as a fighter (p.156).

New rules and facts. the school homepage has a student-visible message board (p.153); points can be sent anonymously via a temporary token key generated from the payer's ID number (pp.157–158); entry to the girls' floor of the dormitory is allowed but barred after 8:00 PM (pp.169–170); a deliberation admits at most two observing classmates (p.165).

Open threads. what Ayanokōji saw on Ichinose's phone (p.158); the request Kushida will make once the case is settled (p.184); what Kushida noticed at lunch (p.168); the stalker, still unidentified and still on campus (p.187); Tuesday's judgment, promised since p.41 and still not delivered (p.165).

Unattributed lines. the manga-magazine exchange at p.167 leaves the second speaker untagged; the blog reveal at p.179 opens with two untagged lines ("Wait, is that a picture of Shizuku?" and "Shizuku?") before any speaker is named.

Chapter 6: Truth and Lies p.190–219

Setting. the day of the disciplinary hearing — Class D's classroom and the faculty room before 4:00 PM, the student council room on the fourth floor for the hearing itself, then the corridor outside it and the entranceway afterwards. The retrial is set for 4:00 PM the following day, outside this chapter.

Narrative beats

  • 6.0 The morning she comes anyway (p.190): Sakura is in the classroom early, gloomier than usual and sitting alone;
    she says she came because the class would be upset if she were absent. Ayanokōji repeats the club-building instruction — "Testify for your own sake, more than anyone else's."
    Ike and Yamauchi now know about the idol work;
    Sakura notices and mouths "It's all right."
  • 6.1 The hearing (pp.191–209): the room is the student council's, with Secretary Tachibana running the bulk of it, President Horikita Manabu at the back, both homeroom teachers, and the three Class C students. Horikita freezes when she learns the council will sit in and has to be physically roused when Sakura's testimony stalls (pp.192–197). Sakura fails to speak for twenty seconds, is ordered out, and then produces the volume's turning evidence from the doorway: "I definitely saw what happened!"
    (pp.199–200). Her photographs establish that she was in the special building, and the president finds the one that shows the fight itself (pp.203–204). Sakagami cannot undo the photo, so he proposes a settlement — two weeks'
    suspension for Sudō, one for his students — and Chabashira accepts it as "a logical conclusion"
    (pp.205–206). Ayanokōji performs surrender to force Horikita back into the argument;
    she delivers a damning closing statement, refuses to sit, and states that Class D will not accept even one day of suspension (pp.206–208). The president sets a retrial for the next afternoon and warns that expulsion is on the table (p.209).
  • 6.2 After the hearing (pp.209–218): Sakura breaks down in the corridor over Sakagami's parting speech;
    Horikita promises to keep fighting and leaves (pp.209–210). The president stays to tell Ayanokōji and Sakura that Class D evidence can never be accepted at full value, then concedes the conversation is the closest he has come to one (pp.210–212). On the way out Sakura recovers enough to thank Ayanokōji, starts a sentence at the entranceway, and loses it when Ichinose and Kanzaki appear (pp.213–214). Class B argues the case is already lost and then offers to keep searching;
    Horikita asks them for something instead — a preparation that requires a lie — and Ichinose lends her phone (pp.214–218). The chapter ends with Horikita collecting the cost of the tickling in the hearing: a blow to the side and a promise to pay it back double (p.218).
  • The chapter's shape: the volume's two engines meet for the first time. The disciplinary case is decided procedurally — the evidence is accepted, then discounted by class;
    the compromise is offered, then refused on principle;
    and the retrial is granted because neither side will call itself a liar. The chapter's real turn is not the photo but Horikita's refusal to sit, and the real cost is paid by Sakura, who wins the room and then has to walk out through Sakagami's sentence.

About these pages. Pages 201 and 219 carry no prose (illustration or section-break pages); p.210 opens with the tail of the corridor scene. Chapter 7 begins on p.220.

New names. Sakagami-sensei, Class C's homeroom teacher (p.193); Secretary Tachibana, the student council secretary (p.193, surname only); Horikita Manabu is named in narration for the first time in this volume (p.208); the Class C trio is fixed by name here — Komiya, Kondou and Ishizaki (pp.194–198).

New rules and facts. a disciplinary settlement is heard by the two homeroom teachers, the concerned students and the student council rather than the faculty room (p.192); the student council president can take over a case and the secretary handles the rest (p.193); a retrial can be set for the next day, with expulsion as a possible judgment if neither side is proven to have lied (p.209); extensions of the retrial window are not granted on request (p.209); a proposed settlement can assign different suspension lengths by degree of injury (p.205).

Open threads. the retrial at 4:00 PM the next day and its outcome (p.209); the plan Horikita asks Class B to prepare, and the phone she borrows (pp.216–218); the Professor's part in it (p.217); what Sakura began to say at the entranceway (pp.213–214); Kanzaki's warning that the favor may come back against Class B (p.217); and the stalker thread from Chapter 5, untouched here.

The chapter's shape. the case is solved offstage by a bluff and ratified onstage without a fight; the point of view changes so the victory never has to be watched, and the corridor afterwards converts it into the volume’s true trade — a declaration of war in exchange for a win Ayanokōji did not want to be part of. The "only one solution" of the title is Ichinose’s: make the incident not exist.

Postscript p.258–261

Setting. outside the story. The author’s afterword, printed after Chapter 7, in which Syougo Kinugasa writes to the reader for the first time in this volume; four months have passed since the last book. No page of it is in-world evidence — no character fact, point total or school rule appears here.

What he says the book was (p.258). "Anyway, this book focuses on a disturbance centered on Sudou, following the midterms." He allows that "since Class D is filled with all kinds of problem children, the day where they can work together is still a long ways off", calls the volume an opening act — "You could say that the first act was a fierce battle waged over class points" — and credits it with showing the cast from an angle the reader did not have: "I think that the classmates also revealed things about themselves that weren’t visible before." The promise attached is "the story will progress greatly from this point on."

The illustrator, and the cover (p.258). "Shunsaku-sama drew such gorgeous illustrations for this book. I nearly suffocated when I saw the cover art with Kushida. It just had the best expression."
