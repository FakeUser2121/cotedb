Chapters

Prologue: "The Monologue of Sakayanagi Arisu" p.7–12

Setting. Sakayanagi Arisu’s recollection of visiting the White Room facility as a child with her father; ends in the present as she wakes from the memory.

Narrative beats

  • pp.7–9.
    • Young Sakayanagi and her father observe children through one-way glass at the facility
    • she argues talent is set at birth and questions whether the experiment can create geniuses.
  • pp.10–11.
    • She notices a boy (Ayanokōji Kiyotaka, the director’s son) excelling calmly and learns he has lived in the facility since birth
    • her father expresses pity for him.
  • p.12.
    • Sakayanagi resolves to prove talent is not decided by education
    • in the present she wakes from the nostalgic dream, thinking of their coming confrontation.

About these pages. monologue/flashback framed by a present-moment awakening on p.12.

New names. none on roster beyond Sakayanagi Arisu and Ayanokōji Kiyotaka (her father appears unnamed in roster terms).

New rules and facts. Sakayanagi visited the White Room as a child and saw young Ayanokōji Kiyotaka there (pp.7–12); she recalls resolving to play chess after seeing him.

Unattributed lines. none.

Translation errors, kept as printed. none observed.

Prologue: White Room memoryY1V11 · p.7–12notes on Sakayanagi Arisu

In a childhood memory, visits a white facility with her father and watches children through one-way glass. Tells her father the experiment cannot create artificial geniuses because potential is set at birth. Notices a boy calmly defeating opponents at chess; her father identifies him as Ayanokōji Kiyotaka, the director’s son. Hears from her father that Kiyotaka has lived in the facility since birth and feels the loneliness of that life. Recalls resolving to prove talent is not decided by education and to start playing chess; wakes in the present thinking of their approaching confrontation.

with Ayanokōji Kiyotaka
