Chapter 1: Chabashira Sae’s Soliloquy p.10–12

Setting. No scene — pure first-person interior monologue, volume cold open. Chabashira narrates; the only other presence is an unnamed "certain young boy" she recalls standing before.

Narrative Beats

  • Opens with the Wings of Icarus myth as an interpretive frame: Greek mythology "deals abundantly with human frailty" (p.10); Daedalus's wax wings, Icarus's fall.
  • She connects the myth to a boy she stood before "considering what these past few months had brought" — the comparison because "he resembled Icarus" (p.10).
  • Immediately breaks the comparison: the boy "possessed neither the bravery nor the arrogance of Icarus" (p.11).
  • Confesses her provocation as a forced gamble: "I had been pushed into a corner. I had no choice but to do it." — she chose to "incur this boy's divine wrath" and turn his quiet rage toward herself; "The die cannot be returned once cast. The gamble had already begun." (p.11)
  • Volume's second straight non-Ayanokōji cold-open soliloquy (after Sakura in Year 1, Vol. 2 ch1), this time the homeroom instructor's.

Page map note. p.12 carries no prose (illustration/section-break page).

New named entities. none — the chapter names only the mythological figures (Daedalus, Icarus, King Minos, Minotaur) inside the retelling.

Attribution gaps. the boy is never named on pp.10–11; he stays unnamed in the records.

Year 1, Vol. 3 p.13–99 — Ch.2: The Boundary Between Heaven and Hell

Setting. First day of the two-week summer programme: luxury-liner deck (pp.13–23), beach landing and rules briefing (pp.24–46), Class D river base camp (pp.63–98); the deserted-island special test runs one week to August 7th.

Narrative beats. The vacation reveal collapses into the deserted-island survival test announced by Mashima (pp.24–29); Class D splits three ways over spending the 300-point budget, with the cardboard-toilet war as the first fracture (pp.32–43); the search parties go out and Ayanokōji's team catches Katsuragi revealing himself as Class A's spot-leader at a cave (pp.52–62); the river is chosen as base camp and Horikita is nominated spot-leader (pp.63–67); Ike's bonfire and apology to Shinohara give the class its first working cooperation (pp.85–88); Hirata's 120-point floor speech settles the spending policy (pp.89–90); Ibuki Mio, beaten and expelled by a boy in Class C, is taken in (pp.78–95); the chapter closes on Kōenji's retirement and the class's thirty-point penalty (pp.97–98). p.21, p.37, p.79 and p.99 carry no prose (blank/short scan pages).

Page map note. The chapter's own section numbers §2.1–§2.8 begin at p.24 (§2.1), p.47 (§2.2), p.53 (§2.3), p.63 (§2.4), p.72 (§2.5), p.76 (§2.6), p.83 (§2.7) and p.87 (§2.8). Printed book page footers lag the corpus page markers by ten from p.13 onward.

New named entities. Mashima Tomonari (Class A homeroom, English), Katsuragi Kōhei and Totsuka Yahiko (Class A), Ibuki Mio (Class C), Shinohara Satsuki and Yukimura Teruhiko and Satō Maya (Class D). The sick Class A student who eats the opening thirty-point penalty is never named. The boy who hit Ibuki is never named. "The Professor" (p.51) is a booed nickname without a printed name. The Class A deck bullies (p.15) are unnamed. Miyake Akito is named only on Hirata's phone (p.16); Sakayanagi Arisu only in Totsuka's reported speech (p.60).

New system facts. Special test "deserted island survival," one week to August 7th; 300 points per class (Class A starts at 270 after the illness penalty); two tents, two flashlights, one matchbox, one cardboard toilet per class; penalties — health retirement 30, pollution 20, roll-call absence 5/student, inter-class violence or robbery = disqualification plus private-point forfeiture; watches mandatory, track temperature/pulse/GPS, emergency button; spots with eight-hour exclusive possession, key card held only by a registered leader, 1 bonus point per occupation (provisional), 50-point penalty for using another class's spot; seventh-day leader-guess rule: +50 per correct guess, −50 per wrong one, and a found-out leader forfeits the class's saved bonus points; base camps cannot be moved without justifiable reason; river water and foraged fruit are legal, forest/sea/defecation is not.

Open threads. Horikita's dishevelled hair and cold on p.22 (her afternoon is never explained on these pages); Kōenji's forest knowledge and his "little curious" remark before he retires; why Ayanokōji was sure the camp-location leak to Ibuki "won't be a problem" (p.82); Katsuragi's claim of two more spotted facilities; Ibuki's unnamed assailant and Class C's internal fight; the boys' sleeping arrangement (open air) and the week's remaining economy.

Attribution gaps. Ike's "Whooooaaaaa! Kikyou-chaaaaaaan!" (p.17) is narration-adjacent shouting, not needed as a quote row. Sudou's practice-Horikita gyaru lines (p.19) are Ike performing, already mirrored on both classmates' pages. The "What the hell, Haruki?" exchange at p.97 keeps both speakers in one quoted block under Ike.

Year 1, Vol. 3 p.100–130 — Ch.3: Rivals on the Move

Setting. The morning after the first night of the deserted-island test: Class D's river base pre-dawn and through roll call (pp.100–107), the Class C beach (pp.108–116), Class B's well camp (pp.117–125), and the Class A cave on the mountainside (pp.126–130).

Narrative beats. Ayanokōji finds a rental digital camera with no data in Ibuki's bag and cuts one blank page out of the manual to copy the island map (pp.100–102); Kanzaki comes to the river and hands over the Class B campsite location (p.103), and the second spot update is postponed so ownership lapses just after eight o'clock (p.104); Komiya and Kondou walk in eating chips and deliver Ryūen's invitation to the beach, which is read as reconnaissance rather than a brawl (pp.105–106); the Class C beach is a lit-up carnival — toilets, showers, a tarp, barbecue, chairs, parasol, jet skis on the shoreline — with a hundred and fifty points or more already burned, and Ryūen waves off the warnings and calls Horikita by her first name (pp.108–116); Ibuki's hitting is admitted and the zero-points strategy is worked out on the walk back — nothing left to penalise, at most 150 points of leader-guessing, retirement as the exit (pp.112–116); Class D tours Class B's camp, trades a full seventy-point inventory for the Class A cave directions, drops the hunt for the leader's identity, and leaves with the verdict that Class B is ahead on teamwork (pp.117–125); at the Class A cave Horikita talks past Yahiko and is turned back by Katsuragi's unspoken one-spot rule (pp.126–130). p.110, p.119 and p.130 carry no prose (blank/short scan pages).

Page map note. The chapter's own section numbers §3.1–§3.4 begin at p.105 (§3.1), p.108 (§3.2), p.117 (§3.3) and p.126 (§3.4). Printed book page footers lag the corpus page markers by nine here (p.116 prints Page 107).

A rebellion or absence drains Class C twenty points a day, and the two missing students swallow what few points remain (pp.115–116); the zero-points strategy leaves nothing to penalise, every rival leader guessed correctly could add at most 150 points, and retiring at zero returns the class to the boat (pp.113–116); Class B's economy — seventy points of gear, a well proved safe before use, a water shower on a gas can, unlimited vinyl sheets granted with the temporary toilet, water sprinkled to drag heat out of the soil (pp.120–122); five days are left as of the Class B visit (p.124); occupation of a spot does not bar looking inside it (p.128); the unspoken rule — one class occupies one spot and holds it to the end, and violating it invites retaliation (pp.128–129).

Year 1, Vol. 3 p.131–143 — Ch.4: The Meaning of Freedom

Setting. The third day of the deserted-island test: Class D base camp and the forest trail out of it (pp.131–137), Kōenji's rise with the cultivated corn (pp.138–141), and back at base camp (pp.141–142).

Narrative beats. Ayanokōji walks out alone before noon to see what Kōenji had been looking at, and Sakura catches up; he takes her along on a no-blame condition (pp.131–132); on the walk the electronics-store stalker's resignation comes out along with her idol photography years — making herself empty to get through the shoots (p.133); they rest against one tree and she says she is glad she came; he reads the distance between them shortening toward friendship, the first relationship he has felt tilt that way (pp.134–135); a hairy caterpillar drops into her hair and is removed without her ever knowing (pp.137–138); at Kōenji's vantage point, found by all five senses, Sakura digs out about fifty ears of cultivated corn whose soil and weed cover prove the school uses the island, and he carries them out in his shirt bag (pp.138–140); Katsuragi and Yahiko arrive, partnership is offered and refused with a fabricated class order, and Katsuragi stays behind to watch the spot while the pair leave (pp.140–141); at base camp the bag comes back full by one o'clock, Yamauchi's jealousy runs over the report, and Hirata hears it first (pp.141–142). p.136 and p.143 carry no prose (blank/short scan pages).

Page map note. The chapter carries no sub-section numbers; printed book page footers lag the corpus page markers by nine here (p.142 prints Page 133).

New named entities. None — every named figure in the chapter is already on the roster. The electronics-store stalker (pp.131, 133) stays unnamed. Named but not present: Kōenji (pp.131, 134, 138, 139), Kushida, Horikita and Ichinose (p.133, remembered from the earlier incident) and Ibuki (p.135, remembered).

New system facts. The cultivated corn — about fifty ears in one hidden spot, soil a different color from the surrounding forest — is proof that school officials come and go on the island (p.139); the walk out to the rise takes about twenty minutes from base camp by the handkerchief marker (p.138); a harvest team forms after the report and everyone is back by one o'clock in the afternoon (p.142); it is the third day of the test (p.131).

Open threads. Katsuragi watching the cornfield after the pair leave — good or bad intent is left unknown (p.142); whether another class will find the spot, which is why the first load is carried out at once (pp.140–141); what Kōenji intends with the corn he saw first and kept to himself (p.139); Yamauchi's shirtless accusation, never answered (p.142); the Ibuki camera question from the first morning now read through Sakura's reason for carrying hers — his inference, not a fact about Ibuki (p.135).

Attribution gaps. The bags line “It would’ve been nice if we brought bags…” (p.139) is untagged, either speaker. “Is this…corn?” and “It seems like it.” (p.139) are untagged between the pair. The camp close “We’ve got lots of food!” and “It was pretty dangerous, though. That guy from Class A, Katsuragi, was nearby.” (p.142) are untagged.
