Reading a page

Bold labels say what kind of note the page produced — Spoken is dialogue, Inner voice is the narrator's thoughts, What happens is action. “Curly quotes” inside them are the novel's own words. A page number in grey, such as p.247, is a printed-page reference: it says where to look in the book, and the book itself is not hosted here. Grey numbers are never links.

Anything bright is a control, and there are two of them. A + beside a scene title opens the notes that scene was read into — every line, including the ones copied word for word, appears at once, so nothing else on the page opens a second copy of them. A chip with an arrow, p.247 ↗, sits on a summary row whose scene is held on another page and takes you there.

Reading in progress? Choose I've read up to… in the bar above and anything later in the story stays folded until you open it. Your choice is remembered on this device.

Prose on this site comes from one place: a line-by-line reading pass, which takes a volume page by page and writes down only what is on a named page. That has reached 2 volumes so far (Year 1, Volume 1, Year 1, Volume 2, 194 anchored scenes between them). Everything those volumes do not cover is clearly marked as not yet worked through, and shows the mechanical facts — who is named on which page — rather than remembered summaries.
