## Psychology & competing readings

- **His violence is procedural, not chaotic: every campaign has rules, evidence, and a stated price** (confidence 0.85)
  - Evidence: y1v6 p.64 · y1v6 p.67 · y1v5 p.155
  - Reading: Directly competes with the 'sadist brute' surface reading: he confiscates phones by protocol, offers kneel-or-points choices, and reasons from evidence ('Evidence trumps theory').
- **He and Ayanokouji share methods but not minds — the series' explicit mirror pair** (confidence 0.8)
  - Evidence: y1v7.5 p.81 · y1v7 p.183
  - Reading: Ryuuen states it himself: 'similar methods, but fundamentally different thought processes.' His engine is amusement; Ayanokouji's is ends. Competing nuance: after the contract (y2v6) the distinction blurs into instrumental partnership.
- **He governs by appetite — fun is the sovereign value, points only instrumental** (confidence 0.8)
  - Evidence: y1v7 p.176 · y2v12 p.42 · y1v4 p.74
  - Reading: Competing reading: the 'fun' is partly persona — y1v6's rule-bound inquisition and y2v6's contract discipline show a calculating operator wearing amusement as armour. The text keeps both live.
- **The y1v7 defeat made him better — humility he never had came through losing** (confidence 0.75)
  - Evidence: y1v7 p.193 · y2v2 p.305 · y2v12 p.196
  - Reading: Counter: he was already outcome-respectful before the fall (y1v4 'shocked me'); the beatdown supplied the personal motivation, not the trait. Both readings fit the corpus.
- **Post-expulsion, ruling by terror became impossible, so he rebuilt on pure leverage** (confidence 0.7)
  - Evidence: y1v7.5 p.89 · y1v8 p.208 · y2v6 p.211
  - Reading: Supported by the mercenary pricing ('make it a million') and contract diplomacy replacing school-sanctioned hierarchy. Competing: he keeps the terror as brand — Ishizaki and Ibuki still fear him.
